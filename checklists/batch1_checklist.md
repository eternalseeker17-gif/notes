## Parmar Ch.2 — Microeconomics
- [ ] Fundamental problem: scarcity vs unlimited wants → choice [A]
- [ ] Scarcity def (money/land/labor/time finite); Choice def [A]
- [ ] Production possibility of economy def [A]
- [ ] PPF/PPC def: graph, max combos, resources used efficiently [A]
- [ ] PPF example (₹6000, shoes vs jeans) [A]
- [ ] PPF slope = downward/negative (trade-off) [A]
- [ ] Marginal Rate of Transformation (MRT) def = slope of PPC, amt of one good sacrificed for one more unit of other [A]
- [ ] PPF point positions: on curve=full utilisation; under curve=under-utilisation; over curve=not achievable [A]
- [ ] Opportunity cost def + example (jeans vs shoes) [A]
- [ ] Free goods (air, sunlight) = no opportunity cost, abundant [A]
- [ ] Utility def: satisfaction/benefit from consumption [A]
- [ ] Cardinal Utility (numerical, less realistic) vs Ordinal Utility (ranked, more realistic) [A]
- [ ] Indifference curve def: combos giving same satisfaction [A]
- [ ] IC example (1 food+12 clothing = 2 food+6 clothing) [A]
- [ ] IC properties: convex to origin (rectangular hyperbola), slope=MRS, two ICs never intersect [A]
- [ ] MRS def + formula: Δsacrifice/Δgain [A]
- [ ] Marginal Utility def: additional satisfaction from one more unit [A]
- [ ] Marginal Product (MP) def; Marginal Cost (MC) def; Marginal Revenue (MR) def [A]
- [ ] AFC def (constant total fixed cost changing per unit, eg rent); AFC curve downward sloping [A]
- [ ] AVC def (variable costs vs output, eg raw materials); AVC curve U-shaped [A]
- [ ] AVC + AFC = ATC [A]
- [ ] Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility: satisfaction from each extra unit decreases, ceteris paribus [A]
- [ ] LDMU graph concepts: Zero MU (point of satiety), Negative MU [A]
- [ ] Isoquant def: combos of inputs(labor+capital) giving same output; slope=MRTS [A]
- [ ] Demand def: qty willing+able to buy at a price in a period [A]
- [ ] Law of Demand: inverse price-qty relationship [A]
- [ ] Law of Demand conditions: income constant, other goods' price constant, no preference change (ceteris paribus) [A]
- [ ] Movement vs Shift in demand curve: movement=price change; shift=other factors (income/preference/related good price) [A]
- [ ] Shift direction: rightward=demand increase; leftward=demand decrease [A]
- [ ] Giffen Goods def: inferior/non-luxury, demand↑ as price↑ (no better alternative); eg wheat price increase [A]
- [ ] Veblen Goods def: luxury, demand↑ as price↑ (opposite of law of demand); eg iPhone, Mercedes [A]
- [ ] Ex-Ante Aggregate Demand def: intended expenditure; AD=C(intended consumption)+I(intended investment) [A]
- [ ] Equilibrium: AD=AS; AD>AS→output↑; AD<AS→inventory buildup→output↓ [A]
- [ ] NOTE: single-commodity-as-aggregate-good simplification rationale (macro) [A]
- [ ] Supply def: amount available for purchase/use [A]
- [ ] Supply curve def: price vs qty producers willing to supply [A]
- [ ] Producers aim to maximise profit [A]
- [ ] Supply curve = positive/upward sloping (direct relation) [A]
- [ ] Equilibrium = demand curve ∩ supply curve = equilibrium price/market clearing price; Qd=Qs [A]
- [ ] Equilibrium shifts if any factor shifts; deficit/surplus zones on graph [A]
- [ ] Demand↓→curve shifts left; Demand↑→curve shifts right [A]
- [ ] Price elasticity of demand def + formula: %Δdemand/%Δprice [A]
- [ ] Perfectly Elastic Demand: e=∞, small price rise→demand to zero; eg gold/cars (as printed, flagged inconsistent w/ p.256) [A]
- [ ] Relatively Elastic Demand: small price Δ → large qty Δ; ed>1 [A]
- [ ] Perfectly Inelastic Demand: qty fixed regardless of price; ed=0; eg water/wheat/rice [A]
- [ ] Relatively Inelastic Demand: qty Δ less than proportional to price Δ; ed<1 [A]
- [ ] Unitary Elastic Demand: rectangular hyperbola; ed=1; proportional price-demand change; TR at max (as printed) [A]
- [ ] Income Elasticity def + formula: %Δqty demanded/%Δreal income [A]
- [ ] Luxury Goods: income elasticity>1(positive); demand rises more than income; eg luxury cars/designer clothes/expensive vacations [A]
- [ ] Inferior Goods: income elasticity<0(negative); demand falls as income rises (switch to better quality); eg low-quality foodgrains/public transport [A]
- [ ] Cross-Price Elasticity def: price Δ of one good → demand Δ of another; eg Pepsi 10%↑ → Coke demand 5%↑ [A]
- [ ] Cross-price example: negative cross-price elasticity = complementary goods (Py↑→Qy↓→Qx↓, consumed together) [A]
- [ ] Substitute vs Complementary Goods table: competitive vs joint demand; price-qty relationship sign; examples (tea/coffee, coke/pepsi vs tea/sugar, car/petrol) [A]
- [ ] Law of Diminishing Returns: additional inputs→smaller output gains after a point; applies in short run (≥1 fixed factor) [A]
- [ ] Graph zones: Most Productive→Point of Diminishing Returns→Diminishing Returns→Point of Max Yield→Negative Returns [A]
- [ ] Abnormal Profits def: profit above opportunity cost of factor inputs [A]
- [ ] Optimum Profit def: max profit where MR=MC [A]
- [ ] Regulated Profit def: govt-set max earnings/prices/ROR to prevent monopoly abuse [A]
- [ ] Bandwagon Effect (Demonstration Effect) def + example (2nd ed Fatman, new phone) [A]
- [ ] Snob Effect def: opposite of Bandwagon; demand↓ as others consume; eg limited-edition watch [A]
- [ ] Fixed Cost def + example (rent/license/registration) [A]
- [ ] Variable Cost def + example (raw materials/shipping) [A]
- [ ] Explicit Cost def + example (wage/rent/materials) [A]
- [ ] Sunk Cost def + example (advertising/legal/accounting fees) [A]
- [ ] Break Even Point def: TR=TC, no profit no loss [A]
- [ ] Production function def: input-output relationship [A]
- [ ] Monopoly: one seller, no substitutes, high entry barriers; eg Indian Railways [A]
- [ ] Oligopoly: few large firms, similar/differentiated products, many buyers, no easy entry; eg telecom/laptop market [A]
- [ ] Monopolistic Competition: many sellers/buyers, differentiated products, some price control, easy entry/exit, highly elastic (not perfectly); eg toothpaste [A]
- [ ] Perfect Competition: many buyers/sellers, homogeneous products, no entry barriers, price by S&D, perfectly elastic (ed=∞); eg agri products [A]
- [ ] MPC def + formula: ΔC/ΔY; MPC lower at higher income; 0<MPC<1; example (10000 income→1000 spend→MPC=0.1) [A]
- [ ] Autonomous Expenditure def: spending independent of income [A]
- [ ] Formula: C=A+cY (A=autonomous consumption, c=MPC, Y=income); Savings=1-c; Multiplier=1/(1-c); MPC+MPS=1 [A]

## Lucent §2 — Economy and Economics
- [ ] Economy def (state of country: production/consumption/money supply); Economics def (production/consumption/transfer of wealth) [A]
- [ ] 3 economic systems evolved as stages [A]
- [ ] Capitalistic Economy: origin Adam Smith Wealth of Nations 1776; Adam Smith bio (1723-1790, Glasgow professor); against govt regulation; division of labour, laissez-faire, invisible hand/market forces; need for competition [A]
- [ ] Capitalism spread: USA adopted post-independence (1 yr after book); by 1800 established; aka Private Enterprise/Free Enterprise/Market Economy System [A]
- [ ] Capitalist system: production/qty/price decided by market/private enterprise; no state economic role [A]
- [ ] State Economy: rooted in Karl Marx (1818-1883); first in USSR post-Bolshevik Revolution 1917; ideal shape in PRC 1949 [A]

- [ ] Capitalist economies called socialist/communist ones "state capitalism" (sole exploitators) [A]

- [ ] Depression spread US→W.Europe: unemployment, demand fall, lockouts [A]
- [ ] Keynes: General Theory of Employment Interest & Money 1936, Cambridge economist (1883-1946) [A]
- [ ] Keynes questioned laissez-faire/invisible hand; "invisible hand strangulates the poor"; wages/prices not flexible enough for full employment [A]
