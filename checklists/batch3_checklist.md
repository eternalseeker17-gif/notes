## Budget Functions & Basic Concepts
- [ ] Allocation Function: dividing resources between public/private goods [Parmar]
- [ ] Redistribution Function: ↓income inequality via progressive taxation + welfare programs [Parmar]
- [ ] Stabilisation Function: adjust spending/taxation to counter recession(↑spending)/inflation(↓spending) [Parmar]
- [ ] Asset def: economic-value resource owned/controlled for future benefit; eg gold, property [Parmar]
- [ ] Liability def: obligation to fulfill debt/duty; eg loan repayment [Parmar]
- [ ] Budget's main objective [B]: allocate public funds/limited resources across sectors (education/health/defence) for growth+welfare — NOT "increase unemployment"/"decrease taxes"/"promote private sector" (tested distractors)

## Revenue vs Capital Budget
- [ ] Budget divided into Revenue + Capital heads [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] Revenue def: funds to finance activities/public services, mainly from taxes+fees+fines+duties [Parmar]
- [ ] Revenue Receipts: don't create liabilities/reduce assets; non-redeemable, no claim on govt [Parmar][B reinforced: eg tax/fees don't need repayment ★]
  - Tax Revenue eg: wealth tax [Parmar]
  - Non-Tax Revenue eg: interest on loans, PSU earnings, railway tickets [Parmar]
- [ ] Revenue Expenditure: doesn't ↑assets or ↓liabilities [Parmar][B: salaries/pensions/interest payments = revenue expenditure, recurring, for normal govt functioning ★]
  - Plan Revenue Expenditure eg: govt grants/financial assistance [Parmar]
  - Non-Plan Revenue Expenditure eg: salary/pension, interest payment (largest component), infra maintenance [Parmar]
- [ ] Capital def: physical/financial resources producing value [Parmar]
- [ ] Capital Receipt: creates liability OR reduces asset value; eg loan, disinvestment [Parmar][B ★: sale of PSU shares = capital receipt (reduces assets, NOT revenue receipt) ★; main sources = borrowings, small savings, recovery of loans ★; loan from RBI = capital receipt (creates future liability) ★]
- [ ] Capital Expenditure: acquire/upgrade/maintain long-term assets; creates new assets, reduces liabilities; eg loan repayment, developmental expenditure, infra (roads/schools/hospitals) [Parmar][B ★: purchase of machinery = capital expenditure (NOT "doesn't create assets" — tested false statement) ★; grants for asset creation at STATE level are still Revenue Expenditure at Centre's books, since no direct asset created for Centre ★]
- [ ] Capital Account (govt budget) records transactions changing assets/liabilities — incl. capital receipts (loans/disinvestment/loan recovery) + capital expenditure (infra/machinery/loan repayment) [B]
- [ ] Revenue Account/Budget = another name = shows revenue receipts+expenditure, day-to-day admin, recurring, no new assets/liability change [B]

## Taxation — Direct, Indirect, Types
- [ ] Direct Tax: directly payable, cannot be passed on, progressive, marginal rate>average rate; eg income/wealth/gift/capital-gain/corporate tax [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] Indirect Tax: indirectly payable, CAN be passed on, regressive/proportional; eg GST, excise duty (petrol/diesel/ATF), customs duty [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] Paper Tax: significant only on paper, no real revenue yield; eg wealth tax, gift tax, estate duty (not levied now) [Parmar][B: called paper tax because widely evaded, generates little revenue, admin significance limited to paperwork ★]
- [ ] Laffer's Curve: tax rate vs govt revenue graph; ↑tax beyond a point → ↓revenue (discourages investment/consumption/work effort); bell-shaped [Parmar]
- [ ] Progressive taxation: tax rate ↑ as income ↑ [B ★: most countries use this for social justice — wealthy pay more, burden on poor reduced ★]. Regressive: rate ↓ as income ↑ (burden on poor). Proportional: rate constant regardless of income.
- [ ] Direct tax: cannot shift burden, paid by imposed entity directly [B, reinforced]
- [ ] Indirect tax regressive because: based on commodity price not income — rich+poor pay SAME tax amount for same good [B ★]
- [ ] CBDT administers direct tax (Income Tax etc.) [B, cross-ref Lucent CBDT below]

## GST
- [ ] Introduced via 101st Constitutional Amendment Act [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] Effective 1 July 2017 [Parmar+Lucent merge][B ★, reinforced: replaced VAT/Service Tax/Excise ★]
- [ ] Article 279A: GST Council = constitutional body, 33 members (2 Centre+31 States/UTs), Chairman=FM [Parmar]
- [ ] Cascading Tax (Tax on Tax): tax levied at every stage without credit for tax already paid → inflates final price [Parmar]
- [ ] GST purpose [B ★]: "One Nation One Tax" — uniform tax system across states, simple/transparent structure, easier inter-state trade (NOT "new direct tax"/"more duty on luxury"/"differentiate goods vs services" — tested distractors)
- [ ] GST subsumed 17+ taxes/cesses incl. Centre's excise duty+service tax, States' VAT/luxury tax/entry tax [B]
- [ ] GST first mooted 2006-07; GST Council constituted 2016 (Art 279A(1), 12 Sept 2016); 101st Amendment assent 8 Sept 2016; IGST launched midnight 1 July 2017 [Lucent]
- [ ] 3-tier levy: Central GST + State GST + Integrated GST; IGST = seamless input-tax-credit flow across states [Lucent]
- [ ] GST coverage: all goods/services except alcoholic liquor (human consumption); petroleum/petroleum products excluded until GST Council notifies [Lucent]
- [ ] Taxes subsumed — Central level: Central Excise Duty, Additional Excise Duty, Service Tax, Countervailing Duty (Addl Customs Duty), Special Addl Duty of Customs. State level: VAT/Sales Tax, Entertainment Tax (non-local-body), Central Sales Tax, Octroi/Entry Tax, Purchase Tax, Luxury Tax, Lottery/Betting/Gambling Tax [Lucent]
- [ ] States compensated for GST-implementation revenue loss [Lucent]
- [ ] GST Collection: April 2024 gross=₹2.10 lakh cr (record high, +12.4% YoY); net (after refunds)=₹1.92 lakh cr (+15.5% YoY) [Lucent]
- [ ] CENVAT: introduced via Union Budgets 2000-01/2001-02; multiple excise rates → single rate of 16% (2001-02); objective: simplify tax, reduce cascading, pave way for GST [B ★]
- [ ] Entertainment tax + Entry tax: historically State-levied (NOT Central), both now subsumed into GST [B ★ — tested "which was Central" = NEITHER]
- [ ] Customs duty = indirect tax on imports+exports, regulated under Customs Act 1962; most exports exempt (promote exports); imports get Basic Customs Duty (BCD)+cesses (protect domestic industry) [B]

## Other Taxes — Surcharge, Cess, Pigouvian
- [ ] Surcharge: additional tax ON income tax, above an income threshold [Parmar]
- [ ] Cess: tax on tax liability, for a specific purpose [Parmar]
- [ ] Surcharge+Cess: levied by Centre, NOT shared with states [Parmar]
- [ ] Pigouvian Tax: tax on negative-externality-causing activities, to reduce the externality [Parmar]
- [ ] Externality def: indirect cost/benefit to an uninvolved third party from another's activity; positive or negative [Parmar]

## Crowding In/Out, Budget Types, Deficit Financing
- [ ] Crowding In: govt spending (infra/public investment) → stimulates private investment → ↑overall economic activity [Parmar]
- [ ] Crowding Out: ↑govt borrowing/expenditure → ↑interest rates → ↓private investment (govt competes for funds) [Parmar][B ★: crowding out = private-investment decline due to high govt borrowing (NOT competition increase/export decline/inflation from printing — tested distractors) ★]
- [ ] Budget types: Balanced (Receipt=Expenditure); Surplus (Receipt>Expenditure); Deficit (Expenditure>Receipt) [Parmar]
- [ ] Deficit Financing (concept by J.M. Keynes): govt expenditure>revenue, gap financed via new money creation or borrowing [Parmar][B: objective = stimulate/accelerate economic growth via ↑public investment (NOT reduce inflation/control black money/decrease money supply — tested distractors) ★]
- [ ] Debt Financing: raise funds via borrowing (banks/FIs/bonds), repay with interest [Parmar]
- [ ] Equity Financing: raise funds via issuing shares, investors become owners, share profits via dividends, no repayment obligation [Parmar]
- [ ] Keynes: during recession, most effective tool = ↑govt expenditure (creates demand/employment via multiplier effect) [B ★, reinforced across multiple Qs: govt should ↑spending+cut taxes = "expansionary fiscal policy" ★]
- [ ] Expansionary fiscal policy = ↓taxes + ↑govt spending → NOT characterised by ↓govt spending (tested "NOT" question) ★
- [ ] Fiscal Policy primary objective [B ★]: influence aggregate demand/economic activity via govt spending+taxation (NOT control money supply/interest rates=monetary policy; NOT trade/BoP/stock market — tested distractors)
- [ ] Fiscal policy examples: govt spending on infrastructure (NOT repo rate/forex regulation/money supply = monetary policy) [B ★]

## Types of Deficit
- [ ] Budgetary Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipt [Parmar][B ★: aka "Fiscal Deficit" in common usage per PYQ explanation — indicates borrowing need ★]
- [ ] Revenue Deficit = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipt [Parmar+Lucent, same][B ★, reinforced many times: RD=RE-RR; concern because govt borrows for consumption, no asset creation, ↑future interest burden — "dis-saving" ★]
- [ ] Effective Revenue Deficit = Revenue Deficit − Grant given for developmental purpose [Parmar]
- [ ] Fiscal Deficit = Total Exp − (Total Receipt − Debt-creating Capital Receipt), OR Total Expenditure − (RR + Non-debt Capital Receipt) [Parmar][B ★ reinforced many times: FD = Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Recovery of Loans + Disinvestment Receipts); = total borrowing requirement, key financial-health indicator ★; worked example: Revenue Receipts₹50L cr, Total Exp₹75L cr, Borrowings₹20L cr → FD=75-50=₹25L cr ★]
- [ ] Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payment [Parmar][B ★, reinforced: = fiscal deficit minus interest on PAST/PREVIOUS loans; indicates borrowing for current expenses excl. interest; "which indicator captures borrowing excl. interest on past debt" = Primary Deficit ★]
- [ ] Deficit def (general): expenses exceed revenue; "expenditure > revenue" = the term for this situation = "Deficit" [B]
- [ ] Revenue deficit ONLY includes current income/expenditure transactions (excludes capital-asset creation/borrowing) — distinguishes it from fiscal/primary/trade deficit [B ★]
- [ ] Continuous borrowing → debt accumulation/"debt trap" (new loans to repay old ones) → ↑interest burden [B ★]

## Fiscal System, Revenue Sources, Public Debt (Lucent §10)
- [ ] Fiscal System def: state's management of revenue+capital expenditure finances (budgetary activities — revenue-raising, borrowing, spending) [Lucent]
- [ ] Fiscal Policy def: use of taxation+public expenditure+public-debt management to achieve specified objectives [Lucent]
- [ ] Centre's revenue: Tax revenue (income/expenditure taxes; property/capital-transaction taxes; commodity/services taxes) + Non-tax revenue (currency/coinage/mint; interest receipts/dividends; other) [Lucent]
- [ ] State's revenue: state tax revenue (land revenue/stamp/registration/estate duty), share in central taxes, income from state enterprises/services [Lucent]
- [ ] Centre's expenditure: Plan (agri/rural dev/irrigation/flood control/energy/industry/transport/comm/S&T/environment) + Non-plan (interest payments/defence/subsidies/general services) [Lucent]
- [ ] State's expenditure: Non-Development + Development Expenditure [Lucent]
- [ ] Public debt: Internal (open-market loans, compensation/prize bonds, treasury bills to RBI/banks) + External (World Bank/IMF/ADB/foreign govts) [Lucent]
- [ ] Internal debt instruments: market loans (dated securities), T-bills (91/182/364-day + 14-day for states only), cash management bills, special RBI securities, compensation/other bonds, non-negotiable non-interest rupee securities to int'l FIs, market-stabilization-scheme securities (reduce gold/import dependency) [Lucent]
- [ ] External debt: from foreign govts/multilateral institutions; Centre doesn't borrow directly from international capital markets; borrows via multilateral/bilateral ODA channels [Lucent]
- [ ] "Other liabilities" (not part of public debt): post office savings deposits, small-savings scheme deposits, post office cash certificates, provident funds, other deposits [Lucent]
- [ ] RBI manages Central+State public debt, acts as their banker (RBI Act 1934, Sections 20/21 for Centre, 21A for States by agreement) [Lucent]
- [ ] Deficit Financing (Lucent's framing): fiscal tool to bridge revenue receipt-expenditure gap [Lucent]
- [ ] 4 deficits per Lucent's budget statement: Revenue, Budget, Fiscal, Primary [Lucent]
- [ ] Revenue Deficit (Lucent) = Total Revenue Expenditure − Total Revenue Receipts = (Non-plan+Plan Expenditure) − (net tax + non-tax revenue) [Lucent]
- [ ] Budget Deficit (Lucent) = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts = (non-plan+plan exp) − (Revenue+Capital Receipts) [Lucent]
- [ ] Fiscal Deficit (Lucent) = Revenue Receipts + Capital Receipts(only loan recoveries+other receipts) − Total Expenditure [Lucent — CONFLICT: sign/ordering differs from Parmar's formula, both preserved]
- [ ] Primary Deficit (Lucent, as printed) = Revenue Deficit − Interest Payments [Lucent — CONFLICT with Parmar's Primary Deficit=Fiscal Deficit−Interest, AND with Lucent's OWN prose definition ("fiscal deficit minus interest payments") — triple inconsistency, all preserved verbatim]
- [ ] Monetised Deficit = Increment in Net RBI Credit to Central Govt [Lucent]
- [ ] Annual Financial Statement = core budget document; kept in 3 parts: Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, Public Account [Lucent]
- [ ] Consolidated Fund: all revenues+loans raised+loan-recovery receipts; all expenditure from it; needs Parliament authorisation to withdraw [Lucent]
- [ ] Contingency Fund: imprest at President's disposal for urgent unforeseen expenditure pending Parliament authorisation; corpus (from India 2023, effective April 2021) = ₹30,000 crore; amount recouped after subsequent Parliamentary approval [Lucent]
- [ ] Public Account: govt acts as banker (provident funds, small savings, deposits); no Parliamentary authorisation needed for these payments [Lucent]
- [ ] Budget = Revenue Budget (current receipts/expenditure — taxation, PSU surplus) + Capital Budget (capital receipts/expenditure — domestic/foreign loans, loan repayments, foreign aid) [Lucent]
- [ ] Article 202: State govts' equivalent AFS requirement (state legislature) [Lucent]
- [ ] Demands for Grants: Consolidated-Fund expenditure estimates voted by Lok Sabha, per Article 113; presented alongside AFS [Lucent]
- [ ] Finance Bill: a Money Bill (Article 110); presented with AFS; details tax imposition/abolition/alteration [Lucent][B ★: Finance Bill's role = tax changes/new tax proposals/rate amendments/tax-rule changes, needs Parliament approval (both Houses); relates to Article 117 too, regulates financial matters beyond Money Bill ★ — Appropriation Bill = separately empowers withdrawal from Consolidated Fund ★]
- [ ] Appropriation Bill: Parliament's approval to withdraw voted Demands-for-Grants amounts + charged expenditure from Consolidated Fund; Article 114(3) — no withdrawal without such enacted law [Lucent]
- [ ] Constitution (80th Amendment) Act, 2000 (retrospective from 1 Apr 1996): most Union-List taxes (excl. Art 268/269 duties, Art 271 surcharges, specific cesses) collected by Centre, distributed Centre-States per President's order on FC recommendation [Lucent]
- [ ] Union tax revenue sources: Customs, Union Excise, Service Tax, Corporate+Income Tax; Non-tax: interest receipts, dividends/profits, fines, regulatory/licence/user charges [Lucent]
