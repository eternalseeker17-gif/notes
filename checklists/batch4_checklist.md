## Inflation — Basics
- [ ] Inflation def: increase in general price level of goods/services; purchasing power decreases [Parmar]
- [ ] During inflation: debtor/borrower benefits more than lender (repaid money worth less) [Parmar]
- [ ] Real Interest Rate = Nominal Interest Rate - Inflation [Parmar]
- [ ] Fisher's Quantity Theory: MV=PT (M=Money Supply, P=Price Level, V=Velocity, T=Transactions); M↑→P↑ (V,T constant); direct M-inflation link [Parmar]
- [ ] Irving Fisher gave "Money Illusion" concept [Parmar]
- [ ] Types of Inflation: Creeping 3-4%, Walking 4-10%, Running 10-20%, Galloping 20-100%, Hyper >100% [Parmar]
- [ ] Inflationary Gap (def 1, p.266): Demand>Supply, Actual GDP>Potential GDP [Parmar]
- [ ] Inflationary Gap (def 2, p.268): excess of aggregate demand over aggregate supply at full-employment level → upward price pressure [Parmar — both printed, kept]
- [ ] Seigniorage: profit earned by central bank via currency issue = Face Value - Production Cost [Parmar]

## Causes & Measurement
- [ ] Demand-Pull Inflation: demand-side; strong growth/govt spending/exports; short-term; "too much money chasing too few goods" [Parmar][B ★: govt ↑spending/↓taxes→↑aggregate demand→firms can't scale production→shortage→price rise = demand-pull ★]
- [ ] Cost-Push Inflation: supply-side; ↑wages/raw material prices/indirect taxes; long-term [Parmar]
- [ ] WPI: wholesale-level price change, pre-consumer; published by Office of Economic Adviser (OEA), Min. of Commerce & Industry; base year 2022-23 (previously 2011-12); monthly (14th) [Parmar]
- [ ] WPI weights: Manufactured goods 64.2%, Primary articles 22.6%, Fuel&Power 13.15% [Parmar]
- [ ] WPI formula = 100×(current-year basket cost/base-year basket cost) [Parmar]
- [ ] CPI: consumer/retail-level price change, cost-of-living; released by NSO/MoSPI; base year 2023-24 (p.266) — CONFLICTS with "CPI Rural/Urban/Combined base year 2024" (p.267, same book) [Parmar — CONFLICT, both kept]; monthly (12th) [Parmar]
- [ ] CPI weights: Food&Beverages 45.8% (highest), Housing 10%, Fuel&Power 6.8% [Parmar]
- [ ] CPI formula = 100×(current-year basket cost/base-year basket cost) [Parmar]
- [ ] 4 CPI types: CPI-IW (base 2016), CPI-AL (base 2019), CPI-RL (base 2019) — released by Labour Bureau; CPI(Rural/Urban/Combined) base 2024, by NSO/MoSPI [Parmar]
- [ ] CPI-Combined used by RBI for inflation targeting/monetary policy [Parmar]
- [ ] Inflation Rate Formula = [(Current Index−Previous Index)/Previous Index]×100 [Parmar]
- [ ] Producer Price Index (PPI): seller's-perspective price change (vs CPI=buyer's); captures "factory gate" prices pre-tax/distribution; leading indicator (cost passed to consumers later) [Parmar]
- [ ] Monetary Policy Framework Agreement (MPFA): signed GoI+RBI, Feb 2015; mandates RBI maintain price stability + CPI inflation target (4±2)% [Parmar]
- [ ] Core Inflation: excludes food+energy prices, calculated via CPI; = Headline Inflation − Volatile Inflation [Parmar]
- [ ] IIP: measures industrial-production growth over time; base year 2022-23; published by NSO/MoSPI [Parmar]
- [ ] 8 core industries = 40% of IIP weight, decreasing order: Refinery Products→Electricity→Steel→Coal→Crude Oil→Natural Gas→Cement→Fertilizers [Parmar]

## Inflation Dynamics Glossary
- [ ] Disinflation: ↓rate of inflation (prices may still rise); measured yr-on-yr; cause=tighter monetary policy/slower money-supply growth [Parmar]
- [ ] Deflation: general price decrease; purchasing power increases; cause=↓money supply/investment/production [Parmar]
- [ ] Phillips Curve: unemployment↔inflation graph, inverse relationship; High inflation=Low unemployment, Low inflation=High unemployment [Parmar]
- [ ] Stagflation = Stagnation+Inflation; high inflation+low growth; →high unemployment; cause=slowdown/decline in production [Parmar][B ★: high inflation combined w/ stagnant growth = Stagflation ★ (lethal combo of high unemployment+rising prices; policy dilemma — controlling inflation worsens unemployment) ★]
- [ ] Shrinkflation: same price, reduced qty/size → ↑price-per-unit [Parmar]
- [ ] Bottleneck Inflation: restricted production/supply at a stage → shortages/price rise [Parmar]
- [ ] Skewflation: essential-goods (food) prices rise sharply vs non-essentials stable/slow — uneven pattern [Parmar]
- [ ] Reflation: deliberate govt policy →↑economic activity/prices via ↑money supply/govt expenditure/↓taxes, to recover from recession [Parmar]
- [ ] Imported Inflation: ↑import/raw-material prices → ↑domestic price level [Parmar]
- [ ] Inflation Target: specific rate central bank aims to maintain via monetary policy [Parmar]
- [ ] Keynesian recession response [B ★, reinforced]: govt should ↑spending+↓taxes → stimulate aggregate demand = "Expansionary Fiscal Policy" ★

## Unemployment — Measurement (NSSO)
- [ ] Unemployment def (Parmar): actively seeking job, unable to find work [Parmar]
- [ ] NSSO's 3 measurement methods: Usual Status Approach, Current Weekly Status Approach, Daily Status Approach [Parmar]
- [ ] Usual Status (US): reference period=365 days; identifies chronic/long-term unemployment; 2 components — Principal Status (PS, majority-time activity) + Subsidiary Status (SS, ≥30 days/yr additional activity); combined = "Usual Status (PS+SS)" [Parmar]
- [ ] Current Weekly Status (CWS): in labour force last 1 week; employed if worked ≥1hr on any 1 day [Parmar]
- [ ] Current Daily Status (CDS): measures unemployment intensity; in labour force 1 day; >4hrs=100% engaged, 1-4hrs=50% engaged, <1hr=unemployed [Parmar]
- [ ] LFPR: % of population in labour force (working/available); WPR: % of population employed; UR: % unemployed among labour force [Parmar]

## Types of Unemployment (Parmar)
- [ ] Structural: skill mismatch vs job requirements; eg tech replacing farm labour [Parmar]
- [ ] Educated: degree-holders unable to find work, aka graduate unemployment; urban India [Parmar]
- [ ] Frictional: short-term, job-search driven, aka Search/Voluntary unemployment; eg switching jobs in cities [Parmar]
- [ ] Disguised: more workers than needed, removal wouldn't hurt productivity; eg farm family of 5 needing only 3 [Parmar]
- [ ] Cyclical: labour force reduced due to business cycles; opportunity returns when economy revives; mostly urban; eg Great Recession [Parmar]
- [ ] Great Recession def: prolonged downturn ≥2 consecutive quarters, eg US Subprime crisis; prolonged recession = Depression, eg Great Depression USA 1929-1939 [Parmar]
- [ ] Seasonal: unemployment at specific times of year; mostly rural; eg firework sellers off-season [Parmar]
- [ ] Classical (Real Wage) Unemployment: real wage too high → more job-seekers than openings; eg workers demand more than company can pay [Parmar]
- [ ] Natural Unemployment: lowest sustainable unemployment level without causing inflation [Parmar]
- [ ] Marginal Worker: employed <6 months/183 days; eg landless agri labourers, low-wage industry workers. Main Worker: employed >6 months/183 days; eg construction workers, seasonal employees, independent contractors [Parmar]
- [ ] Elderly Dependency Ratio [B ★]: ratio of elderly (65+) to working-age population (NOT children-to-working-age, elderly-to-children, or urban-rural elderly ratio — tested distractors ★)

## Unemployment — Lucent §7 Concepts (merge with Parmar's types, avoid duplication)
- [ ] Unemployment (Lucent) = able+willing workers seeking jobs at prevailing wage, unable to get it; in India = structural (capital/resource shortage), NOT Keynesian demand-deficiency [Lucent]
- [ ] Lucent's 7 types (merge with Parmar's list above where same; add new ones): Cyclical (=Parmar); Frictional (=Parmar, phrased as new industries replacing old); Open Unemployment — able+willing but NO work at all [Lucent, NEW]; Seasonal (=Parmar); Educated (=Parmar); Under-employment/Disguised (=Parmar, phrased as producing less than capable of, eg engineer working as clerk); Compulsory Unemployment — willing to work at current rate but can't get work [Lucent, NEW]
- [ ] Lakadawala Formula: used by Planning Commission (pre-NITI Aayog) to collect unemployment data, effective 11 March 1997; prior method = NSSO surveys [Lucent]
- [ ] NSSO's own 3 concepts (Lucent's framing, distinct wording from the US/CWS/CDS above): General Status (>1yr unemployed, long-term); Weekly-unemployment (no work even 1hr/week); Daily unemployment (considered the "best" concept) [Lucent]
- [ ] Main unemployment causes in India (Lucent): slow economic development, population explosion, outdated technique, improper education system, limited govt-planning effect [Lucent]
- [ ] Employment trends: unemployment rate (15+) fell 7.7% (May 2023) from 8.5% (Apr 2023) — CMIE data, due to ↓labour participation (441.9 million, LPR fell 1.1pt to 39.6%) [Lucent]
- [ ] PLFS 2023-24: unemployment rate (15+) declined 6% (2017-18)→3.2% (2023-24); LFPR+WPR also increased [Lucent]
- [ ] Q2 FY25: urban unemployment rate (15+) improved to 6.4% (vs 6.6% Q2 FY24) [Lucent]
- [ ] EPFO net subscriptions: 61 lakh (FY19)→131 lakh (FY24), more than doubled; Apr-Nov 2024 net additions=95.6 lakh, driven by youth (18-25yrs=47% of net payroll additions) [Lucent]
- [ ] Ninth Plan: 3.6 crore fresh unemployed began seeking jobs [Lucent]

## MGNREGS / NREGA Full Detail
- [ ] NREG Bill passed Parliament 7 Sept 2005; Presidential assent same year → NREGA [Lucent]
- [ ] Act provides ≥100 days employment/yr to 1 able-bodied person per rural household [Lucent]
- [ ] NREGA came into force 2 Feb 2006; initially 200 districts → now covers all 740 rural districts [Lucent]
- [ ] Renamed MGNREGA — 2 Oct 2009 [Lucent]
- [ ] Cost sharing: Centre 75% + State 25% (material cost + skilled/semi-skilled wages) [Lucent]
- [ ] FY2025-26 wage hike: 2.33%-7.48% range, ₹7-₹26 increase; ₹7 hike in 5 states; highest ₹26 in Haryana (→₹400) [Lucent]
- [ ] Employment Guarantee Act 2005 — full 9-point features: (1) ≥100 days/yr guaranteed employment, casual manual labour at statutory min wage, paid within 7 days; (2) work provided within 15 days of demand, within 5km; (3) unemployment allowance (≥1/3 min wage) if work not provided in time; (4) medical treatment/hospitalisation for injury + daily allowance ≥half min wage; ex-gratia to legal heirs on death/disability (Workmen Compensation Act); (5) 5% wage deduction possible for welfare schemes (health/accident/survivor/maternity insurance); (6) strict penalties for non-compliance; (7) full transparency/public scrutiny of accounts; (8) District Collector/CEO responsible at district level; (9) Gram Sabha monitors Gram Panchayat via social audit [Lucent]

## Development & Employment Programmes — Full Detail

- [ ] PMKVY: Union Cabinet cleared 21 Mar 2015; skill training for 1.4 million youth, ₹1,120 crore outlay; implemented by Min. of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship via NSDC; targets Class-X/XII dropouts/fresh labour-market entrants; 1.56 crore trained since 2015 (till 8 Oct 2024), 54% aged 15-29 [Lucent]
- [ ] ABRY (Aatmanirbhar Bharat Rojgar Yojana): part of AB 3.0 package, post-COVID employment recovery/incentive; implemented via EPFO; till Mar 2024: 1,52,517 establishments claimed ₹10,188.50 crore benefits for 60.49 lakh beneficiaries [Lucent]
