## Monetary Policy — Balance Sheet, Reserves, MPC
- [ ] Bank Balance Sheet: Liabilities=Deposits(Demand[savings/current]+Time/Term[FDs/RDs]); Assets=Loans granted [Parmar]
- [ ] NDTL = total deposits determining lendable funds+reserve requirements; Demand Liabilities(withdraw anytime) + Time Liabilities(fixed tenure) [Parmar]
- [ ] CRR: cash with RBI, no interest | SLR: liquid assets(cash/gold/securities) held by bank, interest earned [Parmar][B ★: SLR = mandatory liquid-asset(gold/G-Sec) requirement ★ — the tested answer, not CRR/current-ratio/margin-ratio]
- [ ] MPC: based on Urjit Patel Committee recommendations; 6 members (3 RBI: Governor+Dy.Governor+Executive, Governor=Chair; 3 GoI); meets bimonthly [Parmar][B ★: Keynesian theory alt-name = Liquidity Preference Theory ★ (not Cambridge/Cash-Balance/Classical — tested distractor)]

## Monetary Policy Tools — Quantitative & Qualitative
- [ ] Quantitative tools: CRR, SLR, Bank Rate(long-term loan to banks, NO collateral), OMO(=sterilization: Outright Purchase[no repurchase agreement] + Repo["Rere"=Repurchase Obligation, banks borrow w/ G-Secs collateral]+Reverse Repo[RBI borrows from banks]) [Parmar]
- [ ] Repo+SDF(Special Depository Facility, no-collateral bank deposit w/ RBI) together = LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility) [Parmar]
- [ ] MSF: emergency overnight bank borrowing from RBI, above repo rate; penalty for CRR/SLR default=3% above Bank Rate/day; uses G-Secs within SLR quota; only scheduled commercial banks [Parmar]
- [ ] Base Rate: minimum rate below which banks can't lend [Parmar]
- [ ] Interbank Lending Market/Call Money Rate: decided on RRR+MSF basis; short-term overnight interbank loans; limit=2% of NDTL [Parmar]
- [ ] MCLR: introduced by RBI, April 2016; transparent/policy-responsive minimum lending rate [Parmar]
- [ ] Money Multiplier(MM) = 1/CRR; NBFCs/NBFC-MFIs exempt from CRR/SLR; ↑CRR→↓MM (and vice versa) [Parmar][B ★: inverse CRR-multiplier relationship ★; cash held by public = "leakage" reducing multiplier ★]
- [ ] Monetary policy stance table: Inflation→↑CRR/SLR/BR/RRR/RR→↓money supply→Contractionary/Tight/Dear/Hawkish; Deflation→↓ same→↑money supply→Expansionary/Easy/Dovish [Parmar][B ★: expansionary = central bank cuts rates to stimulate growth in a slowdown ★]
- [ ] Qualitative tools: (1) Changing Margin Requirements(↑for non-essential, ↓for agri); (2) Consumer Credit Regulation(loan instalment/down-payment/repayment rules); (3) Moral Suasion(persuasion/implicit threats); (4) Credit Rationing(limits loans per bank/sector) [Parmar]
- [ ] Fiscal Policy anti-inflation steps: ↑Taxes(↓disposable income), ↓Subsidies(↑prices, discourage consumption), ↓MSP(discourage overproduction, control food prices) [Parmar]
- [ ] Monetary Policy=RBI's tool; Fiscal Policy=Govt's tool [Parmar]
- [ ] Debt Monetisation [B ★]: RBI directly buys govt bonds to fund fiscal deficit, prints currency → ↑money supply → often high inflation ★ (distinguish from ↑interest rates/↓money supply/foreign borrowing — tested distractors)
- [ ] A-R [B ★]: Financial intermediaries essential in transmitting monetary policy (TRUE) — because they adjust lending/deposit rates per MPC's policy rate (TRUE, correct explanation) ★
- [ ] A-R [B ★]: CRR/SLR requirements "increase" lending capacity = FALSE (they actually REDUCE it) — reserve requirements reduce banks' surplus lendable funds (Reason correct) ★

## Liquidity, Money Functions & Theories (Ch.8)
- [ ] Liquidity order: Cash>Gold>Cheque>Bond; Demand Deposit>Term Deposit in liquidity [Parmar]
- [ ] Barter limitations: Double Coincidence of Wants, Lack of Divisibility, Lack of Store of Value [Parmar][B ★: money eliminates double-coincidence problem via "measure of value"+"medium of exchange" ★ (barter itself DECREASES efficiency, not increases — tested A-R)]
- [ ] Money functions: Primary=Medium of Exchange; Secondary=Medium of Value/Unit of Account→(a)Standard of Deferred Payment,(b)Store of Value(no intrinsic value, paper value from govt backing),(c)Portable/Durable [Parmar]
- [ ] Liquidity Preference Theory = Keynes's theory of money demand — rates determined by money D/S not just savings/investment [Parmar]
- [ ] Cambridge Cash Balance Approach: developed by Marshall, Pigou, Keynes; value of money ← demand to hold cash balances (not transaction velocity) [Parmar][B ★: Cambridge approach = "Cash Balance Approach" alt-name ★ (vs Fisher's = velocity-of-transactions approach ★)]
- [ ] 3 reasons to hold money: Transaction Motive(daily spending), Precautionary Motive(emergencies), Speculative Motive(by J.M.Keynes, investment timing) [Parmar]
- [ ] Paradox of Thrift(Keynesian): individual ↑savings in downturn → collectively ↓aggregate demand+total savings economy-wide [Parmar]
- [ ] Speculative Demand for Money ∝ 1/Interest Rate; rates↑→bonds preferred→speculative demand↓; rates↓→more cash held→speculative demand↑; "interest = opportunity cost of holding money" [Parmar]
- [ ] Liquidity Trap: rates low, people hold cash not invest→monetary policy ineffective; horizontal LP-curve portion = infinitely/perfectly elastic money demand [Parmar]

- [ ] MIBOR: India's LIBOR-equivalent; replaced by FBIL-Overnight MIBOR in 2015 [Parmar]
