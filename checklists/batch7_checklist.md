## Pre-Independence Planning Proposals
- [ ] Visvesvaraya Plan(1934): India's earliest blueprint for planned econ development, "Planned Economy for India 1934", first systematic economic planning proposal [Parmar]
- [ ] National Planning Committee(1938): formed by S.C.Bose, 1st Chairman J.L.Nehru; first attempt at a National Plan; per Lucent also: 1938 All India Congress Committee demanded planning [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] Bombay Plan(1944): "A Plan of Economic Development for India", by leading capitalists incl J.R.D.Tata(Parmar)/"8 industrialists"(Lucent, DOUBT-count); published 2 parts(1944+1945) [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] Gandhian Plan(1944): emphasised "decentralised economic structure"/"self-contained villages"; per Lucent brought out by Acharya S.N.Agarwala in 1944, reaffirmed 1948; author printed as "Sriman Narayan" elsewhere in same book — internal Lucent conflict [Parmar+Lucent merge][DOUBT: author name conflict]
- [ ] Peoples Plan(1945 per Parmar / April 1944 per Lucent — DOUBT): by M.N.Roy, Chairman Post-War Reconstruction Committee of Indian Trade Union/"labour leader" [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] Sarvodaya Plan(1950 per Parmar / 30 Jan 1950 per Lucent): by Jaiprakash Narayan(formed Congress Socialist Party 1934); "first blueprint" of planned development of Independent India [Parmar+Lucent merge]
- [ ] 5-yr-plan concept borrowed from USSR; Joseph Stalin introduced it 1928 to industrialize Soviet Union [Parmar]
- [ ] Post-independence(1947): committee on economic planning constituted under J.L.Nehru; on its recommendation, Planning Commission constituted March 1950(Lucent)/1950(Parmar); format of 1st FYP prepared 1951 [Lucent]

## Types of Planning & Models of Development (Lucent §6)
- [ ] Types: Imperative Planning(Central authority decides every aspect, socialist economies); Indicative Planning(State sets broad parameters/goals only, adopted in India since 8th FYP); Perspective Planning(15-20yr long-term, via FYPs+Annual Plans); Rolling Plan(3 steps: current-yr plan+3-5yr plan revised annually+10-20yr perspective plan); Core Plan(Planning Commission asks states for revenue estimates→determines Annual Plan expenditure heads, prevents fund diversion) [Lucent]
- [ ] Nehru-Mahalanobis Model: driving force since 2nd FYP, continued to the 1980s; aimed at enlarging opportunities for less-privileged; "Growth with social justice"; State controlled commanding heights via public sector [Lucent]
- [ ] Gandhian Model of Growth: basic objective=raise material+cultural level of Indian masses; emphasis on scientific agri development+cottage/village industries; Nehru prioritized heavy industry, Gandhian model prioritized agriculture+handicrafts+cottage industries [Lucent]
- [ ] LPG Model of Development: introduced 1991 by FM Manmohan Singh; bigger role for private sector; envisages large FDI; export-led growth(vs earlier import substitution); reduces State's role, abandons "planning fundamentalism" [Lucent]

## Industrial Policy Resolutions — 1948, 1956
- [ ] 1st IPR(1948): first official industrial policy of independent India; led by Dr.Shyama Prasad Mukherjee(Industry&Commerce Minister); announced/presented in Parliament 1948 ★; declared India a Mixed Economy; consensus for public+private coexistence ★; 4 industry categories: Strategic(Public-Railways/defence/atomic energy), Basic/Key(Public-cum-Private-coal/steel/aircraft/telecom), Regulated(Controlled Private-sugar/cement/textiles), Other(Private&Cooperative-consumer goods/SSI); started License Raj; laid foundation for public-sector dominance [Parmar+B merge]
- [ ] Karve Committee(Village&Small-Scale Industries Committee): est. by Planning Commission 1955, chair Dattatreya Gopal Karve; promoted rural industrialisation/employment/balanced growth via SSIs during 2nd FYP; recommended labour-intensive small-scale over large-scale capital-intensive industry ★ [Parmar+B merge]
- [ ] 2nd IPR(1956): "Economic Constitution of India" ★; objective="growth with equity" ★/accelerate econ development+equitable income-wealth distribution; framework for socialist industrial growth, "socialistic pattern of society"; gave public sector "commanding heights"(doctrine coined by Vladimir Lenin,1922 — control over heavy industries/mining/infrastructure); classified industries into 3 Schedules: A(17 industries-Public Sector Monopoly: aviation/atomic energy/railways/arms/steel), B(12 industries-Public&Private: aluminium/machine tools/chemicals), C(All Other-Private: consumer goods/textiles/automobiles) ★; based on Mahalanobis Model; encouraged industries in backward regions to reduce regional inequality; licensing objective=state control over resource-use per planned priorities, promote regional equality, prevent monopolies ★ [Parmar+B merge]
- [ ] Lucent §13 framing: IPR1948=importance of both sectors but basic industries→Public Sector responsibility; IPR1956 gave public sector strategic/pre-eminent role, reduced 4 categories(1948)→3(1956); main objective=develop public+cooperative sector, control private monopoly [Lucent]
- [ ] Joint Sector: partnership btwn public+private sector; Dutta Committee(1973) recommended establishment of Joint Venture Sector in Indian Industry ★ [Parmar+B merge]

## Industrial Policy Resolutions — 1977, 1980, 1991 & MRTP/FERA
- [ ] 3rd IPR(1977, extension of 1956 policy): introduced by Morarji Desai Govt(Janata Party); emphasised SSI growth; "Tiny Unit" concept(investment <₹1 lakh); restrictions on MNCs(self-reliance focus); focus=decentralisation of industries+priority to SSIs, introduced concept of District Industries Centres(shift industrial dev from big cities to small towns) ★ [Parmar+B merge]
- [ ] 4th IPR(1980, Indira Gandhi Govt): shifted focus back to heavy industries(Parmar) vs Lucent's framing="influenced by federalism, concessions to agri-based industries"[DOUBT: differing emphasis]; encouraged foreign tech transfer/investment(FERA Act 1973 printed under this policy — date mismatch ⚠); MRTP Act(1969) amended to remove asset-threshold limits for MRTP companies; tiny-unit investment limit ↑to ₹2 lakhs [Parmar]
- [ ] MRTP Act: all firms w/ assets above threshold(₹100cr since 1985) classified MRTP firms, restricted entry to selected industries case-by-case; scrapped by IPR1991; replaced by Competition Act 2002 → Competition Commission of India(CCI) established, checks abuse of dominance/anti-competitive agreements ★ [Lucent+B merge]
- [ ] IPR(1991) — Economic Liberalisation: announced 24 July1991(Lucent/PYQ specific date) ★; marked shift from inward-looking(closed) to outward-oriented(open) economy; introduced under PM P.V.Narasimha Rao, reforms led by FM Manmohan Singh, during 1991 BoP crisis ★ [Parmar+Lucent+B merge]
- [ ] Before-1991(inward-looking): License Raj, Public Sector Dominance, High import barriers(tariffs&quotas), Restricted FDI, Import substitution strategy → Result: low competition/inefficiency/slow growth [Parmar]
- [ ] LPG Model terms: Liberalisation(↓govt regulation/licences/restrictions, frees markets); Privatisation(shift ownership/mgmt of PSUs to private entities, ↑efficiency); DIPAM(Dept of Investment & Public Asset Mgmt, earlier Dept of Disinvestment, under Min. of Finance) established to manage disinvestment [Parmar]
- [ ] Key Measures(1991): End of License Raj(except few industries), Disinvestment in PSUs, FDI liberalisation(automatic route), ↓public-sector monopoly, greater market-force role [Parmar]
- [ ] Macroeconomic Reforms(1991): Rupee Devaluation in 2 stages(9% 1July+11% 2July, Parmar specific %s) ★ cumulative~18-20%; Fiscal Deficit(1990-91)~10% of GDP; Anti-Dumping Duty introduced; HDFC Bank=first private-sector bank after liberalisation [Parmar]
- [ ] After-1991(outward-oriented): ↑FDI inflows(tech access+employment); growth of private sector(↑competition/efficiency); shift ISI(Import Substitution Industrialization)→EOI(Export-Oriented Industrialization); global economic integration; emergence of BPO sector; adopted Managed Floating Exchange Rate [Parmar]
- [ ] Structural changes: services sector share of GDP ↑~39%(1991-92)→55-60%(recent); agriculture GDP share ↓~30%(1990-91)→15-18%(recent); manufacturing share largely stagnated [Parmar]
- [ ] IPR1991 specifics(Lucent): abolished all industrial licensing except security/strategic/social/safety/environmental/hazardous/elitist-consumption industries; FDI up to 51% equity in high-priority industries approved; industries reserved for public sector(17 since 1956)→reduced to 4 areas(arms&ammunition/atomic energy/mineral oils per 1953 order/railways); mandatory convertibility clause(banks' option to convert loans→equity) abolished; industrial-location policy liberalised(no approval needed outside million-plus cities except compulsory-licensing industries; non-polluting-only within 25km of million-plus city periphery); Phased Manufacturing Programmes abolished for new projects [Lucent]
- [ ] IPR1991 focus per PYQ: (I)allowing foreign investment via lower controls/regulations + (II)exit policy for sick private-sector units(smooth exit w/o affecting labour welfare) — both correct ★ [B]
- [ ] Trade policy reforms 1991: reduced import tariffs on capital goods, abolished quantitative restrictions+licensing system(→easier foreign/advanced tech access, globally competitive manufacturing) ★; removed quotas on imports, drastically ↓export duties, abolished import licensing(except hazardous/environmentally-sensitive goods) ★ [B]
- [ ] LERMS(Liberalised Exchange Rate Management System): dual exchange-rate system, introduced 1992(March1992 per PYQ, note vs Parmar's "1992 LERMS" general mention) ★; part of forex converted at market rate, part at RBI-fixed rate [B]
- [ ] FDI limit in foreign private banks progressively ↑to 74%(post-1991 financial-sector reform) ★ [B]
- [ ] Washington Consensus does NOT include export subsidies — instead advocates trade liberalisation+eliminating subsidies for market competition ★(tested distractor) [B]

## Disinvestment & Privatisation
- [ ] Types of disinvestment(Parmar): Minority(govt retains >51%+mgmt control, sells small portion), Majority(govt stake <51%, majority control to private), Strategic(sale of ≥50% shareholding+mgmt-control transfer), Complete Privatization/Full Exit(govt sells 100% equity) [Parmar]
- [ ] Lucent's framing: disinvestment beyond 51% govt stake=change to majority-private control; Privatisation=change in ownership+management; Disinvestment=wider term(dilution of stake→transfer of ownership) [Lucent]
- [ ] Disinvestment Commission: constituted 1996(August1996 specifically per Lucent/B) to advise on PSU disinvestment; first Chairman=G.V.Ramakrishna(printed "Ramkrishna" in one instance) ★; suggested core-sector max 49% disinvestment, non-core up to 74%; shares to small investors/employees(wide dispersal/worker shareholding); greater PSE autonomy suggested [Lucent+B merge]
- [ ] VRS("Golden Hand Shake Scheme"): to minimise financial burden on PSEs, full compensation given to employees exiting voluntarily [Lucent]
- [ ] Globalisation(def): integrates national economy w/ global economy via ↑trade/FDI/free movement of goods&tech [Parmar]
- [ ] Minority sale = govt sells <51% of PSU shares to private investors/public, management/control stays with govt ★ [B]
- [ ] Strategic Disinvestment/Privatisation = govt sells >51% stake + transfers management control ★ [B]
- [ ] Disinvestment primary objectives: ↓fiscal deficit, reallocate resources(health/education), ↑PSU efficiency; leads to reduction in govt's financial assets(not increase, not a liability, not a tax-revenue tool) ★ [B]
- [ ] A-R: disinvestment widely criticised in India(TRUE, due to lack of social security+sale of strategic assets at low prices) — but NOT because it increased PSU market monopoly(Reason FALSE; objective was actually to promote privatisation/competition) ★ [B]

## New Economic Policy 1991 & Reforms (Lucent §8 + PYQ)
- [ ] NEP: aim=reform production pattern, adopt new tech, use capacity fully; first devised/implemented 1985(PM Rajiv Gandhi); second wave 1991(PM P.V.Narasimha Rao); main 1991 trigger=Gulf War+BoP problem [Lucent]
- [ ] 3 main NEP objectives: Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation; main reform sectors: Fiscal/Monetary/Value(Price) Fixation/Foreign/Industrial/Foreign Investment/Business/Public Sector Policy [Lucent]
- [ ] Economic Reforms(1991): First Generation Reforms=macro-level stabilisation(liberalisation/deregulation of industry, financial-sector reforms, taxation reforms); Second Generation Reforms=micro-level structural changes(labour/land/capital-market reforms, expenditure/power-sector reforms) [Lucent]
- [ ] Poverty declined 36%(1993)→26%(end of 10th plan, Lucent as printed); inequality increased per World Bank Report 1999-2000 [Lucent]
- [ ] Reforms(1991→2005-06) made economy "more open, liberal, global" [Lucent]
- [ ] Large-scale reforms initiated 24 July1991(PYQ specific date, matches IPR1991 date) ★, PM P.V.Narasimha Rao+FM Manmohan Singh, "Rao-Manmohan model"/"New Economic Policy"(NEP), based on LPG model ★; objective=overcome BoP crisis, dismantle License Raj, make economy globally competitive ★ [B]
- [ ] 1991 crisis root cause per PYQ: external-debt crisis+BoP crisis; forex sufficient for only ~2 weeks of imports; immediate cause=oil-price rise from Gulf War+rising foreign-loan interest burden → govt forced to mortgage gold ★ [B]
- [ ] Stabilisation measures(short-term objective): control inflation(~17% at the time) + improve BoP via forex-crisis management; rupee devalued ~18-20% in 2 stages July1991 ★ [B]
- [ ] Rupee devalued 1991 to: boost exports+attract forex(NOT reduce inflation/increase foreign aid, distractors) ★ [B]
- [ ] IMF loan ~$7bn to India(1991) conditional on LPG reforms; IBRD/World Bank provided policy guidance+funds for structural(long-term) reforms ★ [B]
- [ ] NOT a goal of 1991 reforms: expanding public-sector control(reforms aimed to LIMIT public-sector role, ↑private participation) ★ [B, repeated as distractor across multiple Qs]
- [ ] Trade-policy reforms post-1991 ↓tariffs+removed licensing on capital-good imports → made Indian mfg competitive globally, easier machinery import ★ [B]
- [ ] Liberalisation objective: remove barriers to entry/growth of firms(License Raj restricted entrepreneurial freedom/competition) ★; ended "license-permit raj"(govt permission needed for nearly every business decision) ★ [B]
- [ ] Economic liberalisation affects ALL 3 sectors(Primary/Secondary/Tertiary) — ↑investment/competition/efficiency ★ [B]
- [ ] A-R: 1991 reforms opened economy to foreign investors(TRUE) — but NOT because govt wanted to rapidly ↑domestic savings rate(Reason FALSE; real primary reason=BoP crisis+forex shortage) ★ [B]
- [ ] Sectors excluded from licensing post-1991: alcohol/cigarettes/hazardous chemicals/industrial explosives kept under compulsory licensing(security/health/environment reasons); textiles/fertilizers/chemicals EXEMPTED from licensing ★ [B]
- [ ] "Streamlining of regulations" = simplifying/clarifying business rules to ↓red tape, promote EoDB ★ [B]
- [ ] Ease of Doing Business(EoDB) 'Enforcing Contracts' indicator = measures time/cost/quality of judicial process for resolving commercial disputes ★; 'Starting a Business' = one of 10 EoDB indicators, not weighted more than others ★ [B]
- [ ] MCA21 = e-governance initiative(Ministry of Corporate Affairs) that digitised company registration/compliance("Starting a Business" EoDB improvement); SPICe+ integrated forms(name/registration/PAN/TAN together) ★ [B]
