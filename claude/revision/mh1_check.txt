5.1 Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798)
Indian rulers paid for a British garrison stationed on their territory and ceded control of external relations.
Adopted by: Hyderabad (first, 1798) → Mysore (1799) → Tanjore (1799) → ★Awadh (1801, ceded half
territory incl. Rohilkhand & the Doab) → Peshwa (1802, per Parmar).
  DOUBT     Lucent's own annexation table instead dates Maratha-confederacy and Nawab of
 Carnatic/Arcot both to 1801 under Subsidiary Alliance, and does not list Carnatic in Parmar's adopter
 list at all; Parmar gives Peshwa 1802. Not reconciled.


 6 Punjab — Sikh Empire & Anglo-Sikh Wars
  ★ Dal Khalsa — combined army of 18th-c. Sikh jathas/misls, established 1748 under Nawab Kapur
  Singh.
  ★ Maharaja Ranjit Singh (Sukerchakia Misl, one of 12 misls) captured Lahore 1799 and made it his
  capital; formally assumed the title "Maharaja" April 1801, founding the Sikh Empire; Treaty of Amritsar
  (1809) fixed the Sutlej as the boundary with British territory. Died 1839; successors Kharak
  Singh→Naunihal Singh→Sher Singh→Duleep Singh amid instability.
  1st Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46): Sikhs defeated at Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal (Sir Harry Smith) &
  Sobraon → Treaty of Lahore + Treaty of Bhairowal (1846); Treaty of Amritsar (1846) ceded Kashmir to
  Gulab Singh for ₹75 lakh; Sir Henry Lawrence, first Resident.
  2nd Anglo-Sikh War (1848–49): Mulraj (Governor of Multan) killed 2 British officers → rebellion;
  Duleep Singh captured, Kohinoor taken; Dalhousie annexed Punjab (1849); Sir John Lawrence, first
  Chief Commissioner of Punjab.

 8 Sindh (PYQ-book-only addition)
★ Sindh was annexed in 1843 under Governor-General Lord Ellenborough (cross-ref Ch.9,
Governors-General & Viceroys — neither source book covers Sindh's annexation anywhere in the Modern
History chapters used for this file). The PYQ book's own explanations add the operational detail: the
conquest is credited to Sir Charles Napier, who defeated the Amirs of Sindh at the Battle of Miani.
Napier himself admitted the annexation was morally/legally unjustified, describing it as "a very
advantageous, useful, and humane piece of rascality" — a line directly tested via an Assertion-Reason
question. None of this (Napier, Miani, the "rascality" quote) is present in either source book; sourced here
to the PYQ book's explanation text alone, per standing practice for PYQ-only facts.
