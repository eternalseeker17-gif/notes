2Brahmo Samaj & the Bengal Reform Family

  • Raja Rammohan Roy: Atmiya Sabha, Calcutta, 1814 (Parmar) / 1815 (Lucent) — forerunner of Brahmo Samaj → renamed Brahmo Sabha → Brahmo Samaj (1828). Founded Hindu College Calcutta (1817, with David Hare); Vedanta College (1825). Against idol worship, polytheism, child marriage; translated the Vedas into Bengali. Journal: Sambad Kaumudi (Parmar) / Sabad Kaumudi, 1819 (Lucent); also Mirat-ul-Akhbar (Persian), Gift to Monotheists, Precepts of Jesus. Titled "Raja" by Akbar II.
  • Debendranath Tagore: Tattvabodhini Sabha + Tattvabodhini Patrika (1839); invited Keshav Chandra Sen (1858); amalgamated with Brahmo Samaj (1859, Lucent); compiled Brahma Dharma from the Upanishads (Lucent).
  • 1866 split: Brahmo Samaj of India (Keshav Chandra Sen) vs Adi Brahmo Samaj (Debendranath Tagore). Keshav Chandra Sen also founded Veda Samaj/Brahmo Samaj of South India (Madras, 1864, with K. Sridharalu Naidu); pushed the Native Marriage Act 1872 via his Indian Reform Association (1870).
  • 1878: Sadharan Brahmo Samaj, Ananda Mohan Bose (further split from Adi Brahmo Samaj).
DOUBTAtmiya Sabha's founding year: Parmar 1814 vs Lucent 1815. Journal name: Parmar "Sambad Kaumudi" vs Lucent "Sabad Kaumudi." Not reconciled.
TRAPDharma Sabha (Radhakanta Deb, revivalist, against Brahmo Samaj) — Parmar 1830, Lucent 1829 — not to be confused with the reformist Brahmo Samaj itself.
  • Young Bengal Movement (late 1820s–early 1830s) — Henry Vivian Derozio, teacher at Hindu College.
  • DOUBT Paramhansa Mandali — Parmar dates it 1849 (its own printed asterisk: "SSC considers this year as 1840"), founders Dadoba Pandurang & Durgaram Mehtaji, Maharashtra; Lucent's table gives 1849, Bombay, Dadoba Pandurang only, and separately lists a different 1844 Surat body, Manav Dharma Sabha (Durgaram Mancharam) — possibly the same "Durgaram" figure under two entries; not reconciled.
  • Prarthana Samaj (1867, Bombay) — Atmaram Pandurang; M.G. Ranade joined 1870, R.G. Bhandarkar also a member (Parmar).

3Arya Samaj, Aligarh & Deoband

Arya Samaj (1875) — founder Swami Dayanand Saraswati (b. Mool/Mula Shankar); first unit Bombay, later Lahore; slogans "Go Back to Vedas," "India for Indians"; caste-less/class-less society but subscribed to Chaturvarna by work not birth; against idol worship; believed Karma/Reincarnation; considered Vedas infallible, criticised the Puranas; wrote Satyarth Prakash. DAV College, Lahore, 1886, founded by Mahatma Hansraj in Dayanand's memory (per Parmar). 1893: Arya Samaj Punjab split into "Mahatma" group (vegetarian) vs "Cultured Party."

DOUBTThe PYQ book's own tested answer for "who set up a Dayanand Anglo-Vedic school in Lahore" is Lala Lajpat Rai (established 1 June 1886), not Mahatma Hansraj as Parmar's raw extraction names — a genuine, unreconciled conflict between the book source and the PYQ book's own explanation. Both given; not resolved. See A.1/A.2.

Aligarh Movement (1875) — Sir Syed Ahmad Khan; Mohammaden Anglo-Oriental College (1875) → Aligarh Muslim University (1920); Indian Patriotic Association (1888); magazine Tahdhib-ul-Akhlaq. PYQLucent separately lists a distinct "Muhammadan Educational Conference, Aligarh, 1886, Syed Ahmad Khan" — not the same institution as the College, per the raw extraction's own note.

Deoband Movement (1866), Saharanpur, UP — Parmar: Muhammad Qasim Nanautavi & Rashid Ahmad Gangohi.

DOUBTLucent instead names founder "Maulana Hussain Ahmed" — a direct cross-book conflict, not reconciled.

  • Singh Sabha Movement (1873, Amritsar) — revive Sikh faith, reconvert lapsed Sikhs.
  • Dev Samaj (1887, Lahore) — Shiv Narayan Agnihotri.
  • Shuddhi Movement — Arya Samaj's reconversion-to-Hinduism campaign (from Islam/Christianity).
