 1 Land Revenue Systems
 System                                Detail
 Permanent Settlement (1793) Lord Cornwallis; planned by John Shore; a.k.a. "Zamindari System"; Bengal,
                             Bihar, Orissa, UP, MP (Parmar) / Benaras & N. Madras districts (Lucent);
                             zamindars keep 1/11th revenue, British get 10/11th (Lucent); "sunset
                             clause" — failure to pay by a fixed date → confiscation/auction of the
                             (hereditary) zamindari
 ★Ryotwari (1820)                      Thomas Munro & Captain Alexander Read (Parmar) / Charles Reed (Lucent,
                                       DOUBT on 2nd name); Ricardian Theory of Rent; direct
                                       farmer("Ryot")↔state settlement; South India (Madras)/Bombay & Madras
                                       (Lucent); revenue fixed ≤30 yrs by soil quality/crop; made cultivator's
                                       position more secure but vulnerable to moneylenders
 Mahalwari (1822)                      Holt Mackenzie (concept), reformed by William Bentinck (1833); N-W
                                       Provinces, Central India, Punjab, parts of UP (Gangetic Valley);
                                       village/village-group ("Mahal") as revenue unit, collected by headman, not
                                       zamindar; W. UP settlement with village communities ("Bhaichare")

   HOOK Region mnemonic: Permanent→Bengal/Bihar/Orissa ("PerBeBO") · Ryotwari→Madras/Bombay
  ("RyotMadBom") · Mahalwari→Punjab/UP/Central India ("MahalPU")
Colonial impact (Lucent): introduced market economy, ended customary rights; cash-revenue encouraged money-
lending; sharpened social differentiation (rich had court access); forced commercial-crop cultivation; broke up village-
society stability.

Full suppression: Gwalior, 20 June 1858. Nature-of-revolt views: "Sepoy Mutiny" (Syed Ahmed Khan,
R.C. Majumdar, John Seeley) vs. "War of Independence" (V.D. Savarkar, Karl Marx, K.M. Panikkar). Failure
causes: disunity/poor coordination; princely states (Scindia, Holkar, Nizam) aided the British; limited to
North/Central/Western India.

5.3 Aftermath — Government of India Act 1858
  East India Company abolished; Crown Rule via Queen's Proclamation (Queen Victoria); Secretary of
  State for India created.
  Governor-General also titled Viceroy — 1st Viceroy: Lord Canning.
  Doctrine of Lapse withdrawn; princely states assured against annexation; army restructured (fewer
  Indian soldiers) on the Peel Commission's recommendation; "divide and rule" pursued thereafter.
  HOOK   Historiography quotes: Savarkar — "the First War of Independence." R.C. Majumdar —
 neither "First," nor "National," nor "a War of Independence."


 6 PYQ-book-only additions (not in either source book)
  ★ Khongjom War (1891) — Anglo-Manipuri War against British annexation of Manipur; led by Yuvraj
  Tikendrajit Singh, who commanded the Manipuri army; publicly hanged 13 Aug 1891 on charges of
  murder/rebellion.
  ★ Peer Ali Khan — a Patna bookseller who led rebellion in Patna during 1857, attacked Gulzarbagh (3
  July 1857), killed opium agent Dr. Lyell; publicly hanged without trial, 7 July 1857, by Commissioner
  William Tayler.
  ★ Bhima Nayak, "Robin Hood of Nimar" — led tribal rebellion & guerrilla resistance in the Satpura
  region of Madhya Pradesh during 1857, per the PYQ book. DOUBT The raw extraction's own "Bhil
  Revolt" entry instead places a "Bhima Naik" in Khandesh, Maharashtra (1818) — possibly the same
  person under two different regional traditions/spellings, or two distinct Bhil leaders; not reconciled here.
  After the Indigo Revolt (1859–60), planters shifted their operations to Bihar as Bengal indigo
  production declined — directly connects to Champaran (1917).
  British forced Assam peasants to cultivate tea (also indigo in Bengal, wheat in Punjab) to meet
  European demand.
