2Formation of Congress

Founder: Allan Octavian Hume (also "Father of Indian Ornithology"). 1885: first meeting called for Poona, venue shifted to Bombay (Parmar: due to plague; Lucent: due to cholera outbreak in Pune — DOUBT). First session: Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, presided W.C. Bannerji, 72 delegates (only 2 Muslims, no women). Lucent adds a prehistory step: Hume first formed the "Indian National Union" (1884), renamed Congress after the Pune/Bombay conference.

★ Immediate cause: Ilbert Bill (1883). Would let senior Indian magistrates try cases involving European/British subjects; provoked fierce British backlash (contemporary "White Mutiny"), diluted/withdrawn — exposed the "real face" of British attitudes to Indians and catalysed INC's formation. Introduced under Viceroy Lord Ripon.

HOOK3 theories of Congress's origin: Safety Valve (Lala Lajpat Rai, ed. Young India) — INC as a controlled outlet for discontent · Conspiracy Theory (R.P. Dutt) · Lightning Conductor Theory (Gopal Krishna Gokhale). Viceroy Dufferin (contemporary): called Congress a "Factory of Sedition."
DOUBTTwo different Dufferin quotations exist across the two books: Parmar's "Factory of Sedition" (Part A, ch.4) vs Lucent's separate "INC represents only a microscopic minority" (Part B, Select Opinions). Not the same remark; both given, neither verified against an outside source.

Consequence: Indian Councils Act (1892) — allowed some Indians to be indirectly elected while keeping the official majority intact.

HOOKModerate leaders (Lucent's list, selected): Dadabhai Naoroji, A.O. Hume, Badruddin Tyabji, M.G. Ranade, W.C. Bannerji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjee, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Madan Mohan Malviya, Tej Bahadur Sapru, R.C. Dutt.
