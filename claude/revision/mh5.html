1Bengal Partition (1905)

Viceroy Lord Curzon proposed the partition (1903); announced July 1905; effective 16 Oct 1905. Split: East Bengal (Muslim-majority; today Assam+Bangladesh) / West Bengal (Hindu-majority; today WB+Odisha+Bihar). Official reason: administrative convenience for a large, populous province. Real aim (both books agree): weaken Bengal's nationalist influence via divide-and-rule, creating a Hindu-Muslim communal gulf. Reaction: observed as a "Day of Mourning."

Curzon's reactionary policies bundled with the Partition: Calcutta Corporation Act (elected-Indian-seat cuts), Official Secrets Act 1904 (curbed press freedom), Indian Universities Act (tightened govt. control of universities).

2Swadeshi Movement & the 1905–07 Congress Sessions

  • ★1905 Benaras (Gokhale): approved Swadeshi/Anti-Partition; formal Swadeshi proclamation, 7 Aug 1905, Calcutta Town Hall.
  • 1906 Calcutta (Naoroji): 4 resolutions — Swaraj (goal), Boycott, Swadeshi, National Education.
  • ★1907 Surat (Rash Behari Ghosh): Congress splits Moderates/Extremists over how far to extend the anti-Partition agitation. British "Carrot and Stick": appeased Moderates, suppressed Extremists (Tilak sent to Mandalay Jail 6 yrs; Aurobindo retired to Pondicherry; Lajpat Rai left for Britain).

Newspapers of the anti-Partition period: Hitabadi (Dwijendranath Tagore), Sanjibani (Krishna Kumar Mitra), Bengalee (Surendranath Banerjee), Bande Matram journal (Bipin Chandra Pal). Regional leaders: Tilak (Poona/Bombay), Lajpat Rai (Punjab), Chidambaram Pillai (Madras).

3Morley-Minto Reforms & Annulment of Partition

  • Indian Councils Act 1909 / Morley-Minto Reforms — Morley (Secretary of State), Minto (Viceroy, "Father of Communal Electorate"); introduced separate Muslim electorate; 1st Indian on the Viceroy's Executive Council: Satyendra Prasad Sinha.
  • 3rd Delhi Durbar (1911, Lord Hardinge II) — for George V's coronation; announced reunification of Bengal; capital shifted Calcutta→Delhi, 1911.

4.1 Key events

  • 1897: Chapekar Brothers assassinated W.C. Rand (Plague Commissioner, Poona).
  • 1908 Alipore/Muzaffarpur Bomb Case: Khudiram Bose & Prafulla Chaki attempted to kill Judge Kingsford; killed Mrs Kennedy & daughter by mistake; Chaki shot himself, Bose hanged; Kanailal Dutta killed approver-witness Narendranath Goswami.
  • ★1907, Stuttgart: Madam Bhikaji Cama hoisted the first Indian flag on foreign soil (green/yellow/red, "Vande Mataram" inscribed) at the International Socialist Congress; smuggled into India by Indulal Yagnik; also published journal Vande Mataram, "Mother of the Indian Revolution."
  • 1909: Madan Lal Dhingra killed Curzon Wyllie (London); Anant Kanhere (Nasik, Lucent) killed District Magistrate Jackson.
  • 1914: Komagata Maru Incident — Japanese ship, 376 Indian (mostly Sikh) passengers denied entry to Canada.
  • 1915: Defence of India Act — suppressed Ghadarites in Punjab during WWI; later replaced by the Rowlatt Act (cross-ref Ch.6).
HOOK★Bagha Jatin (Jatindranath Mukherjee) — killed a tiger single-handedly; Yugantar party leader; led armed rebellion incl. the Indo-German Conspiracy; martyred in an encounter with British officers, 1915.
HOOK★Rash Behari Bose — founded Indian Independence League, Tokyo, 1942; key Ghadar-Movement figure; helped form the Azad Hind Fauj (INA), later handed command to Subhas Chandra Bose; awarded Japan's "Order of the Rising Sun" (2nd-highest civilian honour) — NOT independent India's first Army C-in-C (that was Field Marshal K.M. Cariappa).

World War I context (1914 onward): Moderates supported the war as duty; Extremists supported it hoping for post-war self-governance; Revolutionaries saw it as an opportunity for armed struggle. Allies: Britain, France, Russia, Italy, Japan, USA, Belgium. Central Powers: Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria.

5Home Rule League (1916) & Lucknow Pact

Bal Gangadhar Tilak's HRLAnnie Besant's HRL
HQ Poona; founded first (28 Apr 1916); Centre: Maharashtra(excl. Bombay City)/Karnataka/Central Provinces/BerarHQ Madras; more branches; started Sept 1916 (inspired by the Irish Home Rule movement); Centre: Madras & Bombay City

After Tilak's death (1920), his HRL renamed Swarajya Sabha by Gandhi.

Lucknow Session, 1916 (Ambika Charan Mazumdar): reunion of Moderates & Extremists; Lucknow Pact — INC+Muslim League joint platform (via Tilak's & Jinnah's efforts); Congress accepted separate electorate; joint demand for "dominion status." INC's own demands: self-government, abolish India Council, separate judiciary/executive, Secretary of State's salary off the Indian treasury, half the Executive Council Indian, 1/3rd Muslim representation.

HOOKWritings: New India & Common Weal (Annie Besant) · Al Hilal (Urdu weekly, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, also wrote India Wins Freedom) · Comrade (Maulana Mohammad Ali).

Montagu/August Declaration, 1917 (Lucent) — British policy response to Hindu-Muslim unity at Lucknow: gradual transfer of government control to Indians, gradual development of local self-government.
