1Gandhi — Life to 1917

Full name Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi; b. 2 Oct 1869, Porbandar (parents Karamchand & Putlibai; wife Kasturba). To England for Law, 1888; returned Barrister, 1891.

South Africa (1893–1914): went 1893 for Dada Abdulla's case; thrown off a train at Pietermaritzburg (racial discrimination); Natal Indian Congress (1894, Durban); Transvaal British Indian Assoc. (1903); Indian Opinion newspaper (1903); Phoenix Settlement (1904, near Durban, inspired by Ruskin's Unto This Last); 1906 Johannesburg — 1st Satyagraha vs the Asiatic Registration/"Black" Act; Tolstoy Farm (1910, inspired by Tolstoy); Ambulance Corps in the Boer War → Kaisar-i-Hind Medal (1915).

HOOKReturned to India 9 Jan 1915 — now Pravasi Bharatiya Diwas. Ashrams: Kochrab (1915) → Sabarmati (1917). Political guru: Gopal Krishna Gokhale. First public appearance: BHU inauguration, 1916 (BHU founded by Madan Mohan Malaviya, who also founded Hindu Mahasabha, 1915). Wrote Hind Swaraj; autobiography The Story of My Experiments with Truth.

2Champaran, Ahmedabad & Kheda (1917–18)

MovementDetail
★Champaran Satyagraha (1917)Gandhi's first Civil Disobedience Movement in India; Raj Kumar Shukla invited him to Champaran, Bihar, vs the Tinkathia System (forced indigo on 3/20th of land) + Abwab tax; with Rajendra Prasad, Mazhar-ul-Haq, J.B. Kripalani; Champaran Agrarian Act (1918) abolished Tinkathia & suspended Abwab
Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)Gandhi's first hunger strike; Anusuya Sarabhai invited him; mill owners cut the Plague Bonus, workers demanded 50% wage hike, owners offered 20% → settled at 35%
Kheda Satyagraha (1918)Gandhi's first non-cooperation; Gujarat peasants unable to pay land tax after crop failure; result: tax waived, confiscated property returned, revenue rate reduced

3Government of India Act 1919 & Rowlatt Satyagraha

★ GoI Act 1919 / Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms — Montagu (Secretary of State) + Chelmsford (Viceroy). Introduced dyarchy in the provinces:

ReservedTransferred
Governor + Executive Council; land revenue, police, financeMinisters from Legislative Council; agriculture, health, education

Also: separate electorate extended to Anglo-Indians & Christians; bicameral legislature at the Centre.

TRAPDon't confuse the 1892 Indian Councils Act (budget-discussion right, no vote) with the 1909 Morley-Minto Reforms (separate Muslim electorate) and the 1919 Montagu-Chelmsford Act (dyarchy) — three separate, sequentially-tested constitutional Acts.

★ Rowlatt Act (1919), a.k.a. "Black Act"/Anarchical & Revolutionary Crimes Act — suggested by Justice Sydney Rowlatt; extended the wartime Defence of India Act (1915) after WWI had ended; let govt. detain people without trial, up to 2 years, suspending Habeas Corpus. Gandhi's Satyagraha call: 6 April 1919; arrested 8 April 1919.

4Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)

At Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, on Baisakhi, 13 April 1919: crowd gathered to celebrate Baisakhi + protest the arrest of Saifuddin Kitchlew & Dr. Satyapal. Brig. Gen. Reginald Dyer closed the only exit and opened fire without warning. Official toll: 379 dead; actual estimates >1000 (Parmar)/>1200 (Lucent).

Aftermath: Tagore returned his Knighthood; Gandhi returned his Kaisar-i-Hind title. Hunter Commission (1919) investigated Dyer — imposed no penal action; British Parliament passed the Indemnity Act protecting him; British elites raised funds for Dyer via the "Morning Post" (Rudyard Kipling contributed).

HOOKUdham Singh shot Michael O'Dwyer (Lt. Governor of Punjab during the massacre, backed Dyer) at a London meeting, 13 March 1940 (Lucent)/1940 (Parmar, no exact date), using the alias "Ram Mohammed Singh Azad"; hanged in Britain, 1940.

5Khilafat Movement & Non-Cooperation Movement (1919–22)

Khilafat Movement (1919): post-WWI removal of Turkey's Khalifa angered Muslims worldwide. All India Khilafat Committee, Bombay, March 1919, under the Ali Brothers (Mohammad & Shaukat Ali) + Maulana Abul Kalam Azad + Hasrat Mohani + Ajmal Khan. All India Khilafat Conference, Delhi, Nov 1919 — Gandhi elected its 1st president (opposed by Congress & Tilak, per Parmar).

Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22): approved at Congress's special Calcutta session, Sept 1920; endorsed at Nagpur; Gandhi merged it with Khilafat for Hindu-Muslim unity; 15-member Congress Working Committee formed; Jinnah, Annie Besant, B.C. Pal left Congress over this. Tilak Swaraj Fund (1921), Rs 1 crore target, after Tilak's death (1 Aug 1920).

  • Boycott: titles, govt. darbars, courts (lawyers Motilal Nehru/C.R. Das/Patel/Aruna Asaf Ali gave up practice), foreign cloth/goods; national schools (Jamia Millia, Kashi/Bihar Vidyapith).
  • Eka Movement (1921, UP, Madari Pasi); Moplah/Malabar Revolt (1921, Kerala, Muslim peasants vs Hindu landlords); Awadh Kisan Sabha (1920, Baba Ramchandra).
TRAP★Chauri Chaura, 4 Feb 1922 (Gorakhpur, UP) — mob burnt a police station, 22 policemen died → Gandhi, shocked by the violence, called off Non-Cooperation, jailed March 1922. Do not confuse with the later Bardoli resolution, where the Congress Working Committee formally suspended the movement and pivoted to constructive work.

6Political Vacuum, Swaraj Party & Belgaum Session

GroupLeadersPosition
No-ChangersRajendra Prasad, Vallabhbhai Patel, M.A. AnsariContinue Gandhi's principles; boycott councils; constructive work (ashrams, schools, Khadi)
Pro-ChangersC.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Ajmal KhanEnter legislative councils to oppose British rule from within

Swaraj Party (1923) — formed at the Gaya session (Dec 1922) as the "Congress Khilafat Swaraj Party"; C.R. Das (President, a.k.a. "Deshbandhu," d.1925), Motilal Nehru (Secretary); goal "to end or mend the Council"; defeated the "Public Safety Bill" (1928).

Belgaum Session, 1924 — presided by Gandhi (his only INC presidency); accepted the Swaraj Party within Congress.

HOOKGandhi's other names: Mahatma (Tagore, 1917) · Bapu · Rashtrapita (Subhas Bose, 1944) · "Half-naked fakir"/"Indian Fakir" (Churchill, 1931) · "Malang Baba/Nanga Fakir" (NW Frontier Kabailis, 1930). Editor: Indian Opinion(1903–15) · Harijan(1919–31, per Parmar's 1919-founding date for the Gujarati Navjivan) · Young India(1933–42, Gujarati name Navjeevan). 1st biography: Joseph Doke (1909); 1st full biography: Louis Fischer (1950).
