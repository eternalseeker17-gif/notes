2Kakori, Lajpat Rai's Death & the Lahore Conspiracy Case

  • Kakori Train Robbery (1925), near Lucknow — hanged: Ashfaqullah Khan, Roshan Singh, Ram Prasad Bismil, Rajendra Lahiri (renamed "Kakori Train Action" by UP govt., 2021).
  • 1928: Lala Lajpat Rai, protesting Simon Commission ("Simon Go Back"), fatally struck by police officer Scott during a lathi charge. To avenge his death, ★Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev & Rajguru killed Saunders (mistaken for Scott), Lahore.
  • 8 April 1929: Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt bombed the Central Legislative Assembly (protesting the Public Safety Bill 1928 & Trade Disputes Bill 1929); surrendered deliberately — "not to kill but to make deaf hear."
  • Hunger Strikes (1929–30): demanded political-prisoner status; Jatindra Nath Das died after a 63-day fast, 13 Sep 1929.
  • Lahore Conspiracy Case: special 1930 ordinance let the trial proceed in the accused's absence; approvers Hans Raj Vohra, Jai Gopal, Phanindranath Ghosh, Sobha Singh. ★ Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev & Rajguru hanged 23 March 1931 (a day early) — now Shaheed Diwas. Slogan "Inquilab Zindabad" (coined by Hasrat Mohani, 1921; popularised by Bhagat Singh). Bhagat Singh wrote Why I Am an Atheist; Durgawati Devi ("Durga Bhabhi") was a prominent HSRA member.
  • 1930, Chittagong Armoury Raid — Surya Sen ("Master Da"); hanged Jan 1934; women participants Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Dutta, Suniti Choudhury, Bina Das, Shanti Ghosh.
  • Chandrashekhar Azad shot himself, Alfred Park, Allahabad, 27 Feb 1931 (now Chandrashekhar Azad Park).
HOOK★ 8 Dec 1930, Writers' Building, Calcutta — Benoy Basu, Badal Gupta & Dinesh Gupta (in European attire) killed IG Prisons N.S. Simpson; Dalhousie Square renamed B.B.D. Bagh in their honour — PYQ-book-only, see A.2.

3Simon Commission (1927–28)

Appointed by Secretary of State Lord Birkenhead, 8 Nov 1927 (statutory review of the 1919 Act, due at 10 yrs but called at 8); chairman Sir John Simon; all 7 members British (incl. future PM Clement Attlee) — the "White Commission." Arrived India/reached Bombay, 3 Feb 1928. ★Boycotted for including no Indian members; greeted with black flags and "Simon Go Back" (term also linked to Yusuf Meherally, per Parmar — DOUBT, cross-ref Ch.8 for "Quit India").

HOOKSimon Commission recommendations: abolish dyarchy · federal system · extended voting rights · separate Muslim electorate · rejected Sindh-Bombay separation · rejected 1/3rd Muslim central-assembly seats · ignored the frontier's equal-status demand.

Response: Madras Session 1928 (special, M.A. Ansari) resolved to boycott it; Birkenhead challenged Indians to draft their own constitution → Nehru Report (committee under Motilal Nehru; architects Nehru + Tej Bahadur Sapru): rejected separate electorate, demanded Dominion Status, linguistic provinces, 19 Fundamental Rights, responsible government; accepted at Calcutta Session 1928. Muslim League's Delhi Proposals (1927) preceded Jinnah's 14 Points (9 March 1929), rejecting the Nehru Report. Supporters of Simon Commission (initially): B.R. Ambedkar & Hindu Mahasabha (founded 1915, Malaviya) — Mahasabha later also boycotted it. Other contemporary bodies: Muddiman Committee (1924, dyarchy review), Linlithgow/Royal Agriculture Commission (1926), Lee Commission (1924, Indianisation of Civil Services).

HOOKBardoli Satyagraha (1928) — Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel; protest vs a 22% tax hike; "Sardar" title given by the women of Bardoli.

4Lahore Session 1929 & the Road to Civil Disobedience

  • Irwin's Declaration/Deepavali Declaration (1929): promised future Dominion Status + a Round Table Conference.
  • Lahore Session, Dec 1929 (Jawaharlal Nehru): Purna Swaraj adopted as the goal; tricolour hoisted on the Ravi's bank, 31 Dec 1929 ("Inquilab Zindabad"); 26 Jan 1930 fixed as the first Independence Day; Congress authorised CDM; decided to boycott the 1st RTC.
  • Gandhi's 11 demands (ultimatum, ignored by Irwin): arms licences, 50% cut in military spending, release political prisoners, favourable rupee-pound rate, abolish the Salt Law, 50% cut in civil-service salaries, reserve coastal shipping for Indians, import duty on foreign cloth, reform/abolish the CID, reduce land revenue, total prohibition.
HOOKDominion Status was first demanded by Tej Bahadur Sapru & M.R. Jayakar.

5Dandi March, Dharasana & the Civil Disobedience Movement

★ Dandi March/Salt Satyagraha (12 March–6 April 1930): Gandhi + 78 followers marched ~240/241 miles, Sabarmati Ashram→Dandi (Navsari), breaking the Salt Law on arrival. Time magazine initially mocked Gandhi, later named him "Man of the Year" (1930).

Dharasana Satyagraha (1930): Gandhi arrested 4/5 May 1930 before the planned raid; led instead by Abbas Tyabji, then Sarojini Naidu, Imam Sahib & Manilal (Gandhi's son).

HOOKWomen's role: Sarojini Naidu persuaded Gandhi to include women; Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay: "Do not restrict this movement to men alone."

Campaign by region: Ryotwari areas (non-payment of revenue) · Zamindari areas (no chowkidar tax) · Central Provinces (forest-law violation) · Assam (students vs the Cunningham Circular, a "good behaviour" pledge). Regional leaders: C. Rajagopalachari (TN, Trichinopoly→Vedaranniyam salt march) · K. Kelappan (Malabar) · Gopalbandhu Choudhary (Orissa) · Ambika Kant Sinha (Bihar, Nakhas Pond) · Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ("Frontier Gandhi"/"Badshah Khan," Peshawar — Red Shirt Movement, Khudai Khidmatgar) · Rani Gaidinliu (Manipur/Naga Hills, Heraka Movement, joined aged 13, imprisoned 14 yrs — cross-ref Ch.2 §6/Ch.3 §6). Mobilisation: Prabhat Pheri, Vanar Sena, Manjari Sena.

6Gandhi-Irwin Pact, Karachi Session & the Round Table Conferences

RTCDatesNote
1stNov 1930–Jan 1931Boycotted by INC
2ndSep–Dec 1931Only RTC Gandhi/Congress attended; failed (Gandhi vs PM Ramsay MacDonald on communal representation)
3rdNov–Dec 1932Fruitless — national leaders in prison. B.R. Ambedkar attended all 3.

Gandhi-Irwin Pact/Delhi Pact, 5 March 1931 (Sapru & Jayakar brokered): Congress suspends CDM, joins 2nd RTC. Accepted: release of non-violent political prisoners, return of unsold confiscated property, salt-production rights for personal use. Not accepted: public inquiry into police excesses, commutation of Bhagat Singh/Sukhdev/Rajguru's death sentences. Nehru's remark: "This is the way the world ends, not with a bang, but a whimper."

Karachi Session, 29 March 1931 (Sardar Patel) — Gandhi greeted with black flags over Bhagat Singh's execution; ratified the Gandhi-Irwin Pact; CDM suspended; Purna Swaraj reiterated (with its meaning formally stated for the first time); adopted Fundamental Rights & National Economic Policy resolutions.

2nd RTC's failure → Congress resumed CDM (29 Dec 1931); Viceroy Willingdon refused to meet Gandhi; Gandhi arrested 4 Jan 1932; CDM finally withdrawn April 1934.

A.3 Cross-book & cross-source doubts carried forward

  • Lajpat Rai's death / Saunders murder: Parmar names police officer "Scott" as who struck Lajpat Rai and says Saunders was killed "instead of Scott" (mistaken identity); Lucent names no "Scott," saying only that Bhagat Singh's group killed "a white police official, Saunders."
  • Simon Commission's appointment vs arrival date: Parmar gives 1928 as the arrival year; Lucent/PYQ book both confirm appointment Nov 1927, arrival/Bombay landing Feb 1928 — not a real conflict, just different reference points.
  • Dandi March distance: Parmar 241 miles vs Lucent 240 miles — not reconciled.
  • Gandhi's arrest date (Dandi era): Parmar 4 May 1930 vs Lucent 5 May 1930.
  • Gandhi's 1932 arrest date: Parmar's "4 Jan 1932" arrest vs Lucent's chronology listing "Jan 3, 1932" as the second CDM phase's start — likely the same event described from two angles, not reconciled.
  • "Simon Go Back" coinage: Parmar attributes the term to Yusuf Meherally, who is credited elsewhere (Lucent, cross-ref Ch.8) only with coining "Quit India" — a probable over-attribution, left as printed.
