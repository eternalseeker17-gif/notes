 1 Communal Award, Poona Pact & Untouchability
Communal Award/MacDonald Award (16 Aug 1932) — PM Ramsay MacDonald; separate electorate
for "Depressed Classes" + Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Europeans (Parmar also adds Marathas). Gandhi
strongly opposed it (threat to Hindu unity), fasted unto death; Ambedkar supported it (safeguards
representation). Congress itself "neither accepted nor rejected" it.
Poona Pact, 24 Sep 1932, Yerwada Central Jail, Pune — Gandhi & Ambedkar (Malaviya signed on
Gandhi's behalf): separate electorate dropped for a joint electorate; reserved seats raised from 71
(Award) to 148 in provincial legislatures, 18% at the Centre.
  HOOK Gandhi's anti-untouchability efforts: All India Anti-Untouchability League (1932) →
 renamed Harijan Sevak Sangh; weekly Harijan — cross-ref Ch.2 §4.


 2 Government of India Act 1935 & the 1937 Elections
  GoI Act 1935 — based on the Simon Commission report; royal assent 4 Aug 1935; introduced
  provincial autonomy, abolished dyarchy in provinces (kept it at the Centre, never implemented),
  ministers responsible to the legislature, federal structure. Condemned by Congress; Nehru: "a new
  charter of bondage."
  ★ Its major proposal: establishment of a federal government.
  1937 elections: Congress formed ministries in 7/11 provinces (later 8), except Punjab (Unionist Party),
  Bengal (Krishak Praja Party+Muslim League coalition), Sindh (United Party). Jinnah: "Congress was
  drunk with power and was oppressive against Muslims" (Lucent).
  Congress ministries resigned, Dec 1939 — protesting India's WWII involvement without
  consultation; demanded a Constituent Assembly + full responsible govt.; Viceroy Linlithgow's reply (17
  Oct 1939) rejected this. Muslim League observed 22 Dec 1939 as "Day of Deliverance."

  HOOK Congress sessions, 1934–39: 1934 Congress Socialist Party (JP Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia,
 Minoo Masani) · 1936 Lucknow All India Kisan Sabha (Swami Sahajanand Saraswati) · 1937 Faizpur
 (1st village session; Nehru) · 1938 Haripura (Bose elected President; National Planning Committee
 formed, 1st chairman Nehru) · 1939 Tripuri (Jabalpur; Bose re-elected, defeating Gandhi's candidate
 Pattabhi Sitaramayya; Bose resigned, formed All India Forward Bloc (1939, Unnao)).

 5 Pakistan Movement & Mediation Attempts
  "Pakistan" coined by Rahmat Ali (1933, Parmar / 1935, Lucent — DOUBT), building on Iqbal's 1930
  idea.
  ★ Lahore/Pakistan Resolution, 22–24 March 1940 — Muslim League's Lahore session; demanded
  a separate Muslim homeland in NW+E India, rejecting the GoI Act 1935 federal scheme. Pakistan Day
  observed 23 March 1943.
  C.R. Formula (1944) — Rajagopalachari's plebiscite proposal for Muslim-majority NW/East districts;
  Jinnah objected (wanted only League-approved Muslims voting); Savarkar condemned it.
  Desai-Liaqat Pact (1945) — unsuccessful, Bhulabhai Desai (Congress) & Liaqat Ali Khan (League).
  Wavell Plan & Shimla Conference (14 June–14 July 1945) — Viceroy Wavell's balanced-
  representation Executive Council proposal; failed on Jinnah's insistence that only League-picked
  Muslims join.

 7 Cabinet Mission, Direct Action Day & Mountbatten Plan
  Cabinet Mission (March 1946) — PM Attlee's delegation: Lord Pethick-Lawrence (Chair), Stafford
  Cripps, A.V. Alexander. Rejected a separate Pakistan; proposed 3-tier structure — Federal Union /
  provincial Groups / provinces. Group A (Hindu-majority: Madras/Bombay/UP/Bihar/Odisha/CP), Group B
  (Muslim-majority: Punjab/NWFP/Sindh/Baluchistan), Group C (Bengal+Assam). Constituent Assembly +
  interim government proposed. Attlee's Declaration, 20 Feb 1947: power transferred by 30 June
  1948.
  Response: Congress accepted partially (opposed compulsory grouping); League initially accepted,
  then Jinnah called ★ Direct Action Day, 16 Aug 1946 — triggered the "Great Calcutta Killing."
  Noakhali Riots (Oct–Nov 1946) followed; Gandhi visited to restore peace.
  Mountbatten Plan, 3 June 1947 ("Partition Plan") — basis for the Indian Independence Act; princely
  states could join India, Pakistan, or (initially) stay independent. Of 565 states, 562 joined; 3 held out —
  J&K, Hyderabad, Junagadh. Mountbatten: last Viceroy + 1st Governor-General of independent India
  (1947–48); C. Rajagopalachari: only Indian Governor-General (post later abolished).

 8 After Independence
  Operation Polo (Sep 1948) — Hyderabad Police Action, Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri; Nizam Mir Osman
  Ali Khan surrendered 17 Sep 1948.
  Maharaja Hari Singh signed the Instrument of Accession, 26 Oct 1947 (J&K).
  Gandhi's assassination: 30 Jan 1948, Birla House, Delhi — shot by Nathuram Godse (3 bullets)
  during evening prayer; reason: blamed Gandhi's non-violence/Muslim-appeasement for Partition. Godse
  & accomplice Narayan Apte hanged, Ambala Jail, 15 Nov 1949. V.D. Savarkar (Hindu Mahasabha) was
  Godse's acknowledged political guru, though not formally an RSS leader.

A.2 New facts sourced only from the PYQ book (not in Parmar or Lucent)
  Kanaklata Barua ("Birbala," Assam) — shot dead hoisting the national flag during Quit India; entirely
  absent from the raw extraction, which names only Kushal Konwar and Kanaklata Barua (the latter
  appearing only in Ch.9's PYQ, not the books) as Quit-India martyrs.
  Matangini Hazra (Tamluk, Bengal) — led a procession 29 Sep 1942, shot, martyred chanting "Vande
  Mataram"; not named in either source book (the raw extraction names only the Tamluk parallel-
  government leaders, not Hazra herself).
  Baji Raut (Dhenkanal, Odisha, b. 5 Oct 1926) — refused to ferry British police across the Brahmani
  river during the 1938 Prajamandal (princely-state peoples') movement; martyred aged 12, "youngest
  martyr of India." Chronologically pre-dates Quit India and belongs more properly to the princely-
  states/Miscellaneous bucket, but grouped here since the PYQ book files it alongside the other Quit-
  India-era youth martyrs.

A.3 Cross-book & cross-source doubts carried forward
  Muslim League's response to the August Offer: Parmar says the League also rejected it; Lucent
  says the League welcomed it. A direct, unreconciled contradiction.
  "Pakistan" coinage year: 1933 (Parmar) vs 1935 (Lucent).
  Gandhi's location on 15 Aug 1947: Parmar says Noakhali; Lucent's chronology says Calcutta.
  Congress ministries' resignation month: Parmar says "October 1939"; Lucent and the Muslim
  League's own "Day of Deliverance" both point to 22 December 1939 as the actual resignation date.
  INA trial defence lawyers: Parmar lists Kailash Nath Katju; Lucent lists Muhammad Ali Jinnah instead,
  among otherwise-overlapping names.
  "Rani Lakshmibai Regiment" (Parmar) vs "Rani of Jhansi Brigade" (Lucent) — same unit,
  different naming; used interchangeably in §6.

A.4 Completeness check
