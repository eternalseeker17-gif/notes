Last-minute 12

  1. ★ Warren Hastings (1773/74–85) — 1st Governor-General of Bengal via the Regulating Act 1773; abolished Dual Govt., set up Diwani/Faujdari courts; Pitt's India Act 1784; Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784, with William Jones); 1st Anglo-Maratha War, 2nd Anglo-Mysore War; Ring Fence Policy
  2. Cornwallis (1786–93) — ★Permanent Settlement (1793, to fix regular revenue via a loyal zamindar class); "Father of Indian Civil Services"; codified laws; 3rd Anglo-Mysore War (Treaty of Seringapatam)
  3. ★ Wellesley (1798–1805) — Subsidiary Alliance (Nizam of Hyderabad first, 1798) — ally states disbanded own armies, hosted a British Resident, lost independent foreign policy & couldn't employ non-British Europeans without consent; 4th Anglo-Mysore War, 2nd Anglo-Maratha War, Treaty of Bassein
  4. ★ Lord Hastings (1813–23) — introduced the Policy of Paramountcy (British authority supreme over Indian states); 3rd Anglo-Maratha War, Anglo-Nepal War (Sagauli), Ryotwari (Munro)
  5. Bentinck (1828–35) — "1st Governor-General of India" (Parmar)/split Bengal+India tenure (Lucent); Sati abolished 1829; Macaulay's Minute 1835; "Father of (Western) Education in India"
  6. ★ Dalhousie (1848–56) — Doctrine of Lapse (Satara 1848→Nagpur 1854); 1st railway (1853, Bombay-Thane); Wood's Despatch 1854; Widow Remarriage Act/Bill 1855–56; 2nd Anglo-Sikh War, annexed Punjab
  7. Canning (1856–62) — last GG (1856–58)/1st Viceroy (1858–62); 1857 Revolt; GoI Act 1858; Universities of Calcutta/Bombay/Madras founded 1857 (resolves the "Development of Education" gap, see §3)
  8. Key Viceroys: Mayo(only Viceroy assassinated, in office, Andaman 1872) · Lytton(Vernacular Press Act 1878, Arms Act, age-limit cut 21→19) · Ripon(Ilbert Bill 1883, Hunter Commission 1882, "Father of Local Self-Govt.") · Dufferin(INC formed 1885) · Curzon(Bengal Partition 1905) · Minto II(Muslim League 1906) · Hardinge II(capital to Delhi, 1911–12) · Chelmsford(GoI Act 1919, Rowlatt, Jallianwala) · Irwin(Simon Commission, Dandi March, Gandhi-Irwin Pact) · Willingdon(GoI Act 1935) · Linlithgow(Quit India) · Wavell(Cabinet Mission) · Mountbatten(Partition/Independence)
  9. ★ Governors-General/Viceroys ↔ Policy match: Hastings(Warren)→Ring Fence · Dalhousie→Doctrine of Lapse · Wellesley→Subsidiary Alliance · Hastings(Marquess/Lord)→Paramountcy
  10. Robert Clive: Governor of Bengal 1757–60 and again 1764–67 (managing post-Buxar anarchy); committed suicide in 1774
  11. Development of Education & Press (scattered facts, assembled here): Macaulay's Minute(1835, Downward Filtration)→Wood's Despatch(1854, "Magna Carta")→Hunter Commission(1882, Ripon)→Raleigh/Universities Commission(1902, Curzon)→Sadler/Calcutta University Commission(1917, Chelmsford)→Sargeant Plan(1944, Wavell); ★ Calcutta/Bombay/Madras Universities founded 1857 (on the model of London University) — PYQ-only addition, see A.2

A.2 New facts sourced only from the PYQ book (not in Parmar or Lucent's Education-file coverage)

The 1857 founding of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras Universities "on the model of London University" is given only in the PYQ book's own explanation for the Wood's Despatch question (§3) — this specific framing (as a direct consequence of Wood's Despatch) is not in either source book's Education & Press-relevant pages. It is, however, independently confirmed as a bare fact in Lucent's own Viceroys section under Canning ("universities of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras founded in 1857") — so the underlying fact is real and cross-book-confirmed, just not connected explicitly to Wood's Despatch by either source book the way the PYQ explanation frames it.

A.3 Cross-book & cross-source doubts carried forward

  • Muslim League's 1906 founder — three-way inconsistency across Parmar's two chapters and Lucent, see §2 DOUBT box.
  • DAV School founder — Lala Lajpat Rai (PYQ) vs Mahatma Hansraj (Parmar raw extraction) — see §3 DOUBT box.
  • Bentinck's tenure/title: Parmar calls him "First Governor-General of India" for a single 1828–35 tenure; Lucent splits it into Governor-General of Bengal (1828–33) and of India (1833–35).
  • Widow Remarriage Act/Bill: Parmar puts the whole Act (1856) under Dalhousie; Lucent splits it — Bill (1855) under Dalhousie, Act (1856) under Canning.
  • Mountbatten's title: Parmar's own ch.8 calls him "last Viceroy...first Governor-General of independent India"; ch.9 calls him "Last British Governor-General"; Lucent calls him "first Governor-General of free India" — three overlapping but not identical framings, not reconciled.
  • Tenure-date mismatches (Linlithgow 1936–44/43; Wavell 1944/1943–47) — both given, not reconciled.
