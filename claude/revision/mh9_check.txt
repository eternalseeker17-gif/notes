A.2 New facts sourced only from the PYQ book (not in Parmar or Lucent's Education-file
coverage)
The 1857 founding of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras Universities "on the model of London University" is
given only in the PYQ book's own explanation for the Wood's Despatch question (§3) — this specific framing (as a
direct consequence of Wood's Despatch) is not in either source book's Education & Press-relevant pages. It is,
however, independently confirmed as a bare fact in Lucent's own Viceroys section under Canning ("universities of
Calcutta, Bombay and Madras founded in 1857") — so the underlying fact is real and cross-book-confirmed, just
not connected explicitly to Wood's Despatch by either source book the way the PYQ explanation frames it.

A.3 Cross-book & cross-source doubts carried forward
  Muslim League's 1906 founder — three-way inconsistency across Parmar's two chapters and Lucent, see §2
  DOUBT box.
  DAV School founder — Lala Lajpat Rai (PYQ) vs Mahatma Hansraj (Parmar raw extraction) — see §3 DOUBT
  box.
  Bentinck's tenure/title: Parmar calls him "First Governor-General of India" for a single 1828–35 tenure; Lucent
  splits it into Governor-General of Bengal (1828–33) and of India (1833–35).
  Widow Remarriage Act/Bill: Parmar puts the whole Act (1856) under Dalhousie; Lucent splits it — Bill (1855)
  under Dalhousie, Act (1856) under Canning.
  Mountbatten's title: Parmar's own ch.8 calls him "last Viceroy...first Governor-General of independent India";
  ch.9 calls him "Last British Governor-General"; Lucent calls him "first Governor-General of free India" — three
  overlapping but not identical framings, not reconciled.
  Tenure-date mismatches (Linlithgow 1936–44/43; Wavell 1944/1943–47) — both given, not reconciled.
