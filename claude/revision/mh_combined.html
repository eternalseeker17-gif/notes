4Bengal — Nawabs, Plassey & Buxar

Nawabs of Bengal: Murshid Quli Khan (1717–27, first Nawab; 1717 Golden Farman from Farrukhsiyar — free trade for Rs 3,000/yr + dastaks) → Shuja-ud-Din (1727–39) → Sarfaraz Khan (1739–40, murdered by Alivardi Khan) → Alivardi Khan (1740–56) → ★ Sirajuddaulah (1756–57, last self-governing Nawab).

Siraj seized Kasimbazar & Fort William (Black Hole Tragedy, 1756); Clive recaptured Calcutta & signed Treaty of Alinagar (Feb 1757) with him.

TRAPBattle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Clive vs Sirajuddaulah — betrayed by Mir Jafar (Mir Bakshi) + Manikchand + Amichand + Jagat Seth + Khadim Khan. Mir Jafar made puppet Nawab. Confirms formal beginning of British Empire in India — not the "first victory" in India generally (that's still Plassey per the PYQ's own framing, see A.1) but the decisive one over an Indian ruler.

Mir Jafar (1757–60, granted free trade + 24 Parganas zamindari) → forced to abdicate for son-in-law ★ Mir Qasim (1760–64, shifted capital Murshidabad→Munger, revolted over dastak misuse) → fled to Awadh, formed confederacy with Shuja-ud-Daula (Awadh) + Shah Alam II (Mughal Emperor).

DOUBTWho was Nawab when the Allahabad treaties were signed (1765)? Not reconciled — transcribed as printed in both.

★ Battle of Buxar (fought 1764, Hector Munro vs. the Mir Qasim/Shuja-ud-Daula/Shah Alam II combine) → Treaty of Allahabad (1765), negotiated by Clive: Allahabad & Kara handed to Shah Alam II; Nawab of Awadh paid Rs 50 lakh war indemnity; Diwani (revenue rights) of Bengal, Bihar & Orissa to the Company for Rs 26 lakh/yr, plus Rs 53 lakh from the Emperor for Nizamat (military/judicial) functions — this Diwani+Nizamat control is the Dual Government of Bengal (1765–72), ended by Warren Hastings in 1772.

5.1 Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798)

Indian rulers paid for a British garrison stationed on their territory and ceded control of external relations. Adopted by: Hyderabad (first, 1798) → Mysore (1799) → Tanjore (1799) → ★Awadh (1801, ceded half territory incl. Rohilkhand & the Doab) → Peshwa (1802, per Parmar).

DOUBTLucent's own annexation table instead dates Maratha-confederacy and Nawab of Carnatic/Arcot both to 1801 under Subsidiary Alliance, and does not list Carnatic in Parmar's adopter list at all; Parmar gives Peshwa 1802. Not reconciled.

6Punjab — Sikh Empire & Anglo-Sikh Wars

  • ★ Dal Khalsa — combined army of 18th-c. Sikh jathas/misls, established 1748 under Nawab Kapur Singh.
  • ★ Maharaja Ranjit Singh (Sukerchakia Misl, one of 12 misls) captured Lahore 1799 and made it his capital; formally assumed the title "Maharaja" April 1801, founding the Sikh Empire; Treaty of Amritsar (1809) fixed the Sutlej as the boundary with British territory. Died 1839; successors Kharak Singh→Naunihal Singh→Sher Singh→Duleep Singh amid instability.
  • 1st Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46): Sikhs defeated at Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal (Sir Harry Smith) & Sobraon → Treaty of Lahore + Treaty of Bhairowal (1846); Treaty of Amritsar (1846) ceded Kashmir to Gulab Singh for ₹75 lakh; Sir Henry Lawrence, first Resident.
  • 2nd Anglo-Sikh War (1848–49): Mulraj (Governor of Multan) killed 2 British officers → rebellion; Duleep Singh captured, Kohinoor taken; Dalhousie annexed Punjab (1849); Sir John Lawrence, first Chief Commissioner of Punjab.

7Afghans, Burma & Nepal — Buffer-State Policies

  • Ring Fence Policy — Warren Hastings: defensive ring of allied states around Company territory.
  • Forward Policy — Lord Auckland: pre-emptively intervene in Afghanistan against Russian advance → Tripartite Treaty (1838) (Britain + Shuja Shah Durrani + Ranjit Singh) to depose Dost Mohammad Khan.
  • ★ 1st Anglo-Afghan War (1839–42): initial success installing Shah Shuja, then catastrophic retreat from Kabul (1842); John Lawrence later adopted "Masterly Inactivity."
  • 2nd Anglo-Afghan War (1878–80): Treaty of Gandamak (Yakub Khan); Lytton's "Proud Reserve Policy"; Durand Line drawn 1893 (Sir Mortimer Durand & Amir Abdur Rahman Khan) — today's Afghanistan-Pakistan border.
  • 3rd Anglo-Afghan War (1919): Chelmsford's viceroyalty, Amanullah Khan; Treaty of Rawalpindi.
  • 1st Anglo-Burma War (1824–26): Treaty of Yandaboo. 2nd (1852–53): no further Parmar detail; cross-ref Ch.9 (Dalhousie annexed Lower Burma/Pegu). 3rd (1885): cross-ref Ch.9 (Dufferin).
  • Anglo-Nepal War (1814–16): Treaty of Sugauli (1816) fixed the British India–Nepal border, Nepal ceded territory.
  • Battle of Saragarhi (1897): 21 soldiers of the 36th Sikh Regiment vs thousands of tribesmen, North-West Frontier, part of the Tirah Campaign (1897–98).

8Sindh (PYQ-book-only addition)

★ Sindh was annexed in 1843 under Governor-General Lord Ellenborough (cross-ref Ch.9, Governors-General & Viceroys — neither source book covers Sindh's annexation anywhere in the Modern History chapters used for this file). The PYQ book's own explanations add the operational detail: the conquest is credited to Sir Charles Napier, who defeated the Amirs of Sindh at the Battle of Miani. Napier himself admitted the annexation was morally/legally unjustified, describing it as "a very advantageous, useful, and humane piece of rascality" — a line directly tested via an Assertion-Reason question. None of this (Napier, Miani, the "rascality" quote) is present in either source book; sourced here to the PYQ book's explanation text alone, per standing practice for PYQ-only facts.

2Economic Impact of British Rule

Three phases (Lucent): Mercantile (1757–1813, EIC political power monopolised trade) → Industrial (1813–58, Act of 1813 one-way trade, India flooded with cheap British imports, forced raw-material export/finished-goods import) → Financial (1860 onward, India as market+raw-material supplier; Railways/Post/Telegraph 1853, Avadh Commercial Bank 1881, Tata Iron & Steel 1907).

Constituents: company-servant extortion; "investment" (Bengal-revenue-funded exports); duty-free British trade; official remittances/salaries; "home charges" (pensions paid from Indian treasury); interest to British investors. Effects: stunted Indian enterprise, financed British capitalism, kept India as an uncompetitive free-trade zone, foreign-managed interlocking capitalist firms.

5The Revolt of 1857

5.1 Causes

  • Political: ★Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie, 1848) — Satara(1848), Sambalpur/Jaitpur(1849), Udaipur(1852), Jhansi(1853), Nagpur(1854); Awadh annexed 1856 (misgovernance) — "Nursery of the British Army," last Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, son Birjis Qadr proclaimed new Nawab; Nana Sahib's pension refused (adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II).
  • Economic: heavy taxation, forced evictions, discriminatory tariffs, destruction of traditional handicrafts.
  • Socio-religious: Sati abolition (1829), widow remarriage (1856) — hurt orthodox sentiment.
  • Military: discrimination against Indian soldiers; General Service Enlistment Act (1856, Canning) mandated overseas service (loss of caste).
  • Immediate cause: ★Enfield rifle cartridges greased with cow/pig fat.

1Communal Award, Poona Pact & Untouchability

Communal Award/MacDonald Award (16 Aug 1932) — PM Ramsay MacDonald; separate electorate for "Depressed Classes" + Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Europeans (Parmar also adds Marathas). Gandhi strongly opposed it (threat to Hindu unity), fasted unto death; Ambedkar supported it (safeguards representation). Congress itself "neither accepted nor rejected" it.

Poona Pact, 24 Sep 1932, Yerwada Central Jail, Pune — Gandhi & Ambedkar (Malaviya signed on Gandhi's behalf): separate electorate dropped for a joint electorate; reserved seats raised from 71 (Award) to 148 in provincial legislatures, 18% at the Centre.

HOOKGandhi's anti-untouchability efforts: All India Anti-Untouchability League (1932) → renamed Harijan Sevak Sangh; weekly Harijan — cross-ref Ch.2 §4.

2Government of India Act 1935 & the 1937 Elections

  • GoI Act 1935 — based on the Simon Commission report; royal assent 4 Aug 1935; introduced provincial autonomy, abolished dyarchy in provinces (kept it at the Centre, never implemented), ministers responsible to the legislature, federal structure. Condemned by Congress; Nehru: "a new charter of bondage."
  • ★ Its major proposal: establishment of a federal government.
  • 1937 elections: Congress formed ministries in 7/11 provinces (later 8), except Punjab (Unionist Party), Bengal (Krishak Praja Party+Muslim League coalition), Sindh (United Party). Jinnah: "Congress was drunk with power and was oppressive against Muslims" (Lucent).
  • Congress ministries resigned, Dec 1939 — protesting India's WWII involvement without consultation; demanded a Constituent Assembly + full responsible govt.; Viceroy Linlithgow's reply (17 Oct 1939) rejected this. Muslim League observed 22 Dec 1939 as "Day of Deliverance."
HOOKCongress sessions, 1934–39: 1934 Congress Socialist Party (JP Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Minoo Masani) · 1936 Lucknow All India Kisan Sabha (Swami Sahajanand Saraswati) · 1937 Faizpur (1st village session; Nehru) · 1938 Haripura (Bose elected President; National Planning Committee formed, 1st chairman Nehru) · 1939 Tripuri (Jabalpur; Bose re-elected, defeating Gandhi's candidate Pattabhi Sitaramayya; Bose resigned, formed All India Forward Bloc (1939, Unnao)).

3World War II, August Offer & the Cripps Mission

WWII (1939): Allied Powers (Britain/France/USA/USSR) vs Axis (Germany/Italy/Japan). INC's Oct 1939 demands to Viceroy Linlithgow: Constituent Assembly + responsible govt. at Centre & provinces.

August Offer (1940, Linlithgow, under Churchill's direction per Parmar): Dominion Status promised; Indian-majority Constituent Assembly; advisory war council; minority veto on future constitution. DOUBT Congress rejected it (Nehru: "dead as a doornail"); Parmar says the Muslim League also rejected it, while Lucent says the League welcomed it (hoping grouping led to Pakistan) — a direct cross-book conflict, not reconciled.

Individual Satyagraha (1940): Gandhi's response to the Offer's failure; Vinoba Bhave 1st Satyagrahi, Nehru 2nd, Brahma Dutt 3rd (Parmar)/Patel among later ones (Lucent). Revolutionary Socialist Party founded 1940.

HOOKCripps Mission (Mar–Apr 1942) — background: Japanese advance to Burma, Pearl Harbor pressure (Roosevelt), Operation Barbarossa. Proposals: Constituent Assembly, Dominion Status, province opt-out right, minority safeguards. Rejected by all; Gandhi: "a post-dated cheque drawn on a failing/crumbling bank."

4Quit India Movement (1942)

CWC met at Wardha (July 1942), adopted the Quit India Resolution, ratified at Gowalia Tank, Bombay. Launched by Gandhi 8 Aug 1942 ("August Kranti"), after Cripps Mission's failure. "Do or Die" slogan at Gowalia Tank Maidan. All leaders arrested Day 1 (a "leaderless movement"); ★ Aruna Asaf Ali presided over the CWC and hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank ("Grand Old Lady" of the freedom struggle).

Gandhi's appeals: govt. servants — don't resign, work subtly against British policy; soldiers — don't resign, but refuse to fire on compatriots; peasants — withhold rent from pro-British landlords; princely-state subjects — support anti-British rulers, peacefully oppose pro-British ones.

HOOKUnderground activities: Usha Mehta's underground radio, Bombay. Parallel governments: Ballia, UP (Chittu Pandey, first) · Tamluk, Bengal (Jatiya Sarkar/Satis Samant) · Satara, Maharashtra (Y.B. Chavhan & Nana Patil — longest-lived) · Talchar, Odisha.

Martyrs (raw extraction): Kushal Konwar — only Quit-India-phase martyr who was hanged. ★ Two further martyrs are PYQ-book-only (not in either source book, see A.2): Kanaklata Barua ("Birbala," Assam) shot hoisting the tricolour; posthumous title "Rani"/"Daughter of the Hills" from Nehru, Tamrapatra(1972) & Padma Bhushan(1982); and Matangini Hazra (Tamluk, Bengal), shot leading a procession 29 Sep 1942, martyred chanting "Vande Mataram."

5Pakistan Movement & Mediation Attempts

  • "Pakistan" coined by Rahmat Ali (1933, Parmar / 1935, Lucent — DOUBT), building on Iqbal's 1930 idea.
  • ★ Lahore/Pakistan Resolution, 22–24 March 1940 — Muslim League's Lahore session; demanded a separate Muslim homeland in NW+E India, rejecting the GoI Act 1935 federal scheme. Pakistan Day observed 23 March 1943.
  • C.R. Formula (1944) — Rajagopalachari's plebiscite proposal for Muslim-majority NW/East districts; Jinnah objected (wanted only League-approved Muslims voting); Savarkar condemned it.
  • Desai-Liaqat Pact (1945) — unsuccessful, Bhulabhai Desai (Congress) & Liaqat Ali Khan (League).
  • Wavell Plan & Shimla Conference (14 June–14 July 1945) — Viceroy Wavell's balanced-representation Executive Council proposal; failed on Jinnah's insistence that only League-picked Muslims join.

6INA, Subhas Chandra Bose & the RIN Mutiny

Indian National Army: formed 1942, Mohan Singh (with POWs, Singapore) → handed to Rash Behari Bose → reorganised by Subhas Chandra Bose, Singapore, 1943; Provisional Government of Free India, Singapore, 21 Oct 1943. 3 brigades (Subhas/Gandhi/Nehru) + Rani of Jhansi Regiment (women, Capt. Lakshmi Swaminathan). 14 Apr 1944: Col. Shaukat Ali Malik hoisted the tricolour at Moirang, Manipur — first on Indian soil by the INA.

Bose: b. Cuttack, 23 Jan 1897 (birthday = Parakram Divas); met Hitler as "Orlando Mazzotta" (1942); founded Free India/Tiger Legion, Berlin (1941); slogans "Delhi Chalo," "Jai Hind," "Give me blood, and I shall give you freedom"; d. 18 Aug 1945, air crash; called "Patriot of Patriots" by Gandhi; gave Gandhi the title "Father of the Nation" (1944, Singapore Radio).

HOOKINA Trials, Red Fort, Nov 1945–May 1946 — 1st trial: P.K. Sehgal, Shah Nawaz Khan, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon; defence incl. Bhulabhai Desai (chief), Nehru, Sapru, Asaf Ali. RIN/Ratings Mutiny, 18 Feb 1946 — HMS Talwar, Bombay, over poor pay/food; spread to Calcutta & Karachi; called off via Congress+League persuasion (Patel & Jinnah, per Lucent).

7Cabinet Mission, Direct Action Day & Mountbatten Plan

  • Cabinet Mission (March 1946) — PM Attlee's delegation: Lord Pethick-Lawrence (Chair), Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander. Rejected a separate Pakistan; proposed 3-tier structure — Federal Union / provincial Groups / provinces. Group A (Hindu-majority: Madras/Bombay/UP/Bihar/Odisha/CP), Group B (Muslim-majority: Punjab/NWFP/Sindh/Baluchistan), Group C (Bengal+Assam). Constituent Assembly + interim government proposed. Attlee's Declaration, 20 Feb 1947: power transferred by 30 June 1948.
  • Response: Congress accepted partially (opposed compulsory grouping); League initially accepted, then Jinnah called ★ Direct Action Day, 16 Aug 1946 — triggered the "Great Calcutta Killing." Noakhali Riots (Oct–Nov 1946) followed; Gandhi visited to restore peace.
  • Mountbatten Plan, 3 June 1947 ("Partition Plan") — basis for the Indian Independence Act; princely states could join India, Pakistan, or (initially) stay independent. Of 565 states, 562 joined; 3 held out — J&K, Hyderabad, Junagadh. Mountbatten: last Viceroy + 1st Governor-General of independent India (1947–48); C. Rajagopalachari: only Indian Governor-General (post later abolished).

8After Independence

  • Operation Polo (Sep 1948) — Hyderabad Police Action, Maj. Gen. J.N. Chaudhuri; Nizam Mir Osman Ali Khan surrendered 17 Sep 1948.
  • Maharaja Hari Singh signed the Instrument of Accession, 26 Oct 1947 (J&K).
  • Gandhi's assassination: 30 Jan 1948, Birla House, Delhi — shot by Nathuram Godse (3 bullets) during evening prayer; reason: blamed Gandhi's non-violence/Muslim-appeasement for Partition. Godse & accomplice Narayan Apte hanged, Ambala Jail, 15 Nov 1949. V.D. Savarkar (Hindu Mahasabha) was Godse's acknowledged political guru, though not formally an RSS leader.

3Miscellaneous — Colonial Architecture, Women Achievers & Grab-bag Facts

3.1 Colonial architecture

  • ★ French colonial architecture (Puducherry/Pondicherry's "White Town") — wide verandas, tall doors/windows, sloped tiled roofs, European classical influence adapted to tropics; NOT Mughal domes/arches; a distinctive "Franco-Tamil" blend (French classical + local Tamil).
  • ★ Churches of Old Goa (Basilica of Bom Jesus, Sé Cathedral) — Portuguese Baroque & Manueline styles.
  • ★ Notre Dame des Anges Church, Puducherry — French/Greco-Roman style; Doric & Corinthian columns give a "transparent enclosure" façade.
  • ★ British colonial architecture used lime mortar + large foundation platforms, enabling arches/domes/colonnaded verandas in the Indo-Saracenic style (a separate feature, not a direct consequence of the foundation technique).
  • ★ Rashtrapati Bhavan ("Viceroy's House") — planned after the 1911 Delhi capital-shift decision; construction began 1912, completed 1929; architect Edwin Lutyens.
  • ★ Victoria Memorial, Kolkata — built 1906–21 under Curzon, in memory of Queen Victoria.
