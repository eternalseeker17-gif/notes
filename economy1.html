1Economics, Economy & Economic Systems

Economics = study of how people/businesses/govts use limited resources to produce, distribute, consume goods & services → split into Macroeconomics (whole economy, top-down: GDP, inflation, fiscal policy) and Microeconomics (individual consumers/firms/markets, bottom-up: pricing, consumer behaviour).

HOOK PESTEL/PESTAL analysis — E = Economic ★ (interest rates, inflation, growth are its factors; 6 factors total: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal).
AspectMacroeconomicsMicroeconomics
FatherJ.M. Keynes — General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money (1936) ★Adam Smith — Founding Father of Modern Economics, The Wealth of Nations (1776); Alfred Marshall also called Father of Microeconomics, Principles of Economics (1890)
FocusGovt/country actions — aggregate demand/supply, prices, national income ★ (Ex: national income is a macro variable, price of tea is micro — a repeatedly tested pairing)Individual/business actions — demand, supply, competition
TRAP Aggregate Demand (AD) = C + I + G + (X − M) ★ — total demand for all final goods/services in an economy over a period; treating a single commodity as representative of "all goods" is a simplifying macro assumption ★ (an Assertion-Reason PYQ tests exactly this — the Reason "all goods behave identically under inflation" is FALSE, since some prices rise faster than others).

HOOK Socialist = "Centrally Planned Economy" ★ — govt decides what/how/for whom to produce, objective = social welfare not profit. Mixed economy ★ = private + public co-exist, market forces given freedom in the private sector while govt regulates strategic/social-welfare sectors. A key economic role of govt in both developed and developing countries: framing laws and delivering justice so markets function smoothly ★ (not merely regulating firms/promoting exports/currency exchange).

4Demand, Supply & Elasticity

Law of Demand ★ — inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded, ceteris paribus (income, price of other goods, preferences constant) ★. Demand curve slopes downward.

TRAP Exceptions to the Law of Demand ★ (tested repeatedly — know both):
  • Giffen Goods ★ — inferior/staple goods (e.g. wheat) where demand rises with price due to the income effect / absence of alternatives; the "Giffen Paradox" explains why the Law of Demand fails for such highly essential inferior goods when the consumer spends a large income-share on them.
  • Veblen Goods ★ — luxury goods (iPhone, Mercedes) where demand rises with price because of status/prestige (direct price-demand relationship).
Substitute goods (tea & coffee, Coke & Pepsi) still follow the Law of Demand (price of tea ↑ → demand shifts to coffee) — not itself an exception. "Speculative goods" and "Conspicuous goods" are NOT standard named exceptions per this PYQ's answer key (only Giffen/Veblen are).

Elasticity of Demand & Supply

HOOK Formula: Ed = %Δ demand / %Δ price. Price ↑10%, demand ↓20% → Ed = 20/10 = 2 → Elastic Demand ★ (E>1: small price change, large demand change). Total revenue is maximised when Ed = 1 (unitary elastic — MR becomes zero) ★.
TypeMeaningExample
Perfectly elastic (e=∞)Tiny price rise → demand to zeroPerfect competition, agriculture (Parmar's own example) — raw extraction's "gold/cars" example flagged as an internal inconsistency
Perfectly inelastic (e=0)Demand fixed regardless of priceNecessities: water, wheat, rice
Unitary elastic (e=1)Proportional change; rectangular hyperbola—

Elasticity of supply ★: Es = %Δ quantity supplied / %Δ price. "Relatively elastic supply" ★ = proportionate change in supply > proportionate change in price (Es>1).

HOOK Snob Effect ★ = demand falls as a good becomes common (consumer wants exclusivity — opposite of Bandwagon). Bandwagon/Demonstration Effect = demand rises because others are consuming it (imitation). Veblen Effect = demand rises with price itself (status symbol) — distinct from Bandwagon (imitating consumption pattern, not chasing high price per se).

5Utility, Costs & Returns

Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility ★ — first propounded by Hermann Heinrich Gossen ★ (per the PYQ book's own explanation; not named in the Parmar/Lucent raw extraction). As a consumer consumes more units, marginal utility from each additional unit decreases, can reach zero (satiety) then negative.

LawDescription
Law of DemandPrice & demand move in opposite directions
Law of Diminishing Marginal ProductAfter a point, increasing one input (others fixed) → declining marginal product ★
Law of Variable ProportionsMP rises, then falls, then turns negative as one input is increased — 3 stages ★
HOOK The Average Variable Cost (AVC) curve is U-shaped due to the Law of Variable Proportions ★ (not AFC, which is a simple downward-sloping rectangular-hyperbola-type curve since fixed costs spread over more units).
Returns to ScaleMeaning
Increasing (IRS) ★Output rises more than proportionally to inputs (e.g. inputs +50%, output +75% ★)
Constant (CRS)Output rises exactly proportionally
Decreasing (DRS)Output rises less than proportionally
HOOK Economies of Scale ★ — as production increases, average cost per unit falls (fixed costs spread over more units, bulk-cheaper raw materials, division of labour). Do not confuse with "Law of diminishing returns" (opposite direction) — a direct PYQ distractor pair.

Break-even point ★ = where total revenue = total cost (no profit, no loss) — NOT where MR=MC (that's the profit-maximising point).

Capital Formation ★ = part of income used for investment (machinery, buildings, infrastructure) instead of consumption — "net investment"; increases future productive capacity.

Indifference Curve & Isoquant

Indifference Curve ★ = combinations of two goods giving equal satisfaction; convex to origin (diminishing MRS); two ICs never intersect. Isoquant = combinations of inputs (labour+capital) giving the same output; slope = Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution.

7Macroeconomic Concepts

HOOK MPC (Marginal Propensity to Consume) = ΔC/ΔY, 0<MPC<1. MPC + MPS = 1. Multiplier = 1/(1−MPC).

Aggregate Demand ★ = C + I + G + (X−M) — sum of consumption, investment, govt spending, net exports, for all final goods/services in an economy in a period (usually a year).
