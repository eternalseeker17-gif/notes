1Population Growth & Measurement

TermDefinition ★
Population Density ★Total Population ÷ Total Area (persons/km²) — India's density (2011 Census) = 382/km² ★
Natural Growth Rate ★Birth Rate − Death Rate (excludes migration) ★
Rate of Natural IncreaseSame as above — NOT birth×death rate, NOT working-population ratio (tested distractors)
Annual Growth Rate% growth per year — a 2% rate = 2 new persons added per 100 in the base population ★ (per-100 framing, not per-person)
TRAP Population growth is measured in both absolute numbers AND percentage rate ★ — a tested Assertion-Reason: Assertion (both measures used) is TRUE, but the Reason ("absolute numbers alone give detailed trend insight") is FALSE — absolute numbers don't reveal the pace/speed of growth; only the percentage growth rate does ★.
HOOK Components of population change ★: Births, Deaths, Migration — Employment is NOT a component ★ (it affects distribution/living standards, not population growth/decline directly — a tested "which is unrelated" question).

2Demographic Transition Theory

HOOK Demographic Transition stages ★: Stage 1 (initial) — high birth rate + high death rate → high growth potential once death rate falls first. Population explosion ★ occurs when death rates decline rapidly while birth rates stay high ★ — a tested Assertion-Reason: Assertion TRUE, but Reason ("societal reproductive behaviour changes instantly with healthcare improvements") is FALSE — social/reproductive norms change slowly, over decades/generations, NOT immediately ★.

Replacement Level ★ = Total Fertility Rate (TFR) = 2.1 ★ — population stabilisation occurs when fertility drops to/below this level (transition from "explosive" to "stabilisation" stage) ★. The theory's scope does NOT explicitly account for migration ★, and social/cultural factors can alter its predictions ★ (both statements tested as correct in a multi-statement question).

Age-specific rates ★ are used in demography because birth/death probability varies by life stage — they reveal age-wise variation in fertility/mortality ★ (not literacy, elderly economic condition, or indicator reduction — tested distractors).

3National Population Policy (NPP) 2000

HOOK NPP 2000 objectives ★ — free/compulsory education up to age 14, reduce Infant Mortality Rate below 30 ★, raise girls' marriage age (delayed marriage) ★ — NOT to increase fertility rate ★ (goal is population stabilisation via reducing fertility to replacement level — a tested "which is incorrect" distractor). Long-term objective: stabilise population by 2045 ★.

4Census of India

FactDetail ★
Governing lawCensus Act, 1948 ★ — gives census officers legal authority to collect data
2011 Census — total population~121.08 crore
Population density (2011)382 persons/km²
Urbanisation level (2011)31.16% urban ★, 68.84% rural — urban growth rate during this census: 31.8% ★
Literacy rate — 1951 Census ★18.33% ★ (male 27.16%, female 8.86%) — 2011 rate: 74.04%
Uttar Pradesh's population share ★~19.98 crore = ~16% of India's total population (largest state) ★
Lowest sex ratio (2011) ★Daman & Diu (UT) — 618 females/1000 males ★; lowest among states: Haryana (879)
Most urbanised / least urbanised stateGoa (62.2% urban, highest) / Himachal Pradesh (10% urban, lowest)
Population Enumeration phase, 2011 Census ★Conducted February 2011 (mainly 9-28 Feb) ★ — House Listing/Housing Census was a separate, earlier (2010) phase, NOT postponed ★ (a tested Assertion-true/Reason-false: enumeration timing correct, but claim that Houselisting was "postponed" is false)
HOOK Religious composition (2011 Census) ★ — Muslims = largest minority nationally but a majority in NO state ★; Christians concentrated in urban areas; Jains concentrated in urban Rajasthan/Gujarat/Maharashtra; Buddhists concentrated mainly in Maharashtra ★ (a multi-statement question confirms these specific pairings).
TRAP Birth/death registration reporting ★ — Assertion: demographic statistics rely on accurate household reporting = TRUE; but Reason: "birth/death registration is mandatory and ALWAYS accurately followed" = FALSE ★ (mandatory under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969, but NOT consistently followed in practice due to awareness/administrative gaps ★).
