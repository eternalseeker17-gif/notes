4Classification of Goods

2 criteria → Excludability (can non-payers be excluded?) + Rivalry (does one's use reduce another's?).

  • Excludable → must pay (ticket) | Non-excludable → can't prevent use (air, free YouTube)
  • Rivalrous → my use blocks yours (apple, limited-edition car) | Non-rivalrous → doesn't (sunset, breathing) [B: reinforced — street light example]
RivalrousNon-Rivalrous
ExcludablePrivate Goods → paid, ↓availability for others → food, clothing, cars, parkingClub Goods → paid, shared w/o ↓supply → cinemas, private parks, Netflix
Non-ExcludableCommon Pool Resources → free, finite/depletable → fish stock, forest timber, coalPublic Goods ★ → free to all, non-rivalrous/non-excludable ★ → national defence, street lights, free-to-air TV

8Scarcity, Choice & PPF

Fundamental problem → scarcity (limited money/land/labour/time) vs unlimited wants → forces choice on resource allocation.

Production Possibility Frontier (PPF/PPC) → graph of max combos of 2 goods producible using available resources+tech, all resources used efficiently. Eg: ₹6000 → 6 pairs shoes OR 6 pairs jeans (all-or-mix).

Slope → downward/negative (trade-off: more of one = less of other). MRT (Marginal Rate of Transformation) = slope of PPC → amount of good sacrificed for 1 more unit of other.

On curve → full resource use | Under curve → under-utilisation | Beyond curve → not achievable.

Opportunity Cost → value of what's given up (eg buy jeans → shoes = opp. cost). Free Goods (air, sunlight) → zero opp. cost (abundant, no sacrifice needed).

9Utility, Indifference Curve & Marginal Concepts

Utility → satisfaction from consumption. Cardinal Utility → numerical/quantitative, less realistic | Ordinal Utility → ranked/qualitative, more realistic.

Indifference Curve (IC) → combos of 2 goods giving same satisfaction. Eg: (1 food+12 clothing) ~ (2 food+6 clothing) → same IC.

IC properties: convex to origin (rectangular hyperbola) [B: convexity = diminishing MRS]; slope = MRS (Marginal Rate of Substitution, formula: Δsacrifice/Δgain); 2 ICs never intersect.

Marginal Utility → extra satisfaction from 1 more unit. Marginal Product (MP) → extra output from 1 more input unit. Marginal Cost (MC) → Δtotal cost from 1 more unit. Marginal Revenue (MR) → extra income from selling 1 more unit.

AFC (Average Fixed Cost) → total fixed cost (rent) ÷ output → curve downward-sloping. AVC (Average Variable Cost) → variable costs (raw material) vs output → curve U-shaped ★ [B: U-shape reason = Law of Variable Proportions ★]. AVC + AFC = ATC.

HOOK Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility ★ → each extra unit → ↓additional satisfaction, ceteris paribus [B: propounded by Hermann Heinrich Gossen ★]. Graph: Zero MU = Point of Satiety; beyond → Negative MU.

Isoquant → combos of inputs (labour+capital) → same output; slope = MRTS (Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution).

10Demand, Supply & Equilibrium

Demand → qty willing+able to buy at a price, in a period. Law of Demand → inverse price-qty relation, holds only if: income constant, other-goods' price constant, no preference change (Ceteris Paribus).

Movement on curve → price change | Shift of curve → other factors change (income/preference/related-good price). Rightward shift = demand↑ | Leftward = demand↓.

TRAP Exceptions to Law of Demand ★: Giffen Goods ★ → inferior/non-luxury, demand↑ as price↑ (no better alternative available); eg wheat price rise [B: income-effect reasoning — spends large income share on it, price↑ → drops other items, MORE dependent on it, aka Giffen Paradox ★]. Veblen Goods ★ → luxury, demand↑ as price↑ (status symbol); eg iPhone, Mercedes. NOT exceptions: Substitute goods ★ [B — tested "which is NOT an exception"].

Demand function [B] → math relationship price↔qty; demand curve [B] → its graphical form, slopes down (Law of Demand).

Ex-Ante Aggregate Demand → intended expenditure; AD = C(intended consumption)+I(intended investment) [simplified, Parmar]. Full macro identity [B] ★: AD = C+I+G+(X−M) ★ (consumption+investment+govt spending+net exports).

Equilibrium: AD=AS. AD>AS → output↑ | AD<AS → inventory buildup → output↓.

HOOK Single-commodity-as-aggregate-good (macro simplification) A-R [B] ★: Assertion (simplifies aggregate D/S analysis) = TRUE ★; Reason ("all goods behave identically under inflation") = FALSE ★ (real economy: some prices rise fast, others stable/fall) — tested "A true, R false."

Supply → amount available for purchase/use. Supply curve → price vs qty producers willing to supply; upward/positive sloping (producers maximise profit → more supply at better price).

Equilibrium = D∩S curve intersection → equilibrium price/market-clearing price, Qd=Qs. Shifts if any factor shifts. Demand↓→shift left | Demand↑→shift right.

TRAP Market equilibrium statements [B] ★: "Qty firms want to sell = Qty consumers want to buy" → CORRECT ★. "Excess supply = demand>supply" → WRONG ★ (that's excess demand; excess supply = supply>demand — tested statement-pair).

Income Elasticity: = %Δqty demanded / %Δreal income. Luxury Goods → IE>1 (positive), demand rises more than income; eg luxury cars, designer clothes. Inferior Goods → IE<0 (negative), demand falls as income rises; eg low-quality foodgrains, public transport.

Cross-Price Elasticity → price Δ of Good A → demand Δ of Good B. Eg: Pepsi price+10% → Coke demand+5%. Negative cross-price elasticity = Complementary Goods (Py↑→Qy↓→Qx↓, consumed together).

14Macroeconomic Concepts — MPC, Autonomous Expenditure

MPC (Marginal Propensity to Consume) = ΔC/ΔY; 0<MPC<1; ↓at higher income. Eg: income+₹10,000, spend+₹1,000 → MPC=0.1.

Autonomous Expenditure → spending independent of income. Formula: C=A+cY (A=autonomous consumption, c=MPC, Y=income). Savings=1−c. Multiplier=1/(1−c). MPC+MPS=1.

Capital Formation [B] ★ → part of income used for investment (machinery/buildings/infra) instead of consumption → "net investment" ★ → ↑future productive capacity (NOT ↓FDI, ↑consumer credit, or ↑financial savings — tested distractors).

15Lucent §2 — Economy, Economics & Economic Systems

Economy → state of a country: production/consumption/money supply. Economics → branch of knowledge: production/consumption/transfer of wealth. 3 systems evolved as historical stages.

Capitalistic Economy ← Adam Smith's Wealth of Nations (1776) [Adam Smith 1723-1790, Glasgow professor] → against heavy govt regulation → stressed division of labour, laissez-faire, invisible hand/market forces + need for competition. USA adopted (1 yr post-book) → by 1800 established globally as capitalism, aka Private Enterprise/Free Enterprise/Market Economy System. Production/qty/price decided by market/private enterprise; no state economic role.

State Economy ← Karl Marx (1818-1883) → first in USSR post-Bolshevik Revolution 1917 → ideal form in PRC 1949. Two versions: USSR=Socialist Economy (collective ownership, large state role) | pre-1985 China=Communist Economy (state owns ALL property incl. labour, absolute state power). Marx: socialism = transition stage → communism (never realized). Capitalist economies mocked socialist/communist ones as "state capitalism" (state = sole exploiter).

Mixed Economy ← Great Depression 1929 → Smith's self-correcting-market belief collapsed → US→W.Europe spread → unemployment, demand fall, lockouts. → Keynes' General Theory (1936), Cambridge economist (1883-1946) → questioned laissez-faire/invisible hand ("strangulates the poor," wages/prices not flexible enough for full employment) → proposed strong govt intervention: ↑govt expenditure, discretionary fiscal policy (fiscal deficit, ↓interest rates, cheap money supply) → pulled economies out of Depression → led to govt's active economic role → essential goods made free by state → Mixed Economy born.

Prof. Oscar Lange (1904-65, Polish) → 1950s → suggested capitalist-style reforms for socialist economies (parallel to Keynes for capitalism) → coined 'Market Socialism' → rejected by state economies (seen as compromise toward democracy, "blasphemous" then).

16Economic Growth vs Development

Economic Growth → ↑econ variables over time → quantifiable (IMF/WB yardstick). Measured in: tonnes (food), km (roads), currency (production value), monetary terms (per capita income). Growth rate = annual concept, % of period-difference. Value-neutral but used positively by convention; negative case = "negative growth."

FY24-25 data: Q2 real GDP growth 5.4% (vs 8.1% Q2 FY23-24); H1 real GVA growth 6.2%; Real GDP Q2 FY25=₹44.10 lakh cr (vs ₹41.86 lakh cr); Nominal GDP Q2 FY25=₹76.60 lakh cr (vs ₹70.90 lakh cr, +8.0%). Retail inflation (CPI Combined, base 2012=100): 5.4% (2023-24) ↓from 6.7% (2022-23); avg 4.8% (Apr-Oct FY25).

GDP growth Q1/Q2 FY25 = 6.7%/5.4% (2011-12 constant prices); H1 real GDP growth=6.0%. PFCE H1 FY25 growth=6.7% YoY (rural demand signals: 2/3-wheeler + tractor sales). NABARD survey (Jan 2025): 78.5% rural households reported ↑consumption expenditure.

Manufacturing: industrial sector H1 FY25=6% (Q1=8.3%, Q2 moderated — oil/steel/cement sector challenges); Mfg PMI Dec 2024 = expansionary; RBI Industrial Outlook Survey → improved demand Q3/Q4 FY25. Services: H1 FY25=7.1%; PMI services expansionary; hospitality/air cargo/port/IT sectors performed well.

DOUBT All FY24-25 growth/inflation figures above are time-sensitive (BHARAT 2025/Economic Survey 2024-25/RBI Bulletin Dec 2024 sourced) — will not reflect current (Sept 2026) data; verify before relying.

Economic Development → pre-1950s: growth=development conflated. 1960s+: divergence noted (high growth ≠ high quality of life). Development variables: nutrition level, healthcare reach (hospitals/medicine/water/vaccination/sanitation), education level. Growth = precondition for development (income needed to fund quality-enhancing inputs); higher growth ≠ automatic higher development.
