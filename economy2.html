1Concept, Founders & Circular Flow

National Income = total value of all goods/services produced by a country's residents in a year (macroeconomic concept). First proposed by Dadabhai Naoroji; P.C. Mahalanobis headed the National Income Committee (1949); V.K.R.V. Rao used a scientific approach and gave the two-sector economy classification.

HOOK Circular Flow of Income — Households ⇄ Firms. Households provide land/labour/capital to firms, receive rent/wages/profit; firms sell goods/services back, receive expenditure. Two-sector model = Households+Firms; Three-sector adds Government; Four-sector (open economy) adds Foreign trade.

TRAP Injections (money entering: investment, govt spending, exports) vs Leakages (money exiting: savings, taxes, imports) ★. When households save part of their income instead of spending, that is a leakage from the circular flow ★ (reduces total demand; needs balancing via investment) — a PYQ tests exactly this in a two-sector economy.

2Final vs Intermediate Goods; Stock vs Flow

Final GoodsIntermediate Goods ★
Used directly for consumption/investment; not processed further. E.g. shirt, TV, refrigerator bought by a household ★Raw materials/inputs used to produce other goods — get transformed. E.g. copper used to make utensils ★
HOOK Why only final goods count in GDP ★: to avoid double counting, since intermediate-good value is already embedded in the final good's price. Capital goods (durable, e.g. machines) assist production without being transformed into the product itself — a PYQ pairs Statement I (intermediate goods = inputs) TRUE with Statement II (capital goods = durable, not transformed) also TRUE ★.

4Depreciation & the GVA/GDP/GNP/NDP/NNP Ladder

HOOK Depreciation ★ = wear-and-tear loss in value of fixed assets (machinery/equipment/buildings) during production; also called Replacement Investment ★ (investment needed just to maintain existing capital stock, equal to depreciation). Net Investment = Gross Investment − Depreciation ★ (e.g. ₹50 lakh machinery, ₹5 lakh depreciation → ₹45 lakh net investment ★).

TRAP Factor Cost ★ = payment to factors of production only (rent, wages, interest, profit) — excludes production/product taxes and does NOT subtract subsidy either; it reflects only the actual cost of production. FC = MP − Net Indirect Tax = MP − (Indirect Tax − Subsidy).
DOUBT GDP does not measure welfare/standard of living ★ — ignores income distribution, environmental damage (pollution), non-market activities, quality of life. 5India's GDP Sector Contribution

HOOK Services sector contributes most to India's GDP today (>54%) ★, though Agriculture provides the highest employment. In 1950-51, Industry's GDP share was ~12-13%, Agriculture ~53-59%, Services ~27-30% ★ — India was agriculture-based at Independence.

6Multiplier & Autonomous Investment

HOOK Investment Multiplier ★: a rise in autonomous investment causes a multiplied rise in national income, because MPS < 1 (people don't save their entire income, so extra spending recirculates) ★ — Multiplier = 1/(1−MPC) = 1/MPS. An Assertion-Reason PYQ confirms both statements TRUE and R correctly explains A.

7Real vs Nominal GDP, Per Capita & Personal Income

Real GDPNominal GDP
Constant prices, base year reference, inflation-adjustedCurrent prices, includes inflation, always ≥ Real GDP

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100 — also called Implicit Price Deflator.

Per Capita Income = National Income / Population. Personal Income = total individual income from all sources before taxes. Personal Disposable Income (PDI) ★ = Personal Income − Direct Taxes = money left with the household sector ★ after paying direct taxes/govt fees — this is what households can actually spend or save.

DOUBT GDP base year given as 2022-23 in Parmar vs Lucent's tables still using the 2011-12 base series — time-sensitive, carried forward from the raw extraction, not resolved. India's GDP world-rank figures (5th nominal/3rd PPP per Parmar vs 4th per Lucent's Forbes citation) are similarly time-sensitive and not independently verified — verify against current IMF/World Bank data before relying on either.

India's GDP/national-income data is calculated/issued by the National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) — the raw extraction notes Lucent's own text still names the since-merged CSO in one place.
