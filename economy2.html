1Concept, Founders & Circular Flow

National Income = total value of all goods/services produced by a country's residents in a year — a "Macroeconomic" concept. First proposed by Dadabhai Naoroji. P.C. Mahalanobis headed the National Income Committee (1949). V.K.R.V. Rao used a scientific approach to calculate National Income; he also gave the two-sector economy classification.

HOOK Circular Flow of Income — continuous movement of goods, services, resources and money in an economy. Households own land/labour/capital, provide these to firms for income (wages/rent/profits); firms use resources to produce goods/services, sell to households; households spend on goods/services (expenditure); firms pay households for resources (income) — a continuous cycle. Upper arrows: Goods & Services (Product Method), Consumer Expenditure (Expenditure Method); lower arrows: Wages/Rent/Dividends, Factor for Production (Income Method).

HOOK Law of Variable Proportions — increasing one factor of production (e.g. labour) while others stay fixed: total output first rises at an increasing rate, then rises at a decreasing rate, then declines (TP/AP/MP curves; Stage I, II, III; Point of Inflexion).

TRAP Injections (money entering the economy, ↑spending/income): Investment (capital-goods spending), Government Spending (infra/education/defence), Exports (foreign buyers' money entering). Leakages (money exiting the circular flow): Savings (households not spending), Taxes, Imports (money spent on foreign goods) ★. When households save instead of spending, that's a leakage ★ — reduces total demand, must be balanced by investment.

2Three Methods to Calculate National Income

1. Value Added / Production / Output Method

Calculates the value of a product/service at each stage of production. Value Added Method = Output − Input.

CottonYarnClothShirt
Value of output₹100₹200₹400₹800
Value added₹100₹100₹200₹400

Gross Value Added (GVA) = value producers add to goods/services they buy and sell. GVA = GVA₁ + GVA₂ + GVA₃ + ... + GVAN. The National Statistics Office (printed "CSO") adopted GVA in 2015.

2. Income Method

Includes all income generated by factors of production that are inputs to producing final goods/services, economy-wide. Components:

  • Compensation to Employees — total wages/salaries paid to workers.
  • Operating Surplus — profit earned by businesses (profit, rent, interest) after deducting employee compensation.
  • Mixed Income — income of self-employed individuals/unincorporated businesses, where labour and capital income elements can't be distinctly separated. Eg: a doctor running their own clinic.

3. Expenditure Method ★

Sums all spending on goods/services in the economy — the most common GDP-calculation method. Expenditure Method = C + G + I + (X − M) ★ (C=Consumption, G=Government Expenditure, I=Investment, X=Export, M=Import).

HOOK Orange Economy — the part of the economy based on creativity, culture and intellectual property; ideas/talent/cultural expression converted into economic value/jobs. Examples: Film industry, Music industry, Fashion design.

3Final vs Intermediate Goods; Economic Problems; Inventory

Final GoodsIntermediate Goods ★
Will not undergo any further economic process; used by the consumer for personal consumption or investment; direct consumption/capital investment. Eg: buying tea from a shop for personal use ★Semi-finished products that undergo further economic process/transformation; used as inputs to produce other goods, get customised in the process; further processing or resale. Eg: copper for utensils ★, a tea shop buying tea to prepare and sell to customers ★
HOOK Why only final goods count in GDP ★: avoids double counting, since intermediate-good value is already embedded in the final good's price. Capital goods (durable, e.g. machines) assist production without being transformed into the product itself — a PYQ pairs Statement I (intermediate goods = inputs) TRUE with Statement II (capital goods = durable, not transformed) also TRUE ★.

Every economy faces 3 fundamental problems from scarcity: What to Produce? How to Produce? For Whom to Produce? "What to Produce" = deciding which goods/services to produce and in what quantity, given limited resources must be allocated between different goods types.

Consumer GoodsCapital Goods
Finished goods used directly by individuals for personal consumption, not used to produce other goods. Eg: clothes, food, smartphonesGoods used by businesses to produce other goods/services; help production, not consumed immediately. Eg: machines to produce T-shirts, factory equipment, tools

Inventory = items a business keeps in stock for sale/production (raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods). If sales increase, inventory decumulates; if sales decrease, inventory accumulates — can be planned or unplanned.

Aids/Auxiliaries to Trade = factors essential for bringing goods from production to consumption — essential services/functions assisting business. Eg: transportation, communication, banking, insurance, warehousing, advertising.

StockFlow
Static in nature; measured at a point in time. Eg: wealth, loan, inventory, capital, money supplyDynamic in nature; measured over a period of time. Eg: income, expenditure, change in inventory, depreciation

4Depreciation & the GVA/GDP/GNP/NDP/NNP Ladder

HOOK Depreciation ★ = wear-and-tear loss in value of fixed assets (machinery/equipment/buildings) during production; also called Replacement Investment ★ (investment needed just to maintain existing capital stock, equal to depreciation). Net Investment = Gross Investment − Depreciation ★ (e.g. ₹50 lakh machinery, ₹5 lakh depreciation → ₹45 lakh net investment ★).
AggregateDefinition / Formula
GDP ★Total value of all final goods/services produced within a country's territory in a financial year (1 April–31 March). Second-hand goods are never counted. Only final-goods value counted, to avoid double counting. Developed by Russian-American economist Simon Kuznets in 1934.
GNP ★Total value of final goods/services produced by a country's nationals. GNP = GDP − Factor Income to Abroad + Factor Income from Abroad, i.e. GNP = GDP + NFIA ★ (Net Factor Income from Abroad).
NDPCountry's economic output after adjusting for depreciation of fixed capital. NDP = GDP − Depreciation.
NNP ★Monetary value of finished goods/services produced by a country's citizens in a given period. NNP = GNP − Depreciation ★ — the difference between GNP and NNP is depreciation ★.
Market Price (MP)Factor Cost (FC)
Final value of the product sold, includes indirect taxesCost of factors of production / total value of inputs, indirect tax NOT included

FC = MP − Indirect Tax + Subsidy, equivalently FC = MP − (Indirect Tax − Subsidy) = MP − Net Indirect Tax. Basic Price = FC + Net Production Tax (Net Production Tax = Production Tax − Subsidy).

DOUBT REMEMBER (as printed): Product taxes are levied per unit of goods/services sold (e.g. GST, excise duty, sales tax); production taxes are paid regardless of output volume (land taxes, stamp duties, licence fees). Production taxes are independent of production quantity; product taxes depend on sales volume.
HOOK "To Summarise" mnemonic (as printed, Hindi): (1) Gross से Net में जाना हो तो Depreciation subtract करो — going Gross→Net, subtract Depreciation. (2) Domestic से National में जाना हो तो NFIA add करो — going Domestic→National, add NFIA. (3) MP से FC में जाना हो तो Net Indirect Tax को subtract करो — going MP→FC, subtract Net Indirect Tax.

Calculation of income: Nation's Territory → GDP; Nation's People → GNP; Total Income = Total Expenditure = Total value of goods and services.

Factor Cost — trap

TRAP Factor Cost ★ = payment to factors of production only (rent, wages, interest, profit) — excludes production/product taxes and does NOT subtract subsidy either; it reflects only the actual cost of production.

GDP and Welfare

DOUBT GDP does not measure welfare/standard of living ★. Limitations, in full: (1) Ignores Income Distribution — high GDP can coexist with widespread poverty since distribution isn't shown; (2) Excludes Non-Market Activities — household work, voluntary service, caregiving aren't counted since not sold in a market; (3) Ignores Environmental Damage — pollution-causing production raises GDP but can lower quality of life; (4) Excludes Quality-of-Life Factors — health, education, leisure, safety, happiness aren't counted; (5) Underground Economy Not Included — informal/black-market activity generally uncounted. A tested Assertion-Reason confirms both A (GDP doesn't tell the whole story) and R (ignores wear-and-tear/pollution/distribution) TRUE ★.

5India's GDP Sector Contribution & Real vs Nominal GDP

HOOK Services sector contributes most to India's GDP today (>54%) ★, though Agriculture provides the highest employment. In 1950-51, Industry's GDP share was ~12-13%, Agriculture ~53-59%, Services ~27-30% ★ — India was agriculture-based at Independence.
Real GDPNominal GDP
Base year taken as reference; calculated on constant prices; inflation-adjustedMore than Real GDP due to inflation; calculated on current prices; inflation NOT adjusted

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100 — also called Implicit Price Deflator and Price Index. GDP of India is calculated by the National Statistical Office (NSO), MoSPI. Base year (per Parmar): 2022-23.

Per Capita Income = National Income / Population. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) — amount of goods/services purchasable with a specific sum of money; also called "a common basket of goods." As of 2026 (per Parmar): India's GDP ranked 5th in the world (nominal); India's economy ranked 3rd in the world by PPP, after USA and China (whose economies are considered "inflated" in nominal terms).

Personal Income = total individual income from all sources before taxes. Ranking of GDP (Nominal), per Parmar: 1. USA, 2. China, 3. Germany, 4. Japan, 5. India. PI = NI + Income received but not earned − Income earned but not received; equivalently PI = NI + Govt. transfers + Net interest paid by Govt. − Corporate tax − payment for social security; equivalently PI = NI + Transfer Payment − Undistributed corporate profit (Transfer payment examples: government subsidies, scholarship, pension).

Personal Disposable Income (PDI) ★ = money a person has after paying taxes/mandatory withholdings. PDI = Personal Income − Tax ★ — belongs to the household sector ★.

Potential GDP = total output an economy would produce with resources fully utilised. Green GDP = GDP − Environmental Damage. Potential GDP − Real GDP = Recessionary Gap. Gross Domestic Capital Formation = total investment within a country in a period, to create/increase physical assets. Eg: factory building, new machinery.

6Multiplier & Autonomous Investment

HOOK Investment Multiplier ★: a rise in autonomous investment causes a multiplied rise in national income, because MPS < 1 (people don't save their entire income, so extra spending recirculates) ★ — Multiplier = 1/(1−MPC) = 1/MPS. An Assertion-Reason PYQ confirms both statements TRUE and R correctly explains A.

"Critical Analysis" box — how national income is measured

MoSPI released two interrelated datasets on 30 May 2025: Q4 FY25 growth estimate, and provisional full-year FY25 estimates. Economic growth is measured using two metrics: GDP (adds up all expenditures — by individuals, governments, private businesses — demand-side view) and GVA (supply-side view — sums value added/income at each production stage across sectors). GDP = GVA + (taxes earned by government) − (subsidies provided by government).

Why "provisional"? Estimates get revised over several years. Sequence: First Advance Estimates (FAE) in January → Second Advance Estimates (SAE) at end-February (incorporating Q3 data) → Provisional Estimates (PE) by May-end (incorporating Q4 data) → First Revised Estimates a year later → Final Estimates two years later. For FY25, these arrive in 2026 and 2027 respectively.

Four key takeaways (30 May 2025 data):

  1. Nominal GDP: grew to ₹330.7 trillion (lakh crore) by end-March 2025, +9.8% over FY24. In USD terms (÷85.559 exchange rate) = $3.87 trillion. This 9.8% was the third-slowest nominal growth since the current government took charge (2014) and the sixth-slowest since India's 1991 liberalisation. CAGR since 2014-15 = 10.3%; CAGR since the NDA's 2nd-term start (2019) = 9.8%.
  2. Real GDP: grew 6.5% in FY25 to ₹188 trillion — deceleration from FY24's 9.2% is starker than the nominal-GDP deceleration (gap reflects inflation's effect). Real-GDP CAGR is just above 5% since 2019, just above 6% since 2014 — India's growth momentum has slowed over the decade.
  3. GVA & sectoral health: real GVA across Agriculture/allied, Industry (manufacturing, construction, etc.) and Services grew 6.4% in FY25 (vs 8.6% in FY24) — no sector has grown at a CAGR near 6% since 2019-20.
  4. Manufacturing vs Agriculture: since 2019-20, manufacturing GVA's CAGR (4.04%) has been slower than agriculture and allied activities' (4.72%) — partly explains high urban/youth unemployment and labour moving back to villages/agriculture.

Sources: NSO MoSPI Report Q3 28.02.2025; Indian Express, 31 May 2025.

Formulae (Lucent, in full)

  1. GVA at basic prices (Production Approach) = Output at basic price − Intermediate Consumption
  2. GVA at basic prices (Income Approach) = CE + OS/MI + CFC + Production taxes less Production subsidies
  3. GDP = ΣGVA at basic prices + Product taxes less Product subsidies
  4. NDP/NNI = GDP/GNI − CFC (Consumption of Fixed Capital)
  5. GNI = GDP + Net primary income from Rest of World (Receipts less payments)
  6. Primary Incomes = CE + Property and Entrepreneurial Income
  7. NNDI = NNI + other current transfers from ROW, net (Receipts less payments)
  8. GNDI = NNDI + CFC = GNI + other current transfers from ROW, net (Receipts less payments)
  9. Gross Capital Formation (financing side) = Gross Savings + Net Capital Inflow from ROW
  10. GCF (expenditure side) = GFCF + CIS + Valuables
  11. Gross Disposable Income of Govt. = GFCE + Gross Saving of Central Government
  12. Gross Disposable Income (GDI) of Households = GNDI − GDI of Govt. − Gross Savings of All Corporations

Remarks on the formulae: Production taxes/subsidies relate to production, independent of output volume — e.g. Production Taxes: land revenues, stamps & registration fees, tax on profession; Production Subsidies: subsidies to railways, village/small industries. Product taxes/subsidies are per-unit — e.g. Product Taxes: GST, excise duties, sales tax, service tax, import/export duties; Product Subsidies: food, petroleum, fertilizer subsidies. "Other Current Transfers" = current transfers other than primary incomes. Gross Capital Formation is estimated via two approaches: (i) flow of funds (Gross Saving + net capital flow from RoW), (ii) commodity flow approach (by asset type).

GDP Price Deflator measures how much an economy's real output value is reduced by inflation; accounts for all final goods including exports, but does not factor in import prices. Formula: GDP Price Deflator = (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100.
