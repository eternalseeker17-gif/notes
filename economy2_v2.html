1National Income — Definitions & Pioneers

  • National Income → total value of goods+services by a country's residents in a year → macroeconomic concept
  • Concept first given by → Dadabhai Naoroji
  • National Income Committee (1949) → headed by P.C. Mahalanobis
  • V.K.R.V. Rao → scientific approach to calc. National Income; also gave two-sector economy classification
  • National wealth ↔ National income → wealth = present assets at a point in time; income = production power over a period
  • Calculated on 1-year base → captures all seasons
DOUBT Data-issuing body conflict: Lucent §5 says "issued by Central Statistical Organisation (CSO)"; Lucent §1 (same book) attributes releases to NSO/MoSPI. CSO has since merged into NSO — both statements transcribed as printed, not reconciled.

NNP at Factor Cost = NNP at Market Price − Indirect Taxes + Subsidies = GNP at MP − Indirect Taxes + Subsidies = National Income.

2Circular Flow of Income

  • Def → continuous movement of goods/services/resources/money in an economy
  • Households → provide land/labour/capital to Firms → get income (wages/rent/profit)
  • Firms → produce goods/services → sell to Households (expenditure)
  • Real Flow → movement of goods/services/factors of production | Money Flow → movement of money/payments
Factor of ProductionPayment
LandRent
LabourWages
CapitalInterest
EntrepreneurProfit
  • 2-Sector Model → Households+Firms (simplest)
  • 3-Sector Model → +Government
  • 4-Sector Model (Open Economy) → +Foreign trade
  • Injection ★ → funds entering economy, ↑spending/income → eg Investment, Govt Spending, Exports
  • Leakage ★ → funds exiting circular flow, ↓spending/growth → eg Savings, Taxes, Imports
HOOK 2-sector economy [B] ★: households save part of income → savings = leakage ★ from circular flow → ↓total demand → must be balanced via investment (NOT "automatic firm-profit increase," inflation, or constant output — tested distractors).

3Three Methods to Calculate National Income

MethodMechanism
Value Added / Production / Output MethodValue at each production stage; Value Added = Output − Input
Income MethodSum of all factor-of-production income in producing final goods/services
Expenditure MethodSum of all spending on goods/services — most common GDP method; = C+G+I+(X−M)

Worked example (Value Added): Cotton ₹100 → Yarn ₹200 → Cloth ₹400 → Shirt ₹800 (output at each stage); value added at each stage = 100 / 100 / 200 / 400.

GVA → value producers add to goods/services bought+sold; GVA = GVA₁+GVA₂+...+GVA_N. Adopted by CSO in 2015 (as printed; CSO since merged into NSO).

Income Method components: (a) Compensation to Employees → total wages/salaries; (b) Operating Surplus → profit+rent+interest after deducting employee compensation; (c) Mixed Income → self-employed/unincorporated business income, where labour+capital factor-income can't be separated → eg a doctor running their own clinic.

Orange Economy → creativity/culture/IP-based economy → eg film industry, music industry, fashion design.

5GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP — Definitions & Core Formulas

  • GDP ★ → total value of final goods/services within a country's territory, in a financial year (1 Apr–31 Mar); second-hand goods never counted; only final goods counted (avoids double counting) [B: excludes intermediate goods' value — the "usually excluded" answer ★]
  • GDP developed by → Simon Kuznets (Russian-American economist), 1934
  • GDP full form ★ [B] = Gross Domestic Product
TRAP GDP's 5 limitations as a welfare measure: (1) ignores income distribution; (2) excludes non-market activities (household work/volunteering/caregiving); (3) ignores environmental damage; (4) excludes quality-of-life factors (health/education/leisure/safety/happiness); (5) excludes underground/black-market economy. [B ★ A-R: "GDP doesn't tell the whole wellbeing story" = TRUE; "doesn't count wear/tear, pollution, income distribution" = TRUE, correctly explains it ★.]
  • GNP ★ → total value of final goods/services by nationals of a country (residents, within+abroad) [B: "total global income of Indians" ★]; GNP = GDP − Factor Income to Abroad + FI from Abroad, OR GNP = GDP + NFIA (Net Factor Income from Abroad)
  • NDP → economic output after adjusting for depreciation; NDP = GDP − Depreciation
  • Depreciation → asset's monetary value ↓ over time (wear & tear), seen in capital goods [B: replacement investment = depreciation; Gross Investment − Depreciation = Net Investment ★; worked example: machinery ₹50 lakh, depreciation ₹5 lakh → Net Investment = ₹45 lakh ★]
  • NNP ★ → monetary value of finished goods/services by a country's citizens in a period; NNP = GNP − Depreciation [B: GNP-NNP difference = Depreciation ★ — the tested distinguishing fact]
Market Price (MP)Factor Cost (FC)
Final selling value, includes indirect taxesCost of factors of production; indirect tax NOT included [B ★: FC = ONLY payment to factors — land(rent)/labour(wages)/capital(interest)/entrepreneurship(profit); excludes product/production taxes, no subsidy subtracted ★]

FC = MP − Indirect Tax + Subsidy = MP − Net Indirect Tax. Basic Price = FC + Net Production Tax (Net Production Tax = Production Tax − Subsidy).

Product tax (per-unit, eg GST/excise/sales tax) ↔ Production tax (independent of output, eg land tax/stamp duty/licence fee).

Mnemonic: Gross→Net = subtract Depreciation | Domestic→National = add NFIA | MP→FC = subtract Net Indirect Tax.

Nation's Territory=GDP | Nation's People=GNP | Total Income=Total Expenditure=Total value of goods/services.

6National Income — Full Formula Reference (advanced, Lucent)

FormulaDefinition
GVA at basic prices (Production Approach)= Output at basic price − Intermediate Consumption
GVA at basic prices (Income Approach)= CE + Operating Surplus/Mixed Income + CFC + Production taxes less Production subsidies
GDP= ΣGVA at basic prices + Product taxes less Product subsidies
NDP / NNI= GDP/GNI − CFC (Consumption of Fixed Capital)
GNI= GDP + Net Primary Income from Rest of World (Receipts − Payments)
Primary Incomes= Compensation of Employees + Property & Entrepreneurial Income
NNDI= NNI + other current transfers from RoW, net
GNDI= NNDI + CFC = GNI + other current transfers from RoW, net
Gross Capital Formation (financing side)= Gross Savings + Net Capital Inflow from RoW
GCF (expenditure side)= GFCF + Changes in Stock (CIS) + Valuables
Gross Disposable Income of Govt.= GFCE + Gross Saving of Central Govt.
Gross Disposable Income of Households= GNDI − GDI of Govt. − Gross Savings of All Corporations

Production taxes/subsidies: independent of output volume; eg Land Revenue, Stamps & Registration fees, Tax on Profession (taxes); Railways subsidy, village/small-industries subsidy (subsidies). Product taxes/subsidies: per-unit; eg GST, Excise, Sales Tax, Service Tax, Import/Export duties (taxes); Food/Petroleum/Fertilizer subsidy (subsidies). "Other Current Transfers" = transfers other than primary incomes. GCF estimated 2 ways: flow-of-funds (Gross Saving + net capital flow from RoW) OR commodity-flow (by asset type).

7Real vs Nominal GDP, Deflator & Income Measures

Real GDPNominal GDP
Base-year reference; constant prices; inflation-adjustedCurrent prices; inflation NOT adjusted → higher than Real GDP due to inflation

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100 → aka "Implicit Price Deflator" / "Price Index." Does NOT factor in import prices; accounts for value of all final goods incl. exports. India's GDP calculated by NSO under MoSPI.

HOOK Sectoral GDP-share shift [B] ★: 1950-51 → Industry ≈12-13% of GDP, Agriculture ≈53-59%, Services ≈27-30% (economy mainly agriculture-based then). Today → Services = highest GDP contributor, >54% of GDP ★ — even though Agriculture still employs the most people ★ (economic value-add ≠ employment share); within services, Trade/Hotels/Transport/Communication/Financial Services are the most significant contributors ★.
DOUBT Base-year conflict: Parmar states GDP base year = 2022-23 (as of 2026); Lucent's own data tables use the 2011-12 base series throughout (SAE/FRE/FE tables below). Both transcribed as printed, not reconciled — verify current official base year before relying on either.
  • Per Capita Income → avg money earned per person in an area/period; = National Income / Population
  • Purchasing Power Parity → goods/services buyable with given money ("common basket of goods")
  • Personal Income → total individual income from all sources, before taxes; PI = NI + Income received but not earned − Income earned but not received, OR PI = NI + Govt transfers + Net interest paid by Govt + Corporate tax payment for social security (garbled as printed); PI = NI + Transfer Payment − Undistributed Corporate Profit (Transfer Payment eg = Govt subsidies/Scholarship/Pension)
  • Personal Disposable Income ★ → money left after taxes+mandatory withholding; = Personal Income − Tax [B: belongs to households ★ — the tested "who does PDI belong to" answer]
  • Potential GDP → total output if resources fully utilised; Recessionary Gap = Potential GDP − Real GDP
  • Green GDP = GDP − Environmental Damage
  • Gross Domestic Capital Formation → total investment to create/increase physical assets; eg factory building, new machinery
