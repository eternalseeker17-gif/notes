3Revenue vs Capital (Receipts & Expenditure) — full detail

Budget divided into two heads: Revenue and Capital. Revenue = funds a govt collects to finance activities/public services, primarily from taxes plus fees/fines/duties. Tree: Budget → Revenue {Receipt, Expenditure} and Capital {Receipt, Expenditure}.

Revenue Receipts ★

Do NOT create liability or reduce assets; non-redeemable; do not lead to a claim on government.

  • Tax Revenue: income collected via taxation. Eg: Wealth Tax.
  • Non-Tax Revenue: recurring income from non-tax sources. Eg: interest on loans, PSU earnings, railway tickets.

Revenue Expenditure ★

Spending that neither increases assets nor decreases liabilities.

  • Plan Revenue Expenditure: part of the plan budget, no assets/liabilities created. Eg: govt grants/financial assistance.
  • Non-Plan Revenue Expenditure: day-to-day operations, excl. capital expenditure. Eg: salary/pension, interest payment (largest component) ★, infrastructure maintenance.

Capital Receipts ★

Physical/financial resources used to produce value. EITHER create a liability OR reduce government assets — creates new liabilities, reduces an existing asset.

  • Capital Receipt: Eg. loan, disinvestment ★.
  • Capital Expenditure: funds to acquire/upgrade/maintain long-term assets (property, infra, equipment) — creates new assets, reduces liabilities. Eg: loan repayment, developmental expenditure, infra creation (roads/schools/hospitals).
TRAP Sale of PSU shares/disinvestment ★ = capital receipt (reduces govt assets) — NOT a revenue receipt. Loan from RBI = capital receipt because it creates a future liability ★. Capital Account of the budget = records changes in govt's assets/liabilities ★.

6GST, Cascading Tax & Other Taxes (Parmar)

GST: introduced via 101st Constitutional Amendment Act ★; effective 1 July 2017. GST Council (Art. 279A) = constitutional body, 33 members (2 Centre + 31 States/UTs) ★, chaired by the Finance Minister. Cascading Tax (Tax on Tax) = tax levied at every production/distribution stage without credit for tax already paid — raises final price.

Surcharge = additional tax on income tax for taxpayers above a threshold. Cess = tax on tax liability, levied for a specific purpose. Surcharge and Cess are levied by the Central Government but NOT shared with states ★. Pigouvian Tax = tax on activities causing negative externalities (external costs not in market price) — levied to reduce those externalities.

WMA (Ways and Means Advances), 1997 Agreement — Centre meets temporary receipts/expenditure mismatches via RBI-provided WMA; purpose: manage temporary cash-flow shortages, not a permanent finance source.

8Crowding In/Out; Budget Types; Deficit Financing

Crowding In = govt spending (esp. infra/public investment) stimulates private-sector investment → higher overall economic activity. Crowding Out = increased govt borrowing/expenditure raises interest rates, reduces private investment (govt competes with private sector for funds).

Budget TypeCondition
Balanced BudgetTotal revenue = total expenditure
Surplus BudgetReceipts > expenditure
Deficit BudgetExpenditure > receipts

Deficit Financing (concept by J.M. Keynes ★) = govt expenditure exceeds revenue, gap financed by creating new money or borrowing. Debt Financing = raising funds via borrowing (loans from banks/FIs, bonds) — repaid with interest. Equity Financing = raising funds by issuing shares — investors become owners, share profits via dividends, no repayment obligation.

DOUBT Lucent's own text (§10) gives "Primary Deficit = Revenue Deficit − Interest Payments" — an internal misprint contradicting its own correct definition ("fiscal deficit minus interest payments") one sentence earlier; the PYQ book's tested answer uses Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments, used as correct in the table above.
HOOK Worked example ★: Total Revenue Receipts ₹50L cr, Total Expenditure ₹75L cr, Borrowings ₹20L cr → Fiscal Deficit = 75−50 = ₹25L cr ★ (borrowing of ₹20L cr covers only part of it — remainder from non-debt capital receipts). Monetised Deficit (Lucent) = increment in Net RBI Credit to the Central Government.

Lucent §10's own deficit definitions (fuller wording, all 4 types)

  1. Revenue Deficit = Total Revenue Expenditure − Total Revenue Receipts = Non-plan Expenditure + Plan Expenditure − (net tax revenue + non-tax revenue). Reflects what the government is borrowing for — like an individual borrowing to pay house rent: borrowing+spending with no durable asset created, implying a future repayment obligation with no offsetting investment. Occurs when realised net income is less than projected/budgeted.
  2. Budget Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts = (non-plan + plan expenditure) − (Revenue Receipts + Capital Receipts). Total receipts here include current revenue and net internal/external capital receipts.
  3. Fiscal Deficit = Revenue Receipts (net tax + non-tax revenue) + Capital Receipts (only recoveries of loans and other receipts) − Total Expenditure (plan + non-plan). Difference between total expenditure (revenue, capital, and loans net of repayment) and revenue receipts plus non-borrowing capital receipts that ultimately accrue to government.
  4. Primary Deficit = fiscal deficit minus interest payments — shows how much govt borrows to pay for expenses other than interest, and how much it adds to future repayment burden based on past/present policy.

11Operation Twist

Operation Twist — RBI monetary-policy tool: simultaneously buys long-term govt securities and sells short-term securities, to reduce long-term interest rates and manage the yield curve. Buying long-term bonds raises their prices, lowers yields, making long-term loans cheaper. (Printed at the end of Parmar's Budget/Taxation chapter, though thematically a monetary-policy tool — see Batch 5.)

Budget Documents & Accounts (Lucent, in full)

Every budget gives 3 sets of figures: (a) actual figures for the preceding year, (b) budget/revised figures for the current year, (c) budget estimates for the following year. Core budget document = Annual Financial Statement, under Article 112 (Centre) / Article 202 (States, before the state legislature).

Government accounts kept in 3 parts:

AccountDetail
Consolidated FundAll revenues received, loans raised, and recoveries of loans granted, by Government. All expenditure comes from here; no withdrawal without Parliament's authorisation.
Contingency FundAn imprest at the President's disposal, for urgent unforeseen expenditure pending Parliament's authorisation — later recouped from the Consolidated Fund once approved. Corpus (per India 2023, from April 2021): ₹30,000 crore.
Public AccountTransactions where Government acts as a banker (provident funds, small savings, other deposits) — no Parliamentary authorisation needed for these payments.

Budget comprises Revenue Budget (current receipts — taxation, PSU surplus — and expenditures) and Capital Budget (capital receipts/expenditure — domestic/foreign loans, loan repayments, foreign aid).

Finance, Department of Economic Affairs, Finance Bill, Appropriation Bill, Demands for Grants

Ministry of Finance — administers government finances; handles all economic/financial matters, resource mobilisation, expenditure regulation incl. transfers to states. Department of Economic Affairs — nodal agency for formulating/monitoring domestic and international economic policy; prepares/presents the Union Budget (incl. Railway Budget) and state-government budgets under President's Rule/UT administrations.

Demands for Grants — expenditure estimates from the Consolidated Fund, voted by Lok Sabha per Article 113 — presented alongside the AFS. Finance Bill — a Money Bill (Art. 110), accompanied by an explanatory Memorandum; presented alongside the AFS per Art. 110(1)(a), detailing tax imposition/abolition/remission/alteration/regulation proposed in the Budget. Appropriation Bill — Parliament's approval to withdraw from the Consolidated Fund the amounts voted via Demands for Grants and charged expenditure; under Article 114(3), no withdrawal without such an enacted law.

Sources of Revenue — per the Constitution (Eightieth Amendment) Act, 2000 (retrospective from 1 April 1996): all Union List taxes (except Art. 268/269 duties, Art. 271 surcharges, and specific-purpose cesses) are levied/collected by GoI and distributed between Union/States as the President prescribes on FC recommendation. Main Union tax-revenue sources: customs duties, Union excise duties, service tax, corporate/income taxes. Non-tax revenue: interest receipts, dividends/profits, fines, sovereign-function receipts, regulatory charges/licence fees, user charges for public goods/services.

Public Debt of India — 3 categories: Internal debt, External debt, Other liabilities. Internal debt: fixed-tenure/fixed-rate government papers issued via auction — market loans (dated securities), treasury bills (91/182/364 days), 14-day T-bills (states only), cash management bills, special RBI securities, compensation/other bonds, non-negotiable non-interest-bearing rupee securities to international financial institutions, market-stabilisation-scheme securities (to reduce gold/import dependency). External debt: loans from foreign governments/multilateral institutions — India's foreign borrowing comes via multilateral agencies/bilateral sources as part of Official Development Assistance (ODA); the Union does not borrow directly from international capital markets. Other liabilities (not part of public debt): post office savings deposits, small savings schemes, post office cash certificates, provident funds, other deposits. RBI manages Central/State public debt and acts as their banker (RBI Act 1934, Sections 20/21/21A).

Tax = compulsory contribution by a citizen/organisation to Government, for welfare expenses. Levied/collected at 3 levels: Central, State, Local. Centre-State tax distribution set out constitutionally; rationalised periodically via the Finance Commission.

DOUBT The "Central Government taxes" row above lists Property/Succession/Wealth/Gift Tax and "Tax on agricultural wealth" as Central — contradicts standard practice (agricultural income tax is a State subject) and the source's own statement that "Agricultural income in India is free from income tax" — as printed, not reconciled.

Progressive Tax = takes a higher proportion of income as income rises; Indian Income Tax is progressive and direct. Excise Tax, Sales Tax, VAT, Entertainment Tax are listed as indirect taxes (as printed — VAT/sales/service tax have since been subsumed by GST, book not updated). Main indirect-tax forms: customs/excise duties, sales tax. Centre levies customs/excise (except alcoholic liquor/narcotics); sales tax is exclusively State jurisdiction. Union excise duties form the most significant part of central taxes (as printed — outdated post-GST). Major state tax-revenue sources: shares in union excise duties/income tax, commercial taxes, land revenue, stamp duty, registration fees, state excise on alcohol/narcotics — sales tax is the most important component of commercial taxes.

CBDT — created by the Central Board of Revenue Act 1963; apex body administering direct-tax laws; cadre-controlling authority for IRS officers and Income Tax Department officials.

DOUBT Revenue Collection (time-sensitive): net Direct Tax collection FY2023-24 = ₹19,60,166 crore (provisional). FY2024-25 (up to Nov 2024) = ₹12,46,229 crore = 56.47% of the ₹22,07,000 crore Budget Estimate. Source: BHARAT 2025.

Tax on Income and Wealth — income tax, corporate tax, wealth tax, gift tax (income tax/corporate tax most important for revenue). Personal Income Tax — imposed on individuals, HUFs, and other communities' total income; surcharges also imposed sometimes. Agricultural income in India is free from income tax. Corporate Tax — imposed on registered companies/corporations; rate is equal across all companies (rebates/exemptions vary).

Customs Duties — Centre imposes both import and export duty; a revenue source and a foreign-trade regulation tool. Import Duties — generally ad-valorem (percentage-based) in India. Export Duties — more important than Import Duties for revenue and foreign-trade regulation (as printed).

DOUBT Excise Duties — described as "the largest source of revenue for the Central Government" and imposed on production regardless of sale; taxable on all items except liquor, opium and other drugs. This statement is outdated post-GST (which subsumed excise for most goods) — transcribed as printed, not corrected.

Rupee Symbol — history (Lucent, in full)

On 15 July 2010, the Indian Rupee got its own symbol (like Dollar/Euro/Pound Sterling/Yen). The symbol is an amalgamation of Devanagari 'Ra' and Roman 'R' without the stem. On 5 March 2009, government announced a contest to design the symbol; the winning design, by D. Udaya Kumar (IIT Bombay postgraduate), was selected by the Union Cabinet on 15 July 2010. One Coin and One Rupee note belong to the 'Legal Tender Money' category.

M1 = Narrow Money; M3 = Broad Money (see also Batch 5's fuller M0-M4 table from Parmar Ch.8).

GST — Legislative Development & full mechanics (Lucent)

National-level GST first mooted 2006-07. GST Council constituted 2016, deliberating the Central GST Bill 2017, Integrated GST Bill 2017, GST (Compensation to States) Bill 2017. GST Constitutional Amendment Bill received Presidential assent 8 September 2016 as the 101st Amendment Act. Per Article 279A(1), GST Council was constituted 12 September 2016.

Three-tier levy: Central GST, State GST, Integrated GST. IGST mechanism ensures seamless input-tax-credit flow across states; IGST launched at midnight, 1 July 2017. GST subsumed: central excise duty, additional excise duties, excise duty under the Medicinal & Toilet Preparations (Excise Duties) Act 1955, Service Tax, Additional Customs Duty (Countervailing Duty), Special Additional Duty of Customs, Central Surcharges/Cesses on goods/services supply. IGST levied on inter-state transactions; coverage = all goods/services except alcoholic liquor for human consumption; petroleum/petroleum products deferred until a date notified on GST Council's recommendation.
