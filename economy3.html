ECONOMY — Budget, Taxation & Fiscal System

Batch 3 of 10 · Sources: Parmar Ch.4 (Budget and Taxation) + Lucent §10 (Fiscal System) + §12 (Tax System/GST) · PYQ buckets "04 Indian Tax System" (15 Qs) + "05 Public Finance" (57 Qs) · ★ = tested in a real past SSC paper

1Budget Basics

Budget = estimate of govt receipts & expenditure over a period, called the "Annual Financial Statement" ★ under Article 112 ★ (the word "Budget" itself is NOT in the Constitution — a repeatedly tested trap). Presented by the Finance Minister; laid before Parliament for the Financial Year, 1 April – 31 March ★ (not calendar year, not Jan-Dec, not July-June). Since 2017, presented on 1 February (earlier: last working day of Feb) so the process completes before the new FY starts.

HOOK First Budget: R.K. Shanmukham Chetty; 2nd: John Mathai. Most budgets: Morarji Desai (10, record) > P. Chidambaram/Nirmala Sitharaman (9 each) > Pranab Mukherjee/Yashwant Sinha (8). Indira Gandhi = 1st female FM (temporary charge). Gender Budgeting: 2005-06; Child Budgeting: 2008-09 ★.
HOOK Railway Budget: separated from General Budget in 1921 (Acworth Committee); merged back in 2016 (Bibek Debroy Committee), effective 2017-18 ★ — ending a colonial practice since 1924. Budget 2017-18 reforms ★: (1) presentation date advanced to 1 Feb, (2) Railway Budget merged with General Budget, (3) plan/non-plan expenditure classification abolished.

3Revenue vs Capital (Receipts & Expenditure)

Revenue Receipts ★

Do NOT create liability or reduce assets; non-redeemable. E.g. tax revenue, PSU dividends ★, interest on loans ★ — income tax/GST/dividends are all revenue receipts ★ since they carry no repayment burden.

Revenue Expenditure ★

Neither creates assets nor reduces liability. E.g. salaries, pensions, interest payments ★ (largest non-plan component), subsidies, defence expenditure (= "committed expenditure", little scope to cut ★). Grants given to states for asset creation are STILL Revenue Expenditure ★ (Centre's own assets/liabilities unaffected, even though the recipient creates an asset).

Capital Receipts ★

EITHER create a liability OR reduce government assets. E.g. loans/borrowings (creates liability, incl. loan from RBI ★), disinvestment/PSU-share sale (reduces assets ★), recovery of loans, small savings.

Capital Expenditure ★

Creates assets or reduces liabilities. E.g. machinery purchase ★, infrastructure, loan repayment. A PYQ statement pair confirms: capital expenditure DOES create assets (Statement I "does not create assets" is FALSE) and machinery acquisition IS capital expenditure (Statement II TRUE) ★.

TRAP Sale of PSU shares/disinvestment ★ = capital receipt (reduces govt assets) — NOT a revenue receipt, and it does NOT create a liability itself, it reduces assets. Loan from RBI = capital receipt because it creates a future liability ★. Capital Account of the budget = records changes in govt's assets/liabilities (capital receipts + capital expenditure) ★.

4Deficits — the full ladder

DeficitFormulaMeaning
Revenue Deficit ★Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts ★Only current-account transactions; also called "dis-saving" ★ — govt borrows for consumption, no asset created, future interest burden ↑. A concern because it signals borrowing for current spending, not productive investment ★
Fiscal Deficit ★Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Non-debt Capital Receipts) ★, i.e. excluding borrowingsTotal borrowing requirement of govt ★ — the key indicator of financial health; also called Budgetary/Budget Deficit ★ in some usages
Primary Deficit ★Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments (on previous loans) ★Borrowing needed to meet current expenses, excluding past-debt interest ★ — the macro indicator that isolates new borrowing from legacy interest burden ★
Effective Revenue DeficitRevenue Deficit − Grants for capital-asset creationRefines Revenue Deficit by excluding grants that indirectly build assets
DOUBT Lucent's own text (raw extraction, §10) gives "Primary Deficit = Revenue Deficit − Interest Payments" — an internal misprint contradicting its own correct definition ("fiscal deficit minus interest payments") one sentence earlier; the PYQ book's tested answer uses Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments, used here as correct.
HOOK Worked example ★: Total Revenue Receipts ₹50L cr, Total Expenditure ₹75L cr, Borrowings ₹20L cr → Fiscal Deficit = 75−50 = ₹25L cr ★ (borrowing of ₹20L cr covers only part of it — remainder from non-debt capital receipts).

Deficit Financing (concept by J.M. Keynes ★) = govt spends beyond revenue, gap filled by borrowing/printing money — objective: stimulate economic growth ★ (not reduce inflation, control black money, or shrink money supply — tested distractors). Continuous borrowing → accumulation of debt / debt trap ★ (new loans taken to repay old ones).

HOOK FRBM Act, 2003 ★: mandates fiscal deficit ≤ 3% of GDP ★. Objective: fiscal discipline, reduce govt borrowing/fiscal deficit, ensure inter-generational equity ★ (NOT to regulate private investment, increase fiscal deficit, or promote foreign trade). Escape clause ★ allows exceeding the target only for national security/war/calamity/agricultural crisis ★ (not "just any" reason). Finance Minister's FRBM role ★ = presenting the fiscal-policy statements to Parliament. Gross fiscal deficit hit ~8.4% (~10%) of GDP by 1990-91 ★ — the crisis that triggered 1991 reforms. Revenue deficit must be cut by 0.5% of GDP per year ★ under FRBM rules.

5Fiscal & Monetary Policy, Crowding Effects

HOOK Fiscal Policy ★ = govt spending + taxation to influence aggregate demand/economic activity (Finance Ministry) — distinct from Monetary Policy (repo rate, money supply — controlled by RBI ★). Govt infrastructure spending = fiscal policy ★; repo-rate adjustment = monetary policy ★.

Keynesian prescription for recession ★: increase spending, cut taxes (expansionary fiscal policy) to stimulate aggregate demand via the multiplier effect ★ — NOT raise interest rates or reduce spending. Expansionary fiscal policy is NOT characterised by a decrease in govt spending ★ (that's contractionary).

Crowding Out ★ = heavy govt borrowing → interest rates ↑ → private investment ↓ (credit becomes expensive). Crowding In = govt infra spending stimulates private investment (opposite effect). Post-1991 fiscal reforms linked state loans to fiscal-discipline performance — states had to adhere to fiscal targets ★.

6Direct & Indirect Taxes

Direct Tax ★Indirect Tax ★
Paid directly by the person/entity taxed; burden cannot be shifted; progressive ★ in nature (marginal rate > average rate). E.g. Income Tax, Corporate Tax, Wealth Tax, Gift Tax, Estate Duty. Administered by CBDT ★.Burden shifts to the final consumer; regressive ★ in nature (poor & rich pay the same tax on the same commodity — tax doesn't vary with income ★). E.g. GST, Customs Duty, Excise Duty.
TRAP "Paper Taxes" ★ = Wealth Tax, Gift Tax, Estate Duty — widely evaded, negligible revenue, "significance only on paper." Corporate Tax is a DIRECT tax ★ (levied on company profits) — a repeatedly tested distractor against GST/Excise/Customs (all indirect). GST replaced VAT/Service Tax/Excise ★ (implemented 1 July 2017).
HOOK Progressive taxation ★ = tax rate rises with income (most countries, incl. India's Income Tax — social-justice rationale, wealthy pay more). Regressive = rate falls as income rises (burdens poor more). Proportional = flat rate for all. GST's main objective ★: uniform tax across states ("One Nation, One Tax") — not a new direct tax, not to target luxury goods specifically.
CommitteeYearRemit

Vijay Kelkar ★2002-03 ★Task Force on fiscal reforms (direct & indirect taxes) — simplified tax administration, broadened tax base, paved way for FRBM Act & GST roadmap ★

HOOK CENVAT ★ (Central Value Added Tax, PYQ-book-only — not in Parmar/Lucent, see A.2) — excise duty rationalised to a single 16% rate ★ via the 2000-01/2001-02 Union Budgets, to cut the cascading tax-on-tax effect and prep for GST. Cascading Tax = tax on already-taxed value. Surcharge and Cess ★ = levied by Centre, NOT shared with states. Pigouvian Tax = tax on negative-externality activities. Laffer Curve = bell-shaped tax-rate vs revenue graph — beyond a point, higher tax rates reduce revenue.

7GST

Introduced via the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act ★ (assent 8 Sept 2016), effective 1 July 2017. GST Council (Art. 279A) = constitutional body, 33 members (2 Centre + 31 States/UTs), chaired by the Finance Minister. GST proposal first mooted 2006-07; GST Council constituted 2016.

8Finance Commission

Constitutional body, Article 280, constituted by the President every 5 years to recommend Centre-State tax-revenue distribution. 1st FC chairman: K.C. Neogi. 16th FC constituted 31 Dec 2023, chairman Arvind Panagariya, period 2026-31; recommends Vertical Devolution (states' collective share, retained at 41%) and Horizontal Devolution (Income Distance 42.5%, Population 17.5%, Area 10%, Forest & Ecology 10%, Demographic Performance 10%, GDP contribution 10% — new in 16th FC).

9Public Debt & Ways and Means Advances

WMA, 1997 = RBI mechanism for temporary Centre cash-flow mismatches (not a permanent finance source). Public debt = Internal (market loans, treasury bills, RBI securities) + External (World Bank, IMF, ADB, foreign govts). Operation Twist = RBI buys long-term govt securities + sells short-term ones, to lower long-term interest rates.
