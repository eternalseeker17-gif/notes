1Budget — Constitution, History & Records

  • Budget → approximation of revenue+expenses over a period; official constitutional term = "Annual Financial Statement" (AFS) ★ [B: "Budget" itself NOT in Article 112's text ★]
  • Article 112 → govt must present AFS to Parliament; Article 202 → same requirement for States (state legislature)
  • AFS presented by → President to both Houses; prepared by → Dept of Economic Affairs (Min. of Finance); presented (read out) by → Finance Minister
  • AFS = estimated receipts+expenditure for FY (1 Apr–31 Mar) [B, reinforced]; every budget gives 3 figure-sets → actual (preceding yr), budget+revised (current yr), estimates (following yr) [Lucent]; based on figures of 2 previous years [Lucent]
  • First Budget → R.K. Shanmukham Chetty; Second → John Mathai
  • Most budgets presented: Morarji Desai=10 (record, 6 in a row) → P. Chidambaram=9 → Nirmala Sitharaman=9 (incl. 9 consecutive) → Pranab Mukherjee=8 → Yashwant Sinha=8
  • Gender Sensitive Budget → 2005-06 [also cross-ref Gender Budget Parts below]; Indira Gandhi = 1st (temporary) female FM
  • Child Budgeting → introduced 2008-09 [B ★, reinforced: financial-inclusion step after gender budgeting; covers schemes with ≥30% or 100% child benefit ★]
  • Railway Budget: Acworth Committee (1921) → recommended separation from General Budget; Bibek Debroy Committee (2016) → recommended merger back; merged from FY2017-18
HOOK Budget 2017-18 reforms (3 major) [Lucent]: (1) presentation date advanced to 1 Feb ★ (from last working day of Feb) → avoids Vote-on-Account, single Appropriation Bill passed before FY starts; (2) Railway Budget merged with General Budget → ended colonial practice since 1924; (3) plan/non-plan expenditure classification abolished.

2Budget Functions & Basic Concepts

  • Allocation Function → dividing resources between public/private goods
  • Redistribution Function → ↓income inequality via progressive taxation + welfare programs
  • Stabilisation Function → adjust spending/taxation → counter recession (↑spending) or inflation (↓spending)
  • Asset → economic-value resource owned/controlled for future benefit; eg gold, property
  • Liability → obligation to fulfill a debt/duty; eg loan repayment
TRAP Budget's main objective [B] ★ → allocate public funds/limited resources across sectors (education/health/defence) for growth+welfare — NOT "increase unemployment," "decrease taxes," or "promote private sector" (tested distractors).

Revenue Budget = another name for the Revenue Account [B] → shows day-to-day, recurring, no asset/liability change.

4Taxation — Direct, Indirect, Types

Direct TaxIndirect Tax
Directly payable, cannot be passed on, progressive, marginal rate>average rate; eg income/wealth/gift/capital-gain/corporate taxIndirectly payable, CAN be passed on, regressive/proportional; eg GST, excise duty (petrol/diesel/ATF), customs duty
TRAP Indirect tax = regressive ★ [B] because it's based on commodity price, not income → rich+poor pay the SAME tax amount for the same good (NOT "varies with income"/"only wealthy"/"only corporations"/"equal impact across income groups" — tested distractors ★). Progressive taxation ★ = most countries' income-tax system, for social justice (wealthy pay more, poor's burden reduced) ★.

Paper Tax → significant only on paper, no real revenue yield; eg wealth tax, gift tax, estate duty (not levied now) [B ★: called "paper tax" because widely evaded, generates little revenue, admin significance limited to paperwork ★].

Laffer's Curve → graph of tax rate vs govt revenue; ↑tax beyond a point → ↓revenue (discourages investment/consumption/work effort); bell-shaped.

CBDT administers direct taxes (Income Tax etc.) — cross-ref §12 below for full CBDT detail.

5Goods and Services Tax (GST)

  • Introduced via 101st Constitutional Amendment Act; effective 1 July 2017 ★ [B: replaced VAT/Service Tax/Excise ★]
  • Article 279A → GST Council = constitutional body, 33 members (2 Centre+31 States/UTs), Chairman=FM
  • Cascading Tax (Tax on Tax) → tax levied at every stage without credit for tax already paid → inflates final price
  • Legislative timeline: first mooted 2006-07 → GST Council constituted 2016 (Art 279A(1), 12 Sept 2016) → 101st Amendment presidential assent 8 Sept 2016 → IGST launched midnight 1 July 2017
  • 3-tier levy: Central GST + State GST + Integrated GST; IGST = seamless input-tax-credit flow across states
  • Coverage: all goods/services EXCEPT alcoholic liquor (human consumption); petroleum products excluded until GST Council notifies a date
HOOK GST's purpose [B] ★ = "One Nation, One Tax" — uniform tax system across states, simple/transparent structure, easier inter-state trade (NOT "new direct tax," "more duty on luxury goods," or "differentiate goods vs services" — tested distractors ★). Subsumed 17+ taxes/cesses.

HOOK CENVAT ★ [B]: introduced via Union Budgets 2000-01/2001-02 → multiple excise rates collapsed to a single rate of 16% ★ (2001-02) → objective: simplify tax, reduce cascading, pave way for GST. Entertainment tax + Entry tax ★ → historically STATE-levied (NOT Central) ★, both now subsumed into GST — a tested "which was Central" trap where the answer is NEITHER.

Customs duty = indirect tax on imports+exports, Customs Act 1962; most exports exempt (promote exports); imports get Basic Customs Duty (BCD)+cesses (protect domestic industry).

6Other Taxes & Externalities

  • Surcharge → additional tax ON income tax, above an income threshold
  • Cess → tax on tax liability, for a specific purpose
  • Surcharge+Cess → levied by Centre, NOT shared with states
  • Pigouvian Tax → tax on negative-externality-causing activities, to reduce the externality
  • Externality → indirect cost/benefit to an uninvolved third party from another's activity; positive or negative

8FRBM Act, 2003

  • Enacted 2003, for fiscal discipline/responsible financial management
  • Original aim: limit fiscal deficit to ~3% of GDP ★, prudent public-debt management [B: mandates fiscal deficit ≤3% of GDP ★]
  • FRBM Review Committee → formed May 2016, headed by N.K. Singh
  • NK Singh Committee recommendations: Debt-GDP target 60% (40% Centre+20% States); Fiscal deficit target 2.5% of GDP by 2023 (medium term); Escape Clause = deviate up to 0.5% of GDP in exceptional situations ★ [B: grounds = national security, war, national calamity, severe agriculture situation ★]
HOOK 3 mandatory documents with AFS ★ [Parmar+B, both confirm]: (1) Medium Term Fiscal Policy Statement (3-yr rolling target); (2) Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement (govt's financial goals/methods); (3) Macroeconomic Framework Statement (GDP growth overview) — NOT the Monetary Policy Statement ★ (that's RBI's own, independent document). FM's role = present these to Parliament ★.

FRBM also mandates revenue-deficit reduction by 0.5% of GDP every year ★ [B]. Objective: control govt expenditure+fiscal deficit, inter-generational equity, long-term financial stability, ↓public debt/borrowing. Post-1991: Centre linked state loans to states' fiscal-discipline performance → states had to adhere to fiscal targets [B].

TRAP Gross fiscal deficit reached ~8.4% (≈10% rounded) of GDP by 1990-91 ★ [B] → forced the 1991 liberalisation reforms.

9Crowding In/Out, Budget Types & Deficit Financing

  • Crowding In → govt spending (infra/public investment) → stimulates private investment → ↑overall economic activity
  • Crowding Out ★ → ↑govt borrowing/expenditure → ↑interest rates → ↓private investment (govt competes for funds) [B ★: NOT competition increase/export decline/inflation-from-printing — tested distractors ★]
  • Balanced Budget (Receipt=Expenditure) | Surplus Budget (Receipt>Expenditure) | Deficit Budget (Expenditure>Receipt)
  • Deficit Financing (concept by J.M. Keynes) → govt expenditure>revenue, gap financed via new money creation or borrowing [B: objective = stimulate/accelerate economic growth via ↑public investment — NOT reduce inflation/control black money/decrease money supply ★]
  • Debt Financing → raise funds via borrowing (banks/FIs/bonds), repay with interest
  • Equity Financing → raise funds via issuing shares, investors become owners, share profits via dividends, no repayment obligation
HOOK Keynes ★ [B, reinforced]: during recession, most effective tool = ↑govt expenditure (creates demand/employment via multiplier effect) ★ → "expansionary fiscal policy" = ↓taxes + ↑govt spending ★ — NOT characterised by ↓govt spending (a tested "NOT" question ★). Fiscal Policy's primary objective ★ = influence aggregate demand/economic activity via govt spending+taxation (NOT money supply/interest rates=monetary policy; NOT trade/BoP/stock market ★). Fiscal-policy example = govt spending on infrastructure (repo rate/forex/money-supply = monetary policy, not fiscal ★).

12Fiscal System, Revenue Sources & Public Debt

  • Fiscal System → state's management of revenue+capital expenditure finances (revenue-raising, borrowing, spending)
  • Fiscal Policy → use of taxation+public expenditure+public-debt management for specified objectives
  • Centre's revenue: Tax revenue (income/expenditure taxes; property/capital-transaction taxes; commodity/services taxes) + Non-tax revenue (currency/coinage/mint; interest receipts/dividends; other)
  • State's revenue: state tax revenue (land revenue/stamp/registration/estate duty), share in central taxes, income from state enterprises/services
  • Centre's expenditure: Plan (agri/rural dev/irrigation/flood control/energy/industry/transport/comm/S&T/environment) + Non-plan (interest payments/defence/subsidies/general services)
  • State's expenditure: Non-Development + Development Expenditure
Internal DebtExternal Debt
Open-market loans, compensation/prize bonds, treasury bills (91/182/364-day + 14-day for states only) to RBI/banks, cash management bills, special RBI securities, compensation/other bonds, non-negotiable non-interest rupee securities to int'l FIs, market-stabilization-scheme securities (↓gold/import dependency)World Bank/IMF/ADB/foreign govts; Centre doesn't borrow directly from int'l capital markets — borrows via multilateral/bilateral ODA channels

13Annual Financial Statement — Structure & Related Bills

  • AFS kept in 3 parts: Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund, Public Account
  • Consolidated Fund → all revenues+loans raised+loan-recovery receipts; all expenditure from it; needs Parliament authorisation to withdraw
  • Contingency Fund → imprest at President's disposal for urgent unforeseen expenditure pending Parliament authorisation; corpus (from India 2023, effective April 2021) = ₹30,000 crore; amount recouped after subsequent Parliamentary approval
  • Public Account → govt acts as banker (provident funds, small savings, deposits); no Parliamentary authorisation needed for these payments
  • Budget = Revenue Budget (current receipts/expenditure — taxation, PSU surplus) + Capital Budget (capital receipts/expenditure — domestic/foreign loans, loan repayments, foreign aid)
  • Demands for Grants → Consolidated-Fund expenditure estimates voted by Lok Sabha, per Article 113; presented alongside AFS
HOOK Finance Bill ★ → a Money Bill (Article 110); presented with AFS; details tax imposition/abolition/alteration [B ★: role = tax changes/new tax proposals/rate amendments/tax-rule changes, needs both Houses' approval; also relates to Article 117, regulates financial matters beyond a plain Money Bill ★]. Appropriation Bill ★ → separately empowers withdrawal from Consolidated Fund for voted Demands-for-Grants amounts + charged expenditure; Article 114(3) — no withdrawal without such enacted law ★.

Constitution (80th Amendment) Act, 2000 (retrospective from 1 Apr 1996): most Union-List taxes (excl. Art 268/269 duties, Art 271 surcharges, specific cesses) collected by Centre, distributed Centre-States per President's order on FC recommendation. Union tax revenue sources: Customs, Union Excise, Service Tax, Corporate+Income Tax; Non-tax: interest receipts, dividends/profits, fines, regulatory/licence/user charges.
