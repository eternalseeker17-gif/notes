1Inflation — Definition, Fisher's Theory & Seigniorage

Inflation = increase in the general price level of goods/services. Purchasing power decreases. During inflation, the debtor/borrower benefits more than the lender ★ — repaying a fixed-interest loan later means repaying money worth less than when borrowed. Real Interest Rate = Nominal Interest Rate − Inflation.

HOOK Fisher's Quantity Theory — Irving Fisher also gave the "Money Illusion" concept. Explains the relationship between money supply, velocity of money, price level and transactions: MV = PT (M=Money Supply, V=Velocity of Money, P=Price Level, T=Transactions). ↑M (holding V,T constant) → ↑P → inflation, and purchasing power falls. Changes in money supply directly impact inflation.
TypeRange
Creeping3-4%
Walking4-10%
Running10-20%
Galloping20-100%
Hyperinflation>100%

Inflationary Gap — defined twice in the source: (1) when demand exceeds supply, Actual GDP exceeds Potential GDP; (2) the excess of aggregate demand over aggregate supply at full-employment level, causing upward price pressure — both transcribed, not reconciled.

Seigniorage = profit earned by the central bank from printing/issuing currency = Face Value − Production Cost.

2Causes & Measurement of Inflation

Demand-Pull Inflation ★Cost-Push Inflation
Caused by ↑demand (demand-side); can result from strong growth, ↑govt spending, ↑exports; can be short-term. "Too much money chasing too few goods" ★Caused by ↑production costs (supply-side): ↑wages, raw-material prices, indirect taxes; can be long-term — increase in cost of any factor of production/input cost

WPI and CPI are used to measure inflation. Inflation Rate Formula: [(Current Year Index − Previous Year Index)/Previous Year Index] × 100.

IndexMeasuresPublished byBase YearFrequency
WPIAverage price change at wholesale level; reflects prices before reaching consumersOffice of Economic Adviser (OEA), Ministry of Commerce & Industry2022-23 (previously 2011-12)Monthly (14th of each month)
CPI ★Average price change at consumer/retail level — cost of living for consumers, most accurate to gauge inflation's impact on the common man ★NSO, MoSPI2023-24 (Parmar p.266) / 2024 (Parmar p.267, internally inconsistent)Monthly (usually 12th)

WPI weights: Manufactured goods 64.2%, Primary articles 22.6%, Fuel & power 13.15%. WPI = 100 × (cost of fixed basket, current year / cost of fixed basket, base year). CPI weights: Food & Beverages 45.8% (highest), Housing 10%, Fuel & power 6.8%. CPI = 100 × (cost of fixed basket of goods/services, current year / base year).

CPI variantBase Year
CPI for Industrial Workers (IW)2016 (released by Labour Bureau)
CPI for Agricultural Labourer (AL)2019 (Labour Bureau)
CPI for Rural Labourer (RL)2019 (Labour Bureau)

Producer Price Index (PPI) — measures average change in selling prices received by domestic producers; unlike CPI (buyer's perspective), PPI is the seller's perspective; captures "factory gate" price changes before tax/distribution costs are added; a leading indicator since cost increases here are usually passed to consumers later.

Monetary Policy Framework Agreement (MPFA) — signed between GoI & RBI, Feb 2015; mandates RBI to maintain price stability while keeping growth in mind, target CPI Inflation at (4 ± 2)%.

Core Inflation = inflation measure excluding food and energy prices, calculated using CPI. Core Inflation = Headline Inflation − Volatile Inflation.

Index of Industrial Production (IIP) — measures growth of industrial production over time; Base Year 2022-23; published by NSO, MoSPI. Eight core industries contribute 40% to IIP, in decreasing order: Refinery Products → Electricity → Steel → Coal → Crude Oil → Natural Gas → Cement → Fertilizers.

HOOK Decline in core inflation globally in 2024 ★ mainly due to a drop in international commodity prices ★ (energy/metals/agri-input costs fell, easing "imported inflation") — not export-demand or fiscal-deficit factors.
HOOK Abhijit Sen Committee ★ reviewed/reformed the WPI base year and methodology; report submitted in 2008 ★, recommended base-year shift from 1993-94 to 2004-05.

3Inflation/Deflation Dynamics — full glossary

TermDefinition
DisinflationDecrease in the rate of inflation while prices may still be rising; measured by change in inflation rate year-on-year. Cause: slowdown in money supply via tighter monetary policy. Eg. progression 10%→8%→7%→5%.
DeflationGeneral decrease in price of goods/services; purchasing power increases. Cause: reduced money supply, reduced investment, or lower production.
Stagflation ★Period of high inflation + low economic growth (Stagnation + Inflation). Leads directly to high unemployment. Cause: production of goods/services slows or declines.
ShrinkflationProduct price stays the same but quantity/size is reduced — effectively raises price per unit.
Bottleneck InflationProduction/supply restricted at a particular stage of production/distribution, causing slowdown, shortages and price rise.
SkewflationPrices of essential goods (esp. food) rise sharply while non-essential goods' prices stay stable/rise slowly — an uneven/skewed inflation pattern.
ReflationDeliberate govt policy to raise economic activity/prices via ↑money supply, ↑govt expenditure, or ↓taxes — usually to recover from recession.
Imported InflationInflation from rising prices of imported goods/raw materials, raising the domestic price level.
Inflation TargetThe specific inflation rate a central bank aims to maintain via monetary policy, for price stability.

Phillips Curve = graph of the relationship between unemployment and inflation — downward-sloping; inflation and unemployment have an inverse relationship (high inflation/low unemployment at one end, low inflation/high unemployment at the other).

4Fiscal/Monetary Response to Recession

HOOK Keynesian prescription for a recession ★ (tested repeatedly): increase government spending, cut taxes ★ — "expansionary fiscal policy," stimulates aggregate demand to compensate for weak private investment. NOT raise interest rates, promote imports, or reduce spending.

5Unemployment — Definition & Measurement (NSSO/NSO methods)

Unemployment = a person actively searching for a job but unable to find work. NSSO uses three methods to measure it:

ApproachReference periodDetail
Usual Status (US)Last 365 daysIdentifies long-term/chronic unemployment. Two components: Principal Status (PS) — activity occupying most time in the last 365 days (majority-time criterion); Subsidiary Status (SS) — ≥30 days' additional economic activity apart from the principal one. Together: Usual Status (PS+SS).
Current Weekly Status (CWS)Last 1 weekIn labour force for the last week; ≥1 hr work on any 1 day → Employed.
Current Daily Status (CDS)Per dayMeasures the intensity of unemployment. In labour force for 1 day: >4hrs work = 100% engaged; 1-4hrs = 50% engaged; <1hr = unemployed.

LFPR (Labour Force Participation Rate) = % of persons in the labour force (working or available for work) in the population. WPR (Worker Population Ratio) = % employed persons in the population. UR (Unemployment Rate) = % unemployed among the labour force.

7Lucent §7 — Unemployment (fuller conceptual treatment) & Migration

Colloquially, anyone not gainfully employed is "unemployed" — but voluntary vs involuntary unemployment are distinct; involuntary unemployment = able/willing workers seeking jobs at prevailing wage but unable to get one. In developing economies like India, unemployment is not (in the Keynesian sense) a result of demand deficiency, but of shortage of capital equipment/complementary resources — unemployment in India is structural in nature, due to lack of productive capacity/resources.

Type (Lucent's own list, overlapping/adding to Parmar's)Meaning

FrictionalTemporary — new industries drive out old ones, workers switch to better jobs
Open unemploymentPeople with no work at all, though able/willing
SeasonalFarmers/farm labourers unemployed during non-crop seasons (e.g. between harvest and next sowing)
Educated unemployedMainly urban — educated persons unable to find work
Under-employment (Disguised)Person contributes less to production than they're capable of. Eg: an engineer working as a clerk
Compulsory unemploymentLabour ready to work at the current rate but unable to get work

During the Ninth Plan, 3.6 crore fresh unemployed persons began seeking employment. Before NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission, unemployment data was collected via the 'Lakadawala Formula' (effective 11 March 1997); before that, via NSSO surveys. NSSO's unemployment concepts: General status (unemployed >1 year — long-term), Weekly-unemployment (no work for even 1 hour in a week), Daily unemployment (considered the best concept of unemployment). Main reasons for unemployment in India: slow economic development, population explosion, outdated technique, improper education system, limited effect of government planning.

8Migration & Destination-Region Challenges

HOOK Unskilled rural-to-urban migration ★ → migrant labourers forced into low-wage informal-sector work without social security → exploitation of migrant labour + informal-sector growth ★ = the tested "challenge for destination regions" (not increased per-capita income or reduced unemployment — tested distractors). Push factors (poverty, unemployment, war) force people out; Pull factors (jobs, opportunities) draw them in ★ — both operate together in real migration decisions.

MGNREGS — full detail

National Rural Employment Guarantee Bill passed 7 September 2005; Presidential assent same year (became NREGA). Provides ≥100 days employment ★ to one able-bodied person per rural household per year. Act came into force 2 February 2006; initially 200 districts, now covers all 740 rural districts. Renamed MGNREGA on 2 October 2009 ★. Cost-sharing: Centre 75%, State 25% for material/skilled-semi-skilled wages. FY2025-26 wage hikes: 2.33%-7.48% (₹7-₹26 range); highest ₹26 in Haryana (wage touching ₹400).

Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 — features, in full: (1) every rural household has a right to ≥100 days guaranteed employment/year for ≥1 adult member, as casual manual labour at statutory minimum wage, paid within 7 days of the work-week; (2) work provided within 15 days of demand, within 5 km distance; (3) if work isn't provided in time, a daily unemployment allowance of ≥1/3 minimum wage is paid; (4) workers get medical treatment/hospitalisation for work injury + daily allowance ≥half statutory minimum wage; death/disability → ex-gratia payment to legal heirs per Workmen Compensation Act; (5) 5% of wages may be deducted for welfare schemes (health/accident insurance, survivor/maternity benefits, social security); (6) strict penalties for non-compliance; (7) all accounts/records open to public scrutiny; (8) District Collector/CEO responsible at district level; (9) Gram Sabha monitors the Gram Panchayat via social audit.
