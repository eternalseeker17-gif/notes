1Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

Recommended by the Royal Commission on Indian Currency & Finance (chaired by Hilton Young, 1926). Established under the RBI Act, 1934; began operations 1 April 1935, Calcutta; nationalised 1 Jan 1949; HQ moved to Mumbai, 1937. First Governor: Sir Osborne Smith; first Indian Governor: Sir C.D. Deshmukh.

HOOK RBI Governors' chronology ★: Dr. Y.V. Reddy (2003-08) → Dr. D. Subbarao (2008-13) → [Raghuram Rajan/Urjit Patel gap] → Dr. Urjit R. Patel (2016-18) → [Shaktikanta Das] → Shri Sanjay Malhotra (2024-present) ★ — a tested chronological-ordering question.

RBI = "Bank of Banks" (regulator under Banking Regulation Act 1949: licensing, CRR/SLR, repo rate) + Monetary Authority + Currency printer (except ₹1 note/coins — Ministry of Finance) + "Lender of Last Resort".

3Banking History & Nationalisation

Bank of Hindustan (1770, Calcutta) = first bank. Presidency Banks: Calcutta (1806), Bombay (1840), Madras (1843) → merged into Imperial Bank of India (1921) → nationalised & renamed State Bank of India, 1955. First Indian-owned bank: Allahabad Bank (1865).

HOOK Bank nationalisation, 19 July 1969 ★ — 14 major private banks (deposits >₹50 crore) nationalised, to extend rural banking, ensure agri/small-industry credit, reduce private monopoly ★. A further 6 banks nationalised in 1980 (deposits >₹200 crore).

Lead Bank Scheme ★ — launched by RBI, December 1969 ★, based on Nariman Committee recommendations ★; assigns one bank per district (Area Approach) to lead rural credit/banking expansion.

Regional Rural Banks (RRB): RRB Act 1976; ownership split Centre 50% / sponsor commercial bank 35% / State 15%; supervised by NABARD, regulated by RBI; first RRB = Pratham Grameen Bank, 2 Oct 1975, Moradabad.

4Types of Money & Motives for Holding It

TypeMeaning
Fiat Money ★Govt-declared legal tender, NOT backed by gold/silver ★ — modern currency systems run on trust + govt backing, not convertibility to physical commodities (a tested concept)
Fiduciary MoneyAccepted via mutual trust — cheques, tokens
Plastic MoneyDebit/credit/prepaid cards
Hot MoneyFunds moved quickly across borders/markets for short-term gain
HOOK Reasons to hold money ★: Transaction (daily spending), Precautionary (emergencies), Speculative (by J.M. Keynes — investment timing, e.g. buying stocks on a dip). Liquidity Preference Theory ★ = Keynes's theory of demand for money — interest rates determined by money demand/supply, not just savings/investment (a tested "another name" question). Cambridge Cash Balance Approach ★ (Marshall, Pigou, Keynes) — value of money tied to demand to hold cash balances, contrasted with Fisher's velocity-of-transactions approach ★ (a repeatedly tested "another name for X approach" pairing).
HOOK Speculative Demand for Money ∝ 1/Interest Rate — inverse relationship: rates ↑ → people invest in bonds, hold less cash; rates ↓ → returns low, hold more cash. Liquidity Trap ★ = rates so low, speculative money-demand curve is perfectly/infinitely elastic (horizontal) — monetary policy becomes ineffective (Keynes).

Paradox of Thrift (Keynesian) — individuals saving more in a downturn collectively reduces aggregate demand and total savings economy-wide.

5Reserve Requirements & Monetary Policy Tools

CRRSLR ★
Cash reserve with RBI; no interest earnedLiquid assets (cash/gold/govt securities) held by the bank itself ★; interest earned; mandatory reserve requirement of commercial banks
TRAP ↑CRR/SLR reduces banks' lending capacity ★ (they must set aside more, less left to lend) — NOT increase it (a tested Assertion-Reason flips this). Money Multiplier = 1/CRR ★ — inversely related to CRR/reserve requirements; cash held by the public (not deposited) is a "leakage" that also reduces the multiplier ★.
ToolMechanism
Repo Rate ★Rate at which RBI lends short-term to banks against govt securities as collateral ★ — ↑repo → costlier bank borrowing → ↓money supply → controls inflation ★
Reverse RepoRBI borrows from banks (banks park surplus funds), against govt securities
Bank RateRBI's long-term lending rate to banks, without collateral
MSFEmergency overnight borrowing by banks from RBI, above repo rate, using G-Secs within SLR quota
OMO ★RBI buys/sells govt securities in the open market to manage liquidity ★ (also called sterilization)

MPC (Monetary Policy Committee) — based on Urjit Patel Committee recommendations; 6 members (3 RBI incl. Governor as chair + 3 Govt); meets bi-monthly; decides repo rate/CRR changes.

HOOK Expansionary Monetary Policy ★ = central bank cuts rates to stimulate growth/spending during a slowdown (NOT contractionary/fiscal-stimulus/QE as separate labels — the exact tested term is "expansionary"). Debt Monetisation ★ = RBI directly buys govt bonds/securities to fund the fiscal deficit, printing new currency → raises money supply → often high inflation ★ (opposite of raising rates or cutting money supply).
HOOK Financial intermediaries (banks) transmit MPC's repo-rate changes to the real economy by adjusting their own lending/deposit rates ★ — a directly tested Assertion-Reason (both TRUE, R correctly explains A).
