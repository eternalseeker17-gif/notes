7Monetary Policy Tools — Quantitative & Qualitative

  • Quantitative: CRR, SLR, Bank Rate (long-term loan to banks, NO collateral), OMO (=sterilization: Outright Purchase[no repurchase agreement] + Repo["Rere"=Repurchase Obligation, banks borrow w/ G-Secs collateral] + Reverse Repo[RBI borrows from banks])
  • Repo + SDF (Special Depository Facility, no-collateral bank deposit w/ RBI) together = LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility)
  • MSF → emergency overnight bank borrowing from RBI, above repo rate; CRR/SLR-default penalty=3% above Bank Rate/day; uses G-Secs within SLR quota; only scheduled commercial banks
  • Base Rate → minimum rate below which banks can't lend
  • Interbank Lending Market/Call Money Rate → decided on RRR+MSF basis; short-term overnight interbank loans; limit=2% of NDTL
  • MCLR → introduced by RBI, April 2016; transparent/policy-responsive minimum lending rate
TRAP Money Multiplier(MM) = 1/CRR ★; NBFCs/NBFC-MFIs exempt from CRR/SLR; ↑CRR→↓MM (and vice versa) [B ★: inverse CRR-multiplier relationship ★; cash held by public = "leakage" reducing multiplier ★].

  • Qualitative: (1) Changing Margin Requirements(↑for non-essential, ↓for agri); (2) Consumer Credit Regulation(loan instalment/down-payment/repayment rules); (3) Moral Suasion(persuasion/implicit threats); (4) Credit Rationing(limits loans per bank/sector)

9Liquidity, Money Functions & Theories

  • Liquidity order: Cash>Gold>Cheque>Bond; Demand Deposit>Term Deposit
  • Barter limitations: Double Coincidence of Wants, Lack of Divisibility, Lack of Store of Value [B ★: money eliminates double-coincidence problem via "measure of value"+"medium of exchange" ★ — barter itself DECREASES efficiency, not increases, tested A-R]
  • Money functions: Primary=Medium of Exchange; Secondary=Medium of Value/Unit of Account→(a)Standard of Deferred Payment,(b)Store of Value(no intrinsic value, paper value from govt backing),(c)Portable/Durable
HOOK Liquidity Preference Theory = Keynes's money-demand theory — rates determined by money D/S not just savings/investment. Cambridge Cash Balance Approach ← Marshall, Pigou, Keynes → value of money ← demand to hold cash balances (not transaction velocity) [B ★: Cambridge approach's alt-name = "Cash Balance Approach" ★ vs Fisher's = velocity-of-transactions approach ★].

3 reasons to hold money: Transaction Motive(daily spending), Precautionary Motive(emergencies), Speculative Motive(by J.M.Keynes, investment timing). Paradox of Thrift(Keynesian) → individual ↑savings in downturn → collectively ↓aggregate demand+total savings economy-wide.

Speculative Demand for Money ∝ 1/Interest Rate → rates↑→bonds preferred→speculative demand↓; rates↓→more cash held→speculative demand↑; "interest = opportunity cost of holding money". Liquidity Trap → rates low, people hold cash not invest→monetary policy ineffective; horizontal LP-curve portion = infinitely/perfectly elastic money demand.
