1Poverty — Concept, Types & Measures

  • Poverty → lacking financial resources/essentials for basic living standard; causes: env/legal/social/econ/political
  • Types: Absolute(lacking food/water/shelter/clothing/healthcare/education) | Relative(income far below society avg) | Subjective(feeling poor regardless of income)
  • Poverty Gap Index → measures depth of poverty (extent below line, not just headcount)
  • World Poverty Clock → managed by World Data Lab, real-time global poverty estimates
  • India's poverty estimation → NITI Aayog task force + NSSO data (under MoSPI); BPL = income/spending below set minimum
  • Types of Poor: Usually Poor(normally BPL) | Churning Poor(in/out of poverty) | Occasionally Poor(normally above line)
  • Head Count Ratio(HCR) → %population BPL = poverty headcount index = No. of multidim. poor / Total population
  • ILO's 6 Basic Needs → Education, Food, Clothing, Shelter, Health Care, Safe Drinking Water
  • HDI → concept by Amartya Sen & Mahbub ul Haq, introduced 1990, score 0-1 → Life Expectancy Index(long/healthy life) + Education Index(knowledge) + GNI Index(GNI per capita PPP$, decent living)
HOOK MPI → introduced 2010 by UNDP+OPHI; 3 dimensions(Health-nutrition/child mortality; Education-schooling yrs/attendance; Living Standards-fuel/sanitation/water/electricity/housing/assets), 10 indicators. National MPI(NITI Aayog, Nov 2021) → 12 indicators(+antenatal care+bank account); declining since 2005-06; 24.82cr escaped multidim. poverty in 9yrs; UP=most lifted; Bihar=highest MPI, Kerala=lowest MPI.

5Balance of Payments — Structure

  • BoP → record of all economic transactions, country↔rest of world = Current Account + Capital Account
  • Current Account → Visible(physical goods/tangible) + Invisible(services/intangible) + Transfer Payments(unilateral, remittances/aid)
  • Capital Account → FDI/FPI + External Assistance/Deposits/Borrowings + Other Flows
  • India = top global remittance receiver; Mexico 2nd
  • FDI → long-term, ≥10% equity in foreign physical asset (eg Walmart-Flipkart) | FPI/FII → short-term financial-asset investment, <10% equity(stocks/bonds/MFs/NRI deposits)
  • FDI limit, defence sector(enhanced 2020) → 74% Automatic route, up to 100% Government route
  • Current Account Deficit → imports>exports; incl trade balance/net foreign income/foreign aid/net current transfers
  • Twin Deficit → simultaneous current account deficit + fiscal deficit

Current account surplus/deficit/balance ★ [B]: receipts>payments=surplus(net lender); payments>receipts=deficit; receipts=payments=balanced. ★ Statement pair ★ [B]: Imports=leakage from circular flow(↓aggregate demand, TRUE); Exports=injection into circular flow(↑aggregate demand, TRUE) — both correct.

61991 BoP Crisis & Reforms

  • BoP/currency crisis → can't pay essential imports/repay foreign debt; India 1991, PM P.V.Narasimha Rao, FM Manmohan Singh → LPG model response
  • India approached IMF + World Bank(via IBRD) for $7bn loan; conditions = SAP(privatisation/trade liberalisation/fiscal discipline/FDI opening)
  • Rupee devalued in 2 stages, July 1991, cumulative ~18-20% vs USD ★; objective = cheaper exports/discourage imports/boost forex inflow
  • Rupee fully convertible on Current Account → 1994(RBI, IMF Article VIII), 19 Aug 1994 specifically ★
  • Current Account Convertibility = trade-transaction freedom | Capital Account Convertibility = capital-transaction freedom (partially convertible in India, RBI-regulated)
  • Tarapore Committee → constituted 1997 by RBI(reviewed 2006), chair S.S.Tarapore → examined CAC feasibility; phased roadmap preconditions = low fiscal deficit/controlled inflation/strong banking(low NPAs)/stable financial system
HOOK 1991 crisis facts ★ [B, all PYQ-tested]: by June1991 India left with only $1.1bn forex = sufficient for just 2 weeks of imports(petroleum/essential goods) ★. Shortage = foreign exchange(not water/electricity/labour) ★. A-R: India approached IMF+WB 1991 for bailout(TRUE) — because forex declined to ~2-weeks-import level(TRUE, correct explanation) ★. Immediate measure = rupee devaluation(not import restriction/revaluation/fixed exchange rate) ★. Primary cause = govt's inability to repay foreign borrowings(fiscal deficit+forex debt+Gulf War oil prices+inflation) — NOT natural disaster/state-govt inefficiency/illegal migration ★. Primary reason for 1991 reforms = BoP crisis(not high GDP growth/overpopulation/agri stagnation) ★. A-R: devaluation improved export competitiveness(TRUE) — weaker rupee made Indian goods cheaper globally(TRUE, correct explanation) ★. Late-1980s: India approached IBRD(World Bank's main institution) for the crisis ★.

Currency conversion needed to buy from US ★ [B]: Rupee not legal tender in US, seller only accepts Dollar — based on int'l trade rule requiring reserve currency for foreign-goods import. FII role in reforms ★ [B]: invest in India's financial markets(stock/bond) → linked Indian markets globally post-1991, ↑capital availability.

7Forex Reserves, IMF/World Bank & Bretton Woods

  • Forex reserves → central bank/monetary-authority assets, import cover+shock protection; 4 components = FCA, Gold, SDRs("Paper Gold"), Reserve Tranche Position in IMF(higher quota=higher voting power)
  • SDR basket currencies: USD, Euro, Yen, Yuan, Pound
  • Bretton Woods Conference 1944(UN Monetary & Financial Conference) → 44 nations, 730 delegates → created IMF + World Bank("Twin Orgs"), HQ Washington DC; also created IBRD → system collapsed 1971(US suspended gold convertibility)
  • IMF: established 27 Dec 1945(Bretton Woods) → began functioning 1 Mar 1947; 188 members(2017); promotes financial/economic cooperation+world trade
  • IBRD → established 1945; with IFC+IDA+MIGA = "World Bank"; capital investment+long-term balanced development
  • India ranks 8th in IMF voting power; Top IMF borrowers: Argentina, Egypt, Ukraine, Pakistan, Ecuador
  • Washington Consensus → 10 free-market policy prescriptions(John Williamson, 1989) for crisis-prone developing nations(Latin America); promoted by IMF/WB/US Treasury: privatisation/deregulation/trade liberalisation/fiscal discipline
  • IMF major reports: World Economic Outlook, Global Financial Stability Report, Fiscal Monitor, External Sector Report

Statement check ★ [B]: unlimited convertibility+fixed-price conversion instability→↓currency credibility(TRUE); int'l monetary system's PRIMARY objective = ensure global payment/exchange stability(NOT regulate internal fiscal/tax policy — statement FALSE) ★. Low forex reserves critical problem ★ [B]: India could barely afford essential imports for a very short time(not investment attractiveness/printing/travel-related distractors) ★.

8Exchange Rate Mechanisms & Currency Terms

  • NOSTRO("our") → domestic bank's foreign-currency a/c in foreign bank | VOSTRO("your") → foreign bank's domestic-currency a/c in domestic bank
  • FERA(1973) → simplify forex system; violation=criminal offence; abolished(rigid rules hindered growth) → replaced by FEMA(1999) → enhances forex reserves, promotes trade/payments; violation=civil offence(decriminalised)
  • Depreciation(market fall)/Appreciation(market rise) → under Floating Exchange Rate | Devaluation(govt-induced fall)/Revaluation(govt-induced rise) → under Fixed regime
  • Exchange rate evolution: 1928-48 pegged to Pound Sterling → post-1948(IMF system) pegged to USD(Fixed) → 1975 delinked from Pound → 1992 LERMS(dual rate) → 1993 fully Market-Determined/Floating
  • NEER → weighted avg vs trade-weighted currency basket, no inflation adjustment | REER → NEER adjusted for inflation, real external competitiveness
  • Currency types: Hard(stable, USD/Euro), Soft(weak/unstable), Hot(moves quickly for short-term profit), Heated(high demand/speculation), Cheap(depreciated), Dear(appreciated)
  • Lorenz Curve → income/wealth distribution graph, measures inequality; Gini Coefficient 0(perfect equality)-1(perfect inequality)

9Tax Evasion, Money Laundering & Anti-Avoidance

  • BEPS → Base Erosion(shell cos across jurisdictions, ↓taxable profit in high-tax jurisdiction) + Profit Shifting(moving eroded profits to low/no-tax jurisdiction eg Cayman Islands/Bahamas)
  • Round Tripping → domestic black money sent abroad → brought back as FDI via shell cos/tax havens to appear legitimate
  • FATF → HQ Paris, founded July 1989(G7); global AML/terror-financing standards
  • 3 Stages of Money Laundering: Placement(illegal funds enter system) → Layering(concealing origin) → Integration(funds absorbed into economy)
  • Shell Company → paper-only entity, holds assets/routes funds/hides ownership
  • MAT → Section 115JB, Income Tax Act 1961; min tax when normal liability <15% of "book profit"(per Companies Act 2013 calc); applies to all domestic/foreign cos; prevents "zero-tax" profitable cos

11Trade History — Colonial to 1990s (Lucent §15)

  • Ancient Indian trade highly developed; British East India Co(1600) → trade favoured India till 1757
  • British imposed heavy cloth duty to destroy Indian industry; post-Industrial-Revolution Britain flooded markets w/ cheap goods → colonisation
  • British cotton monopoly → costlier raw material for Indian weavers → handloom business ruined by 1813; late-19th century power-machine textile industries emerged
  • Pre-independence trade = colonial principles | Post-independence = inward-looking/import-replacement policy
  • India's world-trade share: 1.78%(1950) → 0.6%(1995, persisting through 1997-98 per ES2001-02) → FY2019-20: 18th-largest exporter(1.7% share, INDIA2021), 10th-largest importer(2.6% share, INDIA2020)
