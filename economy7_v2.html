1Pre-Independence Planning Proposals

  • Visvesvaraya Plan(1934) → "Planned Economy for India 1934" → India's earliest blueprint for planned dev, first systematic economic-planning proposal
  • National Planning Committee(1938) → formed by S.C.Bose, 1st Chairman J.L.Nehru → first attempt at a National Plan (AICC also demanded planning, 1938)
  • Bombay Plan(1944) → "A Plan of Economic Development for India" → by leading capitalists incl J.R.D.Tata(Parmar)/"8 industrialists"(Lucent) → published 2 parts(1944+1945)
  • Gandhian Plan(1944) → "decentralised economic structure"/"self-contained villages"
  • Peoples Plan → by M.N.Roy, Chairman Post-War Reconstruction Committee of Indian Trade Union(labour leader)
  • Sarvodaya Plan(1950) → by Jaiprakash Narayan(founded Congress Socialist Party 1934) → "first blueprint" of planned dev of Independent India
  • 5-yr-plan concept borrowed from USSR ← Joseph Stalin introduced it 1928 to industrialize Soviet Union
  • Post-independence(1947): planning committee under Nehru → Planning Commission constituted March 1950 → format of 1st FYP prepared 1951
DOUBT Internal Lucent conflicts (as printed, not corrected): Peoples Plan dated 1945(Parmar) vs April 1944(Lucent p.260); Gandhian Plan author "Sriman Narayan"(Lucent p.260) vs "Acharya S.N. 2Planning Commission & NITI Aayog

  • Planning Commission → set up 15 March 1950 ★, non-constitutional advisory body; first Chairman=Jawaharlal Nehru(PM=ex-officio Chair); first Deputy Chairman=Gulzarilal Nanda ★ [B]
  • Pranab Mukherjee → served as Vice Chairman of Planning Commission
  • 4 goals of FYPs: Growth(GDP), Modernisation(new tech), Self-reliance(inward-looking trade), Equity(dev of all) → "Dependency on foreign aid" was NEVER a goal ★ [B]
  • Perspective Plan → long-term(20yr) plan FYPs build toward; ~75% of population dependent on agriculture at Independence
  • Planning Commission dissolved Aug 2014 → NITI Aayog constituted 1 Jan 2015 (cooperative federalism, "bottom-up" approach ★) [B]; NITI Aayog chairman=PM of India; policy think tank, non-constitutional; NITI=National Institution for Transforming India

7Types of Planning & Models of Development

  • Imperative Planning → Central authority decides every aspect(socialist economies) | Indicative Planning → State sets broad parameters only, adopted in India since 8th FYP | Perspective Planning → 15-20yr long-term, via FYPs+Annual Plans | Rolling Plan → 3 steps(current-yr plan + 3-5yr revised-annually plan + 10-20yr perspective plan) | Core Plan → Planning Commission uses state revenue estimates to set Annual Plan expenditure heads, prevents fund diversion
  • Nehru-Mahalanobis Model → driving force since 2nd FYP till the 1980s; "Growth with social justice"; State controlled commanding heights via public sector
  • Gandhian Model → raise material+cultural level of masses; scientific agri dev+cottage/village industries(Nehru prioritized heavy industry instead)
  • LPG Model → introduced 1991 by FM Manmohan Singh; bigger private-sector role; large FDI envisaged; export-led growth(vs earlier import substitution); reduces State's role

8Industrial Policy Resolutions — 1948 & 1956

  • 1st IPR(1948) → first official industrial policy of independent India; led by Dr.Shyama Prasad Mukherjee(Industry&Commerce Minister); presented in Parliament 1948 ★; declared India a Mixed Economy; public+private coexistence consensus ★
  • 4 categories(1948): Strategic(Public-Railways/defence/atomic energy), Basic/Key(Public-cum-Private-coal/steel/aircraft/telecom), Regulated(Controlled Private-sugar/cement/textiles), Other(Private&Cooperative-consumer goods/SSI); started License Raj; laid foundation for public-sector dominance
  • Karve Committee(Village&Small-Scale Industries Committee) → est. by Planning Commission 1955, chair Dattatreya Gopal Karve; promoted rural industrialisation via SSIs during 2nd FYP; recommended labour-intensive small-scale over large-scale capital-intensive industry ★
HOOK 2nd IPR(1956) → "Economic Constitution of India" ★; objective="growth with equity" ★/equitable income-wealth distribution; "socialistic pattern of society"; public sector given "commanding heights"(doctrine coined by Vladimir Lenin,1922); 3 Schedules ★: A(17 industries-Public Monopoly: aviation/atomic energy/railways/arms/steel), B(12 industries-Public&Private: aluminium/machine tools/chemicals), C(All Other-Private: consumer goods/textiles/automobiles); based on Mahalanobis Model; encouraged backward-region industries; licensing objective=state control per planned priorities+prevent monopolies ★.

Joint Sector → partnership btwn public+private; Dutta Committee(1973) recommended Joint Venture Sector in Indian Industry ★.

9Industrial Policy Resolutions — 1977, 1980 & MRTP/FERA

  • 3rd IPR(1977, extension of 1956) → introduced by Morarji Desai(Janata Party); emphasised SSI growth; "Tiny Unit" concept(investment <₹1 lakh); restricted MNCs(self-reliance); decentralisation focus, introduced District Industries Centres concept(shift industry from big cities to small towns) ★
  • 4th IPR(1980, Indira Gandhi Govt) → shifted focus back to heavy industries(Parmar)
  • Encouraged foreign tech transfer/investment(FERA Act 1973, printed under this policy despite date mismatch ⚠); MRTP Act(1969) amended to remove asset-threshold limits; tiny-unit limit ↑to ₹2 lakhs
  • MRTP Act → firms w/ assets above threshold(₹100cr since1985) classified MRTP, restricted case-by-case entry; scrapped by IPR1991; replaced by Competition Act 2002 → Competition Commission of India(CCI) established ★
DOUBT IPR 1980 emphasis: Parmar says it shifted focus back to heavy industries; Lucent frames it as "influenced by federalism … concession to agriculture-based industries" — differing framing, both preserved.

10IPR 1991 — Economic Liberalisation

  • IPR 1991 announced 24 July 1991 ★; shift from inward-looking(closed) to outward-oriented(open) economy; PM P.V.Narasimha Rao, reforms led by FM Manmohan Singh, during 1991 BoP crisis ★
  • Before-1991: License Raj, Public Sector Dominance, High import barriers, Restricted FDI, Import substitution → Result: low competition/inefficiency/slow growth
  • LPG terms: Liberalisation(↓govt regulation, frees markets); Privatisation(shift PSU ownership/mgmt to private); DIPAM(Dept of Investment&Public Asset Mgmt, ex-Dept of Disinvestment, Min. of Finance) manages disinvestment
  • Key Measures(1991): End of License Raj(except few industries), Disinvestment in PSUs, FDI liberalisation(automatic route), ↓public-sector monopoly, greater market-force role
  • Macroeconomic Reforms: Rupee Devaluation 2 stages(9% 1July+11% 2July) ★, cumulative~18-20%; Fiscal Deficit(1990-91)~10% of GDP; Anti-Dumping Duty introduced; HDFC Bank=first private-sector bank after liberalisation
  • After-1991: ↑FDI inflows; private-sector growth(↑competition/efficiency); shift ISI→EOI(Export-Oriented Industrialization); global integration; BPO-sector emergence; Managed Floating Exchange Rate adopted
  • Structural changes: services GDP-share ↑~39%(1991-92)→55-60%(recent); agriculture GDP-share ↓~30%(1990-91)→15-18%; manufacturing largely stagnated
HOOK IPR1991 specifics(Lucent): abolished all industrial licensing except security/strategic/social/safety/environmental/hazardous/elitist-consumption industries; FDI up to 51% equity in high-priority industries approved; public-sector-reserved industries(17 since1956)→reduced to 4 areas(arms&ammunition/atomic energy/mineral oils per1953 order/railways); mandatory convertibility clause(banks' loan→equity option) abolished; location policy liberalised(no approval outside million-plus cities except compulsory-licensing industries); Phased Manufacturing Programmes abolished for new projects. PYQ confirms focus=(I)easier foreign investment + (II)exit policy for sick private units(smooth exit w/o hurting labour welfare) — both correct ★.

Trade-policy reforms(1991) ★: ↓import tariffs on capital goods, abolished quantitative restrictions+licensing(→easier foreign-tech access, globally competitive mfg); removed import quotas, drastically ↓export duties, abolished import licensing(except hazardous/environmentally-sensitive goods). LERMS(Liberalised Exchange Rate Management System) → dual exchange-rate system, introduced 1992(March1992 per PYQ) ★, part-market-rate/part-RBI-fixed-rate conversion. FDI limit in foreign private banks progressively ↑to 74% ★. Washington Consensus does NOT include export subsidies — advocates trade liberalisation+eliminating subsidies for market competition ★(tested distractor).

12Disinvestment & Privatisation

  • Types(Parmar): Minority(govt retains >51%+mgmt control), Majority(govt stake <51%), Strategic(sale ≥50%+mgmt-control transfer), Complete Privatization/Full Exit(100% equity sold)
  • Lucent framing: disinvestment beyond 51% govt stake=majority-private control; Privatisation=ownership+mgmt change; Disinvestment=wider term(stake dilution→ownership transfer)
  • Disinvestment Commission → constituted August 1996; first Chairman=G.V.Ramakrishna ★(printed "Ramkrishna" in one instance); suggested core-sector max 49% disinvestment, non-core up to 74%; shares to small investors/employees; greater PSE autonomy suggested
  • VRS("Golden Hand Shake Scheme") → full compensation for voluntarily-exiting employees, ↓PSE financial burden
  • Minority sale ★ = govt sells <51% shares to private/public, mgmt/control stays with govt | Strategic Disinvestment/Privatisation ★ = govt sells >51% stake + transfers mgmt control
  • Disinvestment objectives ★: ↓fiscal deficit, reallocate resources(health/education), ↑PSU efficiency → leads to REDUCTION in govt's financial assets(not increase/liability/tax-revenue)

A-R ★ [B]: disinvestment widely criticised in India(TRUE, lack of social security+strategic assets sold cheap) — but NOT because it increased PSU market monopoly(Reason FALSE; actual objective=promote privatisation/competition).

13New Economic Policy 1991 & Reforms

  • NEP: reform production pattern, adopt new tech, use full capacity; first devised/implemented 1985(PM Rajiv Gandhi); second wave 1991(PM Rao); 1991 trigger=Gulf War+BoP problem
  • 3 NEP objectives: Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation; reform sectors: Fiscal/Monetary/Value(Price) Fixation/Foreign/Industrial/Foreign Investment/Business/Public Sector Policy
  • First Generation Reforms=macro-level stabilisation(industry liberalisation/deregulation, financial-sector reforms, taxation reforms); Second Generation Reforms=micro-level structural(labour/land/capital-market reforms, expenditure/power-sector reforms)
  • Poverty declined 36%(1993)→26%(end of 10th plan, Lucent); inequality increased per World Bank Report 1999-2000
HOOK Large-scale reforms initiated 24 July 1991 ★, PM Rao+FM Manmohan Singh, "Rao-Manmohan model"/"New Economic Policy", based on LPG model ★; objective=overcome BoP crisis, dismantle License Raj, make economy globally competitive ★. 1991 crisis root cause ★: external-debt+BoP crisis, forex sufficient for only ~2 weeks of imports; immediate cause=oil-price rise from Gulf War+rising foreign-loan interest → govt forced to mortgage gold ★. Stabilisation short-term objective ★: control inflation(~17% then)+improve BoP; rupee devalued ~18-20% in 2 stages July1991 to boost exports+attract forex(NOT reduce inflation/↑foreign aid, distractors) ★. IMF loan ~$7bn conditional on LPG reforms; IBRD/World Bank gave policy guidance+funds for structural reforms ★.

NOT a goal of 1991 reforms ★: expanding public-sector control(reforms aimed to LIMIT public-sector role, ↑private participation — repeated distractor across multiple PYQs). Trade-policy reforms ↓tariffs+removed licensing on capital-good imports → globally competitive mfg, easier machinery import ★.

14Liberalisation, Privatisation & Globalisation — PYQ Deep-Dive

  • Liberalisation objective ★: remove barriers to entry/growth of firms(License Raj restricted entrepreneurial freedom); ended "license-permit raj"(govt permission for nearly every business decision)
  • Economic liberalisation affects ALL 3 sectors(Primary/Secondary/Tertiary) ★ — ↑investment/competition/efficiency
  • A-R ★: 1991 reforms opened economy to foreign investors(TRUE) — but NOT because govt wanted to rapidly ↑domestic savings rate(Reason FALSE; real primary reason=BoP crisis+forex shortage)
  • Sectors excluded from licensing post-1991: alcohol/cigarettes/hazardous chemicals/industrial explosives kept compulsory-licensed(security/health/environment); textiles/fertilizers/chemicals EXEMPTED ★
  • "Streamlining of regulations" ★ = simplifying business rules to ↓red tape, promote EoDB
  • EoDB 'Enforcing Contracts' indicator ★ = time/cost/quality of judicial process for commercial-dispute resolution; 'Starting a Business' = 1 of 10 EoDB indicators, NOT weighted more than others ★
  • MCA21 ★ = e-governance(Ministry of Corporate Affairs) digitising company registration/compliance; SPICe+ integrated forms(name/registration/PAN/TAN together)
  • Financial-sector reforms based on Narasimham Committee recommendations ★ → allowed private-sector banks(ICICI/HDFC emerged); ↑FDI limit in banking
  • Globalisation opened economy to global markets, allowed MNC entry/investment ★; FDI=key globalisation component(capital/tech/mgmt skills)
TRAP Two A-R pairs frequently confused ★: (1) Globalisation ↑inequality in India(TRUE) — because growth unevenly distributed(urban-elite/service-sector benefit more)(Reason TRUE, correct explanation). (2) Globalisation ↑outsourcing of services to India(TRUE) — but NOT because India offers "high-cost,low-skill" services(Reason FALSE; India actually offers LOW-cost,HIGH-skill IT/software services).

WTO role ★: ensures fair/rule-based trade(NOT military/human-rights/migration control); established 1 Jan1995, HQ Geneva; handles dispute settlement, ↑market access for developing countries via MFN+national-treatment. Open economy ★: trade in goods/services allows wider consumer/producer choice. Tax reforms post-1991 ★: lowered income-tax rates — based on Laffer Curve(excessive rates→↑evasion; moderate rates→↑compliance&revenue). Agri-sector decline post-1991 ★: shift to export-oriented cash crops + reduced public investment in irrigation/power/rural infra. WTO membership(1995, founding member) ★ → multilateral framework integrating India w/ global trade, ↓import barriers, ↑global-value-chain participation. FDI=key component of Globalisation pillar ★(not Liberalisation/Privatisation/Nationalisation). LPG: "P"=Privatisation(PSU ownership/mgmt/control transfer to private) ★; L=Liberalisation(relax rules); G=Globalisation(integrate w/ world economy). Second Generation Reforms(late1990s-2000s) ↑FDI limits in automobiles/telecom/insurance → significant ↑FDI inflow ★. License Raj negative consequence ★: entry barriers, stifled innovation, inefficiency; Hazari Committee(1960s) called it "Corporate Imperialism" ★; eliminated largely via 1991 Reforms. Key 1991 reform addressing PSU inefficiency = disinvestment+strategic privatisation+abolishing compulsory licensing(↑private entry/competition) ★. A-R ★: NEP1991 led to more efficient/competitive markets(TRUE) — because Privatisation ↓govt control+↑PSE productivity(Reason TRUE, correct explanation).

15Public Sector, SSIs & MSMEs

  • PSE(def) → all undertakings owned by govt/public; 7 Public Sector objectives: rapid econ dev via infra, generate dev finance, redistribute income/wealth, create employment, encourage SSI/ancillary industries, promote exports+import substitution, promote balanced regional dev
  • Sick Industrial unit(def) → company(≥5yrs) w/ accumulated losses≥net worth OR ≥50% of avg net worth(preceding 4FYs) + failed debt repayment 3 consecutive quarters despite written demand

16Industry Classification — PYQ Deep-Dive

  • Karve Committee(1955) supported rural industrialisation via SSI, NOT large-scale capital-intensive urban mfg(A-R: A true, R false) ★
  • SSI classification → earlier only investment-based; from 2020, composite(investment+turnover) criteria ★
  • PSU ownership/mgmt held entirely/primarily by govt; primary objective NOT just profit but social welfare+strategic-sector development(defence/railways/atomic energy) ★
  • Cooperative sector industries → owned/operated on mutual cooperation, profit&loss shared among members, objective=socio-economic welfare not just profit ★
  • India Industrial Land Bank(IILB) → GIS-based platform for industrial-park/land/infra info; managed by DPIIT(Commerce&Industry Ministry), NOT Ministry of Mines ★
  • IBC 2016 → resolves failed-business debt disputes within 180-330 days; accelerates NPA recovery, revives debt-ridden cos, promotes EoDB ★
  • Capital goods industry = produces machines/tools/equipment for producing other goods(not direct consumption); "backbone of economy" ★
ClassificationBasis / Examples
Heavy Industries ★capital-intensive, bulky/heavy raw materials(iron ore/coking coal/limestone); produce machinery/cranes/large vehicles — eg shipbuilding, automobile, iron&steel
Light Industries ★light raw materials → consumer goods, low capital/energy — eg sewing machines, paintbrushes, watches, electric bulbs
Basic Industriesbackbone-of-economy, supply raw materials to other sectors — eg aluminium smelting→automobile/construction/electricity
Consumer Goods Industries ★direct-consumption goods — toothpaste/soap/clothes/shoes (distinct from basic/capital-goods: iron&steel/aluminium/copper smelting)
Agro-based Industries ★raw material from agri produce — cotton textile(cotton), sugar(sugarcane) — distinct from mineral-based(cement/petrochemicals/steel/aluminium)

Sugar industry ★: seasonal(sugarcane harvest-bound); sucrose content decreases during transport(mills sited near cane fields, "weight-losing raw material"); major states=UP/Maharashtra/Karnataka/TN(Bihar in some variants); India=2nd-largest sugar producer after Brazil, 1st in gur&khandsari. Sindri Fertilizer Plant ★ = first major public-sector industrial unit of independent India, est.1951, Dhanbad(Jharkhand), sited near Jharia coalfield. Fertilizer industry ★: India NOT self-sufficient in potash(fully import-dependent); Urea=most-produced/demanded nitrogenous fertilizer; production ↑ after Green Revolution; Gujarat&Kerala are LEADING(not marginal) fertilizer producers — tested false-statement combo. Chennai="Detroit of India" ★(auto hub: Hyundai/Ford/BMW/TVS/Ashok Leyland) — NOT a petrochemical hub(that's Jamnagar/Dahej, Gujarat). Rising urban non-manual tertiary employment ★ = signals "emerging service economy" transition.

SEZ policy launched India April2000(China-model), 8 existing EPZs converted; SEZ Act2005 effective 10Feb2006 ★. FIPB constituted under Industrial Policy 1991 to facilitate/regulate FDI; abolished 2017(EoDB) ★. National Infrastructure Pipeline(NIP): announced 2019-20, launch considered 2020; ₹111 lakh crore infra investment(2020-2025) target ★. DPIIT=nodal dept for mfg/industrial promotion(+Gati Shakti Logistics Division) ★; Deepak Parekh Committee(2008)=affordable-housing financing ★. Industrial-Revolution-era Britain ★: 18th-century capital(trade/colonial expansion/agri revolution) reinvested by merchants/industrialists(not feudal aristocracy) into industrial tech/factories — power shifted from feudal aristocracy to trader/industrialist/capitalist class.
