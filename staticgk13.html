1Orbits & Satellite Mechanics

ItemFact
Low Earth Orbit (LEO) speed ★At 800 km altitude (Sun-Synchronous Orbit), a satellite travels at ≈7.5 km/s ★; speed decreases as altitude increases
Minimum satellite altitude ★Satellites generally don't fly below 180 km ★ — denser atmosphere below causes drag/burn-up
Geostationary Orbit (GEO) ★Altitude ≈35,786 km; orbital speed ≈3.07 km/s ★, matching Earth's rotation (24-hour period) — hence appears stationary over one location ★, used for continuous cloud/weather monitoring and as a "TV tower in the sky" at ≈36,000 km ★
Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) ★Satellites in MEO do follow defined orbital paths (NOT arbitrary) ★; MEO (≈2,000–35,786 km) is used by navigation satellites — GPS, GLONASS, Galileo ★
International Space Station (ISS) ★Completes ≈16 revolutions/day ★ (one orbit every ≈90 minutes, Low Earth Orbit)
Sun-Synchronous Orbit (SSO)A satellite crosses the same local solar time on every pass — used for remote-sensing/Earth-observation satellites like NISAR
Artificial satellite examples ★Sputnik-1 (world's first, USSR, 1957) and India's GSAT-1 are both correct examples of artificial satellites ★
Remote sensing satellites ★Used for both drought assessment AND crop-yield estimation ★ (via rainfall/soil-moisture/NDVI data) — not one or the other
Launch vehicle physics ★A satellite launch vehicle works on Newton's Third Law of Motion ★ (action-reaction; expelled gases push the rocket up)

2ISRO Launch Vehicles

VehicleFact
PSLV ★= Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle ★; ISRO's most reliable "workhorse" — launched Chandrayaan-1 (2008), Mangalyaan (2013), Aditya-L1 (2023), 104 satellites in one mission (PSLV-C37, 15 Feb 2017 — a world record then) ★; versatile — places satellites in Polar/SSO, GTO, and LEO ★; no cryogenic stage (that's GSLV/LVM3) ★
GSLV ★"G" = Geosynchronous ★; places satellites into geosynchronous orbit; GSLV-D1 (first developmental flight, Sriharikota, 18 April 2001) had three stages ★
GSLV Mk-III / LVM3 ★Now officially called LVM3 ★ (Launch Vehicle Mark-3); India's heavy-lift vehicle; used for Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3, and the human-rated HLVM3 (Human-rated LVM3) for Gaganyaan ★
SSLV ★Small Satellite Launch Vehicle — distinct because it enables on-demand, low-cost launches ★, assembled in ≈72 hours vs. 60–90 days for PSLV/GSLV
RLV-TD / RLV-LEX ★Reusable Launch Vehicle-Technology Demonstrator, ISRO's reusable-rocket testbed; "Pushpak" vehicle released from a Chinook helicopter and performed autonomous runway landing (RLV-LEX-02, cross-range corrections; RLV-LEX3, 23 June 2024, landed at >320 km/h) ★; RLV programme phases: HEX, LEX, REX, SPEX ★
Next Generation Launch Vehicle (NGLV) ★Also called 'Soorya'/'Soham' ★; approved by the Union Cabinet to support the Bharatiya Antariksh Station and a crewed Moon landing by 2040 ★; key feature = reusable booster with a semi-cryogenic (LOX-Methane) engine ★ — cleaner combustion, lower refurbishment cost than kerosene
Cryogenic engine — why hydrogen ★Hydrogen has the highest energy density per kilogram ★ — more energy for less weight
Third launch pad, Sriharikota ★Proposed to handle rising launch frequency for SSLV, Gaganyaan, and commercial payloads ★ (not a data-relay hub)

3Moon Missions

MissionFact
Chandrayaan-1 ★India's first lunar mission, launched 22 Oct 2008 (PSLV-C11, Sriharikota) ★; its M3 (Moon Mineralogy Mapper) payload (NASA) discovered water/hydroxyl on the lunar surface ★ — first confirmation of lunar hydration ★, overturning the "completely dry Moon" view
Chandrayaan-2 ★Launched 2019 by GSLV Mk-III/LVM3 ★; orbiter + lander Vikram + rover Pragyan ★ (in that order); achieved detailed lunar South Pole mapping via the orbiter after the lander's hard landing
Chandrayaan-3 ★Key objective = demonstrate safe soft landing and rover operations ★ on the Moon (3 goals: soft landing, rover ops, in-situ experiments); lander Vikram added a Lander Hazard Detection and Avoidance Camera ★ (LHDAC) vs. Chandrayaan-2; discovered a buried crater near the South Pole, ≈160 km diameter and ≈4.4 km deep ★; the RAMBHA-LP payload studies lunar plasma ★ (electron density/temperature); the Laser Retroreflector Array (LRA) onboard was supplied by NASA ★
LUPEX / Chandrayaan-4 ★Lunar Polar Exploration Mission — joint ISRO-JAXA mission ★ to study water-ice quantity/quality/purity at the Moon's permanently-dark South Pole regions

4Mars & Venus Missions

MissionFact
Mangalyaan / Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM) ★India's first interplanetary mission ★, launched 5 Nov 2013 via PSLV-C25 ★ from Satish Dhawan Space Centre; entered Martian orbit 24 Sept 2014 — India became the first country to reach Mars on its first attempt ★ (also the first mission in the world to do so on a first attempt)
Shukrayaan-1 (Venus Orbiter Mission) ★Aims to map Venus's surface and study its atmosphere ★, particularly airglow and atmospheric dust ★; planned launch March 2028 ★

5Solar Missions — Aditya-L1

ItemFact
Aditya-L1 — basics ★India's first solar mission ★, launched 2 Sept 2023 (PSLV-C57, Sriharikota) ★; placed in a halo orbit around the Sun-Earth Lagrange Point L1 ★ (≈1.5 million km from Earth, ≈1% of the Earth-Sun distance); took 127 days ★ to reach L1, entered halo orbit 6 Jan 2024; studies the Sun's photosphere, chromosphere, and corona ★ using visual/X-ray/UV instruments — NOT radar waves ★; does not orbit the Sun directly, and is not near the Moon ★
Why L1? ★Lagrange points are where Sun-Earth gravity balances, letting a satellite stay stable with minimal fuel ★ — enables continuous, uninterrupted solar observation without eclipses
Aditya-L1 payloads (7 total) ★VELC = corona imaging ★; SUIT = UV imaging of photosphere/chromosphere ★; SOLEXS = soft X-ray spectrometer (solar flares/corona heating) ★; ASPEX (incl. SWIS, Solar Wind Ion Spectrometer) = solar wind protons/alpha particles ★
Space-weather significance ★Aditya-L1's corona/solar-wind data improves space-weather forecasting to protect satellites and power systems ★ from solar storms/CMEs
Other solar missions (non-ISRO) ★Parker Solar Probe = NASA ★, first spacecraft into solar orbit; SOHO (Solar and Heliospheric Observatory) = joint ESA+NASA, Dec 1995, studies the Sun's interior/corona/solar wind from L1 ★

6Human Spaceflight — Gaganyaan & Bharatiya Antariksh Station

ItemFact
Gaganyaan ★India's first human spaceflight mission; sends 3 astronauts ★ ("Vyomanauts"/"Gagan-yatris" — Prashant Nair, Ajit Krishnan, Angad Pratap, Shubhanshu Shukla named) into LEO at 400 km ★ altitude for 3 days, brought back safely; uses the human-rated HLVM3 ★; would make India the 4th country with human spaceflight capability after USA, Russia, China ★
Crew Escape System (CES) ★Pulls the crew module away from the rocket to safety during a launch emergency ★; validated by the TV-D1 test flight (abort motor separated the crew module, parachute landing in the Bay of Bengal) ★
Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS) ★India's first space station, in LEO ★, proposed altitude ≈400–450 km ★; first module (BAS-1) targeted for 2028 ★, fully operational by 2035 ★ (per ISRO's "Vision 2047"); significance = microgravity research, biotech experiments, long-duration spaceflight, Earth observation for disaster monitoring ★
Vision 2047 targets ★BAS in orbit by 2035; first Indian on the Moon (indigenous technology) by 2040 ★
Space docking (SpaDeX) ★India became the 4th country to demonstrate space docking (after USA, Russia, China) ★, via the SpaDeX mission, achieved 16 January 2025 ★; crucial for BAS construction and future lunar-sample-return missions ★

7ISRO Satellites & Earth-Observation

SatelliteFact
INSAT ★Indian National Satellite System, est. 1983 ★; multipurpose — TV/radio broadcast, telecom, weather forecasting, disaster warning ★; largest domestic communication satellite system in the Asia-Pacific region; INSAT-3D/3DS = advanced meteorological satellites (cyclone/rainfall/climate monitoring) ★
INCOSPAR ★"N" = National ★ (Indian National Committee for Space Research); constituted 1962 under Vikram Sarabhai; predecessor of ISRO
Cartosat-3 ★High-resolution (25 cm) civilian remote-sensing satellite, launched 27 Nov 2019 via PSLV-C47 ★ — monitors agriculture/forestry/coastal resources ★
AstroSat ★India's first dedicated multi-wavelength space observatory ★ (UV, optical, and X-ray simultaneously — unique among world satellites), launched Sept 2015 via PSLV-C30 ★; studies black holes, neutron stars, active galactic nuclei
NISAR ★Joint NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar mission ★; uses radar imaging ★ with dual-frequency L-band (NASA) and S-band (ISRO) ★; orbits in Sun-synchronous/polar orbit ★; revisits every 12 days ★ for land-deformation/earthquake monitoring; GS2 stage flagged off March 2025 ★
GSAT-9 (South Asia Satellite) ★Gift to SAARC nations under PM Modi's "Neighbourhood First" policy ★, launched via GSLV-F09, 5 May 2017
GSAT-20 (GSAT-N2) ★Delivers high-speed Ka-band broadband across India, esp. Andaman-Nicobar/Lakshadweep ★ (≈48 Gbps)
NVS-02 ★Navigation satellite strengthening NavIC (NavIC = Navigation with Indian Constellation) ★ — precise Position/Velocity/Time services
DISHA ★Twin-satellite mission studying Earth's upper atmosphere — Ionosphere and Thermosphere ★, and space weather
SCATSAT-1 ★Scatterometer for ocean winds/weather/cyclone prediction ★, launched 26 Sept 2016 (PSLV-C35)
Trishna ★Joint ISRO-France(CNES) ★ satellite for high-resolution thermal infrared imaging (Land Surface Temperature)
Megha-Tropiques ★Joint India-France mission studying the water cycle/energy exchange in tropical regions ★ — helps predict monsoons
IDRSS ★Indian Data Relay Satellite System — enables real-time communication with LEO satellites/astronauts ★
XPoSat ★Studies black holes/neutron stars via X-ray polarimetry; POLIX measures X-ray polarization (8–30 keV) ★; XSPECT does X-ray spectroscopy/timing (not polarization) ★
DS-SAR (PSLV-C56, 30 July 2023) ★SAR imaging satellite for Singapore's government/clients ★

8ISRO — Institutions, Programme & Private Sector

ItemFact
ISRO ★Established 1969 ★, HQ Bengaluru ★
Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC-SHAR) ★ISRO's main spaceport, Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh ★ — houses launch pads, Vehicle Assembly Building, Mission Control Centre; all major missions (Chandrayaan, Mangalyaan, Aditya-L1) launched from here
Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC) ★Kerala; designs and builds launch vehicles ★ (PSLV, GSLV, LVM3) — not a launch site
YUVIKA ★ISRO's "Young Scientist Programme" ("Catch Them Young") ★ — introduces school students to space science
Space Situational Awareness (SSA) ★Monitors space debris and protects Indian space assets ★, via radar/optical telescope network
Skyroot Aerospace ★First private Indian company to launch a rocket into space ★ (18 Nov 2022, Sriharikota)
RHUMI-1 ★India's first reusable hybrid rocket ★, by Tamil Nadu startups Space Zone India/Martin Group ★, launched 24 Aug 2024 from Mahabalipuram via a mobile launcher
QpiAI ★Indian startup that built India's first full-stack 25-qubit quantum computer ★, under the National Quantum Mission
MATSYA 6000 ★India's first manned scientific submersible, part of the Deep Ocean Mission ★ — carries 3 people to 6,000 m depth

9Other Space Agencies & Missions

Mission/AgencyFact
Europa Clipper ★NASA mission to study Jupiter's moon Europa ★ — possible subsurface ocean
CryoSat ★Operated by ESA ★ — studies polar ice thickness/climate change
James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) ★Observes in near/mid-infrared ★ (statement 1 correct); located at Sun-Earth L2 ★ (NOT L1 — statement 2 in the tested set was actually false since it claimed L2 was correct but item framing tested distinguishing this); carries a coronagraph for exoplanet detection ★
Proba-3 (ESA) ★Launched via India's PSLV-C59, Oct 2024 ★ — tests precision "formation flying" between two satellites; a milestone in India's international space cooperation
Saturn's rings ★Per NASA, disappearing via "Ring Rain" — estimated to vanish in ≈100 million years ★ (based on Cassini mission data)

10Miscellaneous Science & Technology

ItemFact
Mission Mausam ★Enhances weather-forecasting capability AND develops high-resolution monsoon-prediction models ★ (both objectives correct)
Bharat Forecast System ★Adopted by IMD, 26 May 2025, developed by IITM Pune ★ (Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology)
Beginning of India's space age ★21 Nov 1963 ★ — first sounding rocket (Nike-Apache) launched from Thumba, near Thiruvananthapuram

Prachand ★India's first indigenous Light Combat Helicopter ★, by HAL, for high-altitude combat zones
