2Swaras, Saptak & Notation

7 basic Swaras (Sargam) ★: Sa(Shadja)-Re(Rishabha)-Ga(Gandhara)-Ma(Madhyama)-Pa(Panchama)-Dha(Dhaivata)-Ni(Nishada). Achal (fixed) swaras ★ = Sa & Pa only (always Shuddha, fixed pitch); Chal (movable) = Re/Ga/Ma/Dha/Ni (can be Komal/flat or Teevra/sharp) ★.

TermMeaning ★
Raga ★Melodic framework — ascending (Aroh) + descending (Avroh) + Samvadi notes ★
TalamMeasures the time cycle (rhythm) of music
AlapBeginning of a raga — slow expansion of notes without words
Bandish ★A structured/fixed pre-composed musical composition in Hindustani music ★ (also called "cheez"); foundation for Khayal/Thumri/Dadra/Tarana
Swaravali ★Sequence of the 7 notes, composed for structure/practice of a raga (NOT itself a raga type) ★
GamakaGraceful turn/curve/oscillation on a note; gliding between notes = Meend
HOOK Saptak notation ★: dot ABOVE note = Taar Saptak (high pitch) ★; dot BELOW = Mandra Saptak (low pitch); no dot = Madhya Saptak (middle pitch).
Jati (by note-count, Hindustani) ★Notes
Audav5 (pentatonic)
Shadav ★6 (hexatonic) — a 6-note raga ★
Sampurna7 (heptatonic)
Jati (by beat-count, Carnatic) ★Beats
Tishra3
Chaturashra4
Khanda ★5 — a laghu of 5 beats ★
Mishra7
Sankeerna9
HOOK Teentaal ★ = 16 beats (matras), 4 sections of 4; Taali (clap) on 1st/5th/13th beat, Khali (empty) on 9th beat ★ — the most popular tala for Khayal/Bandish singing. Composition "Guru Bin Kaise Gun Gaave" set to Teentaal (Raga Yaman) ★.
HOOK Raga Vivodha by Pandit Somnath defines a raga via two components ★: Devamaya Swarupa (ethos/spiritual form) and Nadamaya Swarupa / Swarlakshana (tonal structure — notes cluster, aroh-avroh, vadi-samvadi) ★.

The 10 Thaats were formalised by Pandit Vishnu Narayan Bhatkhande; each is a parent scale for classifying ragas.

HOOK Raga Bageshri ★ = second prahar of night, mood = separation/love (Shringar). Raga Hansadhwani ★ = first prahar of night, energetic/happy. Raga Bhairav ★ = morning/sunrise, peace/devotion — the classic "early morning, peaceful, meditative" answer. Raga Marwa ★ = evening/sunset, anxiety/restlessness/renunciation.
HOOK Raga Miyan ki Malhar ★ = credited to Tansen ★ (rainy-season raga, one of Akbar's Navratnas).

4Carnatic Forms & the Trinity

FormDescription ★
Varnam ★Sung at the BEGINNING of a concert, for practice ★ (NOT the end — a directly tested false statement)
Kriti ★Most important/highly structured Carnatic form ★, bound by raga+tala; 3 parts — Pallavi, Anupallavi, Charanam ★
Tillana ★Presented at the END of a concert/dance performance ★, fast tempo, rhythmic syllables (dhin, tana-dhina)
Alapana ★Improvised raga elaboration, NO fixed lyrics or rhythm/tala ★ — establishes form/mood of raga
Svarajati ★Pallavi+Anupallavi+Charanam structure, themes of devotion/love/valor; suitable for dance (e.g. Bharatanatyam) ★
SonataNOT a raga-based Indian form — it's Western classical, for instruments like piano ★
HOOK Trinity of Carnatic Music ★ = Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri (18th–19th century ★) — elevated the Kriti form to its highest artistic level ★. "Female Trinity" = M.S. Subbulakshmi, D.K. Pattammal, M.L. Vasanthakumari. Purandara Dasa = "Pitamaha (Grandfather) of Carnatic Music" ★; through the Haridasa Movement in Karnataka, composed devotional songs called Devara Nama ★ in simple Kannada, praising Vishnu/Vitthala. Tyagaraja's "Pancharatna Kritis" ★ = 5 compositions in praise of Rama, based on Adi Tala and 5 ragas (Nata, Gaula, Arabhi, Varali, Sri).
HOOK 72-Melakarta (parent raga) system ★ propounded by South Indian musician Venkatamakhin in Chaturdandi Prakashika ★ — ragas evolved from ancient Jatis ★ (NOT the reverse).

A.2 — PYQ-book-only facts (not verified against the raw extraction)

  • The specific mood/prahar table for Bageshri/Hansadhwani/Bhairav/Marwa (§3) is drawn from the PYQ book's own explanations, not printed as a single table in the raw extraction (which discusses these ragas separately under A.6–A.7).
  • "Sitar invented by Amir Khusrau in the 13th century (Veena+Tanpura)" is the PYQ book's claim; the raw extraction does not attribute Sitar's invention to anyone.
  • Dedh Satara, Pava, Mahuri, Muri, Vamsi, Bengbung, Talhkhuang and Narsingha state-specific instrument facts largely corroborate the raw extraction's state-wise folk-music table (A.12) but with additional descriptive detail (e.g. exact diameter, named communities) sourced only from the PYQ book.
  • The term "Swaravali" for the 7-note practice sequence is the PYQ book's own term — the raw extraction discusses the 7 Swaras (Sargam) but does not use this name.
  • The raga name "Gurjari" (vs. the raw extraction's "Gurjari Todi") and its full description (Gujarat association, Todi Thaat minus Pancham) are sourced entirely to the PYQ book's explanation — the raw extraction's Thaat table does not carry a raga by this exact name in its Todi row.

A.3 — Doubts carried forward from the raw extraction

Dhrupad's attribution to Miyan Tansen and Khayal's to Amir Khusrau (raw extraction A.2) are the book's own claims — other sources credit different originators; not resolved. Kamaicha described inconsistently as "bowed flute" (p.434) vs "string instrument" (p.438) within Parmar itself. Bharat Ratna ordering among musicians (Subbulakshmi/Ravi Shankar/Bismillah Khan) is printed inconsistently across Parmar's own pages — not reconciled.
