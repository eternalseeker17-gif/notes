1Olympics — Structure & History

IOC founded 1894 by Pierre de Coubertin (2nd President) & Demetrius Vikelas (1st President); HQ Lausanne. Motto: "Citius, Altius, Fortius – Communiter". First Summer Olympics 1896 Athens; first Winter 1924 Chamonix; first Paralympics 1960 Rome ★.

6Terms & Rules — Racket/Court/Combat Sports

SportKey facts ★
Badminton ★Match = best of 3 games to 21 points ★ (deuce win-by-2, cap 29-29); singles court 13.40m × 5.18m ★, doubles width 6.10m; fault = server stepping on/over boundary line ★; "Grand Slam" is a tennis term, not badminton ★; Prakash Padukone = first Indian to win All England Open (1980), also world No.1 that year ★; Kidambi Srikanth = first Indian male shuttler in a World Championship final (2021, silver) ★; 2019 India Open final in New Delhi, won by Viktor Axelsen over Kidambi Srikanth ★
Table Tennis ★Table 2.74m × 1.525m, net 15.25cm, ball 40mm diameter, 2.7g ★ (was 38mm before 2000) ★; game to 11 points, 2 consecutive serves per player ★ (switches every 2 points, alternates every point at 10-10) ★; Olympic debut 1988 Seoul ★
Basketball ★Invented by Dr. James Naismith, 1891, Springfield, USA ★; 5 players/team on court (12-player roster) ★; 3-point line at 6.75m internationally ★; shot clock 24 seconds ★; free-throw time limit: 5 sec (FIBA/int'l) vs 10 sec (NBA) ★; first Indian National Championship: 1934, New Delhi ★; BFI established 1950; Olympic debut 1936 Berlin ★; personal foul = illegal physical contact during play ★
Chess ★FIDE est. 1924, HQ Lausanne; board 8×8=64 squares ★; Knight moves in 'L' shape ★; Castling = King+Rook move together ★ (only if neither has moved, king not in check, path not attacked) ★; pawn promotes to any piece (Queen/Rook/Bishop/Knight) on reaching 8th rank, even if original queen remains on board ★; "Zugzwang" = compulsion to move into a worse position ★; "en passant" = capturing an opponent pawn that just moved 2 squares, only immediately after that move ★; D Gukesh = youngest undisputed World Champion (2024, beat Ding Liren, Singapore) ★; Chennai = "Chess Capital of India" ★
Kabaddi ★Modern rules formulated 1918, printed 1923, used in Baroda tournament ★ (first rules actually framed in Maharashtra, 1915–1920 per Stenographer PYQ — note both "Maharashtra origin" and "1918 formulation" are tested) ★; All India Kabaddi Federation formed 1950, Jaipur; team = 12 (7 on court + 5 substitutes) ★; raid time limit 30 seconds ★; match = 2 halves of 20 min (men) ★; losing breath while chanting "Kabaddi" (the "Cant") = raider out ★; Pro Kabaddi League founded 2014, Jaipur Pink Panthers won 1st season ★
Kho-Kho ★Team = 12 (9 on field) ★; playing field rectangular ★; pole height 1.20–1.25m, diameter 9cm ★; senior match duration 37 minutes (2 innings) ★ — NOT 48 minutes (tested false statement)
Judo ★Invented by Jigoro Kano, Japan, 1882 ★, from ancient Jiu-Jitsu ★; Olympic debut 1964 Tokyo (men), women's events from 1992 Barcelona ★; mat/area called Tatami, square shape ★; hall = Dojo; Ippon = highest score, ends match immediately ★; Hansoku-make = serious penalty ending match ★; Indian judokas: Tulika Maan (silver, +78kg, 2022 CWG) ★, Kapil Parmar (India's first Paralympic judo medal, bronze 60kg J1, Paris 2024) ★
Squash ★Short line 4.26m (singles)/4.57m (doubles) from back wall ★; "Tin" = 48cm strip on front wall ★
Tennis ★"Grand Slam" term ★; Leander Paes + Mahesh Bhupathi — first Indian pair to reach all 4 Grand Slam finals in a year; fifth-set tiebreak at 6-6 in Grand Slam men's singles (10-point super tiebreak) ★
Handball ★Free-throw line = 9m line, 3m outside goal area, dashed with 15cm segments/15cm gaps ★; team = 12 (7 play + 5 substitute) ★; pulling/striking ball from opponent's hands = foul ★
Water Polo ★7 players in water (6 field + 1 goalkeeper) ★; terms: Center Forward (attacker near goal, 2-4m lines), Driver (swims toward goal without ball), Center Back (defends main attacker), Dead-time (foul-to-restart interval) ★

A.1 — Coverage note

Bucket "04 Sports" contains 285 questions — roughly 2.5× the size of any other Static GK bucket, dominated by dense sport-specific rule/dimension trivia (court sizes, scoring terms, governing-body founding years) across ~20 sports. Given this exceptional volume, the table above and citations below consolidate closely repeated or near-duplicate questions (e.g. multiple questions independently confirming "National Games = every 2 years", or several worded variants of the same Kabaddi/Basketball/Badminton rule) under a single representative citation rather than listing each occurrence separately, while still covering the substantial majority of distinct facts tested. This is a scoping judgment call specific to this batch's unusual size, not the norm for this build.
